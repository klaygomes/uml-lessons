No Free Lunch

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can state and prove the No-Free-Lunch theorem step by step (the family of tasks on 2m2m2m messages, the expectation as an average over sequences, max ≥ average ≥ min, keeping only unseen messages, the twin pairing, and the split that turns an expectation of at least 1/4 into a probability of at least 1/7 of risk at least 1/8); deduce that the class of all functions over an infinite domain is not PAC learnable; split ERM's true risk into approximation and estimation error, say what each depends on, bound the estimation error of a finite class from lesson 4's results; and choose a class along a nested family for a given number of examples (the bias-complexity tradeoff).

Misconceptions:

  • "No free lunch means learning is impossible." Why wrong: every task the theorem builds is learned by ERM over {f}\{f\}{f} with no examples; the theorem rules out one learner for all tasks, not learning.
  • "So every learner is equally good." Why wrong: equal only on average over all targets weighted uniformly; real tasks are not uniform random targets, and prior knowledge is exactly that claim.
  • "A bigger class is safer." Why wrong: it lowers εapp\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}εapp​ but raises εest\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}εest​; at 50 labels the only class containing the truth (H8\mathcal H_8H8​) is the worst rung but one (0.424; only H0\mathcal H_0H0​ at 0.499 is worse).
  • "Approximation error is bad luck with the data." Why wrong: it is fixed by class and task before any example; more examples leave it unchanged.
  • Lesson 1's two failure modes map here: the rat's failure (class misses the truth) is approximation error; the pigeon's (class too large for the examples) is estimation error.
