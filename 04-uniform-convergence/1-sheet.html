Misconceptions (all four in "What people get wrong"):

  • "Each row is unbiased, so the winner's row is too." Why wrong: unbiasedness holds for a hypothesis fixed before the data; the winner is chosen by the data (proposition; 0.179 shown, 0.25 best, 0.2755 delivered).
  • "More hypotheses can only help." Why wrong: more lucky candidates; at m=60m = 60m=60, 30 to 465 rows moves the winner's empirical risk 0.181 to 0.127 while its true risk stays about 0.27 (act 1 numbers below).
  • "The theorem says I need 1,419." Why wrong: sufficient, not necessary; about 900 suffice in this world.
  • "Uniform convergence is the law of large numbers." Why wrong: one average in the limit versus all averages at a finite mmm with a number.
