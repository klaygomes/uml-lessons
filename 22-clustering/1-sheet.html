Overview

Objective: after this, you can explain why clustering is ill-defined (non-transitive similarity, no ground truth); define a distance function, a clustering (partition), soft clusterings and dendrograms; run single, average and max linkage with the three stopping rules and prove single linkage is Kruskal's algorithm; define the k-means, k-medoids, k-median, center-based and SOD costs; prove the mean minimizes squared spread and that each k-means iteration does not increase the cost; explain cut, RatioCut, the unnormalized Laplacian, prove v⊤Lv=12∑Wr,s(vr−vs)2v^\top L v = \frac12\sum W_{r,s}(v_r - v_s)^2v⊤Lv=21​∑Wr,s​(vr​−vs​)2 and RatioCut =trace⁡(H⊤LH)= \operatorname{trace}(H^\top L H)=trace(H⊤LH), and state the spectral relaxation; compute mutual information and the information-bottleneck objective; state scale invariance, richness and consistency and prove that no clustering function has all three.

Misconception: "k-means finds the best clustering." Why wrong: it reaches a fixed point; on the twelve messages, start (0,0),(0,2) stops at 35 against the optimum 20; 1,000 random starts end 692 at 20, 208 at 23, 100 at 35.

Second misconception: "looks right on the plot means the method is right." Why wrong: average linkage drew the four kinds (purity > 0.95) on 25 of 30 lab batches but optimizes no stated cost; nothing checkable.

Third misconception: "single linkage is the natural choice." Why wrong: chaining; on the lab mail with k=4k = 4k=4, mean purity 0.595 (min 0.28), sizes like [1, 1, 25, 77] (seed 0) and [1, 2, 2, 99] (seed 2).

Fourth misconception: "there is a correct clustering." Why wrong: no ground truth even with full knowledge of D\mathcal DD; the impossibility theorem.

Derivation (all proofs in the prose):

  • Single linkage = Kruskal (p. 311 says "it is also possible to show"): induction on rounds, ours; Kruskal's correctness cited as a standard algorithms fact, not proved.
  • Mean minimizes squared spread: the book says "note that" (p. 314); the decomposition proof is ours.
  • Lemma 22.1 (each iteration does not increase the cost): the book's two inequalities (22.4) and (22.5), with the per-message argument and the empty-cluster remark spelled out (ours).
  • Termination with ties kept in the current cluster, and the "stop if GGG fails to drop strictly" safeguard: ours. The book says only that there is no guarantee on the number of iterations (p. 314).
  • Quadratic-form identity and Lemma 22.3: the book's, with the "standard algebraic manipulations" written out.
  • "Separate pieces are free" proposition (zero RatioCut, columns are 0-eigenvectors): ours, formalizing the book's intuition on p. 317. Nonnegative eigenvalues: ours, one line.
  • Relaxation solved by the kkk smallest eigenvectors: stated, proof deferred to lesson 23a as the book does (Theorem 23.2).
  • Theorem 22.4: the book's proof, with the positivity requirement made explicit and α=max⁡d1/d2\alpha = \max d_1/d_2α=maxd1​/d2​ given explicitly.

Algorithms:

  • Linkage (pp. 310 to 311): pseudocode ours; O(m3)O(m^3)O(m3) time, O(m2)O(m^2)O(m2) space with the cluster-distance table and Lance-Williams-style updates (min, max, size-weighted average); O(m2n)O(m^2 n)O(m2n) for distances; single linkage O(m2)O(m^2)O(m2) via Prim. Build-it asserts pass with sol_linkage.py (also prints max heights [2, 2, 7, 13] and "mst True" over 200 random 9-point sets).
  • k-means (p. 313): O(mkn)O(mkn)O(mkn) per iteration, O(mn+kn)O(mn + kn)O(mn+kn) space, O(RTmkn)O(RTmkn)O(RTmkn) total. Build-it asserts pass with sol_kmeans.py (smaller-index ties, keep empty centre, stop when unchanged or GGG not strictly lower); its internal assertions of both lemma inequalities never fired on 1,000 random 15-point sets with k=3k = 3k=3.
  • Unnormalized spectral clustering (p. 317): O(m2n)O(m^2 n)O(m2n) to build WWW, O(m3)O(m^3)O(m3) eigendecomposition, O(mk2)O(mk^2)O(mk2) per k-means iteration, O(m2)O(m^2)O(m2) space. No separate build-it (its k-means step reuses the k-means build-it; the power-iteration snippet computes the Fiedler vector).
  • Information bottleneck: no algorithm in the book (hard; EM-like methods, lesson 24b). None built.
