# Feature Selection and Generation

Objective: after this, you can explain why the representation is prior knowledge and why a feature’s quality depends on the learner; state feature selection as \(\min L_S(\mathbf w)\) s.t. \(\|\mathbf w\|_0 \le k\) and say why it is solved approximately; derive the filter-method score for linear regression (best single-feature line, Pearson’s \(\rho\)) and show a case where it fails; run forward greedy selection, prove that OMP makes the same choices with the new-part score \(\langle \mathbf u_j, \mathbf y \rangle^2/\|\mathbf u_j\|^2\), derive the steepest-coordinate rule from \(\beta\)-smoothness, and describe backward elimination; prove soft thresholding and explain why the \(\ell_1\) price gives exact zeros where \(\ell_2\) does not; solve the LASSO by coordinate descent; show how normalization and clipping change what ridge and linear regression can do, and apply the toolbox of transformations; describe auto-encoders, \(k\)-means and sparse coding as feature learning; and certify a few-feature mail filter with the finite-class uniform-convergence bound.

Terms:
- feature: a measurement of a message, a function from messages to numbers; feature vector: \(d\) of them stacked in \(\mathbb R^d\).
- \(\|\mathbf w\|_0 = |\{i : w_i \neq 0\}|\); not a norm (no homogeneity). Feature selection: \(\min L_S(\mathbf w)\) s.t. \(\|\mathbf w\|_0 \le k\).
- filter method: score each feature alone, keep the top \(k\). For least squares the score is \(|\rho|\), \(\rho = \langle \mathbf v - \bar v, \mathbf y - \bar y \rangle/(\|\mathbf v - \bar v\|\|\mathbf y - \bar y\|)\); best single-feature loss \(= \frac1m\|\mathbf y - \bar y\|^2(1 - \rho^2)\).
- forward greedy selection: \(j_t \in \operatorname{argmin}_j \min_{\mathbf w}\|X_{I_{t-1}\cup\{j\}}\mathbf w - \mathbf y\|^2\).
- OMP: orthonormal basis \(V\) of chosen columns, \(\mathbf u_j = X_j - VV^\top X_j\), choose \(\operatorname{argmax}\langle \mathbf u_j, \mathbf y \rangle^2/\|\mathbf u_j\|^2\), append \(\mathbf u/\|\mathbf u\|\) and \(\theta = \langle \mathbf u, \mathbf y \rangle/\|\mathbf u\|\).
- backward elimination: from all features, remove the one whose removal hurts least.
- soft thresholding: \(\operatorname{argmin}_w \frac12 w^2 - xw + \lambda|w| = \operatorname{sign}(x)[|x| - \lambda]_+\).
- LASSO: \(\operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf w} \frac1{2m}\|X\mathbf w - \mathbf y\|^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf w\|_1\); coordinate-descent update \(w_j \leftarrow \operatorname{soft}(\langle X_j, \mathbf r_j \rangle/m, \lambda)/(\|X_j\|^2/m)\).
- ridge regression: \(\operatorname{argmin}\frac1m\|X\mathbf w - \mathbf y\|^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf w\|^2 = (X^\top X + \lambda m I)^{-1}X^\top\mathbf y\).
- transformations: centering \(f - \bar f\); unit range \((f - f_{\min})/(f_{\max} - f_{\min})\); standardization \((f - \bar f)/\sqrt\nu\); clipping \(\operatorname{sign}(f)\min\{b, |f|\}\); sigmoidal \(1/(1 + e^{bf})\); logarithmic \(\log(b + f)\).
- auto-encoder: encoder \(\psi: \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^k\), decoder \(\phi: \mathbb R^k \to \mathbb R^d\), minimize \(\sum_i\|\mathbf x_i - \phi(\psi(\mathbf x_i))\|^2\); PCA (linear, \(k < d\)), \(k\)-means (one-hot codes, \(\phi(\mathbf v) = \sum v_i\boldsymbol\mu_i\)), sparse coding (\(\|\mathbf v\|_0 \le s\), or \(+\lambda\|\mathbf v\|_1\)).
- certificate: \(L_S(h) + \sqrt{\log(2|\mathcal H_k|/\delta_k)/(2m)}\), \(\delta_k = \delta/2^k\), \(|\mathcal H_k| \le \binom{d}{k}4^k 2^k(20k + 1)\).

Third misconception: “normalization is cosmetic”. Why wrong: ridge true risk 0.2 raw vs \(4.9 \times 10^{-6}\) normalized; clipping takes risk 0.9608 to 0 at \(a = 100\).

Derivation (all in the prose with proofs, except as noted):
- Filter-method score: centering equality (deferred to Problem 1, checked numerically), \(b = 0\) from centered sums, \(a = \langle\rangle/\|\|^2\), minimum value, ranking by \(|\rho|\), \(|\rho| \le 1\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
- Filter failure: \(\mathbb E[yx_1] = 0\) by linearity and independence.
- OMP proposition: same span, expand, \(\langle \mathbf u, V\boldsymbol\theta \rangle = 0\) from \(V^\top V = I\), separate minimization, parabola in \(\alpha\).
- Steepest coordinate: one-dimensional quadratic upper bound from \(\beta\)-smoothness (lesson 12), minimized at \(\eta = -\partial_j R/\beta\).
- Penalized ⇒ budgeted with \(k_1 = \|\mathbf w_\lambda\|_1\) (proved); converse via Lagrange duality (stated, not proved).
- Soft thresholding: three cases. One-dimensional LASSO by reduction; \(\ell_2\) closed form by derivative.
- LASSO coordinate update by reduction to soft thresholding (convergence not proved).
- Ridge closed form by gradient, invertibility; objective comparison; clipping \(w^\star = (2a - 1)/(a^2 + a - 1)\).
- Linear predictor on one-hot cluster codes is constant per cluster.
- Certificate: lesson 4’s two-sided uniform convergence for finite classes, union over \(k\) with \(\delta_k = \delta/2^k\).

Algorithms: filter method (\(O(dm + d\log d)\) time, \(O(d)\) space); forward greedy (\(O(dk^3m)\) for \(k \le m\)); OMP (\(O(dmt)\) per round as written, \(O(Tdm)\) with stored new parts, \(O(dm)\) space; SVD variant for stability); steepest-coordinate rule (\(O(dm)\) per round); backward elimination (\(\approx O(d \cdot (md^2 + d^3))\) per first round, \(\approx O(d^5)\) total when \(m \sim d\)); LASSO coordinate descent (\(O(dm)\) per sweep with maintained residual). Build-it challenge: `ls_loss`, `pearson_filter`, `forward_greedy`, `omp`, `backward_elimination`, `lasso_cd` on the cancelling-world data; asserts: filter method picks [1, 2] (old, bangs), forward greedy [1, 0], OMP = forward greedy for k = 1, 2, 3, backward elimination {0, 1}, loss on [1, 2] about 3.4286 (> 3.4), loss on [0, 1] = 0, LASSO at λ = 0.5 all zeros (max \(|\langle X_j, \mathbf y \rangle|/m = 0.5\), attained by old and bangs), at λ = 0.2 weights (0.133, 0.667, 0.133, 0.0). Ties broken by smaller index; backward elimination removes bangs, then noise.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(\nabla g(\mathbf 0) = -\frac1m X^\top\mathbf y\), so \(\partial_j g(\mathbf 0) = -\langle X_j, \mathbf y \rangle/m\). Convexity (tangent inequality, lesson 12): \(g(\mathbf w) \ge g(\mathbf 0) + \langle \nabla g(\mathbf 0), \mathbf w \rangle\). Hence \(F(\mathbf w) \ge g(\mathbf 0) + \sum_j\left(\lambda|w_j| - \frac{\langle X_j, \mathbf y \rangle}{m}w_j\right) \ge g(\mathbf 0) + \sum_j\left(\lambda - \frac{|\langle X_j, \mathbf y \rangle|}{m}\right)|w_j| \ge g(\mathbf 0) = F(\mathbf 0)\). Just below the value (here 0.5, attained by old and bangs with \(\langle X_j, \mathbf y \rangle = 4\), \(m = 8\)), a coordinate-descent update from \(\mathbf 0\) gives \(\operatorname{soft}(0.5, \lambda)/1 > 0\) for those coordinates, and the one-sided directional derivative of \(F\) along \(+\mathbf e_j\) is \(\lambda - 0.5 < 0\), so \(\mathbf 0\) is no longer a minimizer. Look for the learner noticing that the bound must hold for all coordinates at once.
2. The step “the cross term loses \(\boldsymbol\theta\)”: \(\langle \mathbf u, V\boldsymbol\theta \rangle = \langle V^\top\mathbf u, \boldsymbol\theta \rangle\) and \(V^\top\mathbf u = V^\top X_j - (V^\top V)V^\top X_j = \mathbf 0\) only if \(V^\top V = I\). With unnormalized chosen columns (e.g. \(V = X_2\), \(\|X_2\|^2 = 10\)), \(VV^\top X_j\) is ten times the projection, \(\mathbf u\) is not orthogonal to the span, the cross term \(2\alpha\langle \mathbf u, V\boldsymbol\theta \rangle\) couples \(\alpha\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta\), so the minimum does not split, and the score \(\langle \mathbf u, \mathbf y \rangle^2/\|\mathbf u\|^2\) no longer equals the loss decrease; the implementation can pick a different feature from forward greedy. (The same-span step still holds, since \(\mathbf u\) still differs from \(X_j\) by a vector in the span; accept that observation.)
3. \(a = 4\): \(w^\star = 7/19 \approx 0.3684\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(w^\star) = 27/76 \approx 0.3553\) (square loss, no ½); after clipping 0. A hurting example: the target is the magnitude itself, e.g. real-valued \(y = x\) with \(x \in \{1, a\}\) each with probability ½; raw \(w = 1\) gives 0, clipped \(x' = 1\) always gives \(w = (1 + a)/2\), risk \(((a - 1)/2)^2 > 0\). Any distribution where values above 1 carry information works. The bet: values beyond the threshold carry no more information than the threshold.

2. Exercise 3 (AdaBoost as forward greedy selection).
   - Hint 1 (first bullet): restrict \(\mathcal H\) to \(\{x_1, \dots, x_m\}\); by Sauer’s lemma (lesson 6) the number of distinct restrictions is at most \((em/\mathrm{VC})^{\mathrm{VC}}\), finite; take one representative per pattern.
   - Hint 2: \(\partial R/\partial w_j = \frac{\sum_i \exp(-y_i f_{\mathbf w}(x_i))(-y_i h_j(x_i))}{\sum_i \exp(-y_i f_{\mathbf w}(x_i))}\); recognize \(D_i\).
   - Hint 3: for \(\pm1\) values, \(y_i h_j(x_i) = 1 - 2\,\mathbb 1[h_j(x_i) \ne y_i]\); use \(\sum_i D_i = 1\). With \(\varepsilon_j \le \frac12 - \gamma\), \(\partial R/\partial w_j \le -2\gamma\), so its absolute value is at least \(2\gamma\) (the book writes “\(\ge \gamma/2\)”, weaker but true, and with the sign dropped).
   - Hint 4 (last bullet): AdaBoost’s round adds \(w_t = \frac12\log(1/\varepsilon_t - 1)\) to coordinate \(j_t\); \(R(\mathbf w^{(t+1)}) - R(\mathbf w^{(t)}) = \log\sum_i D_i^{(t)}e^{-w_t y_i h_t(x_i)} = \log\left(2\sqrt{\varepsilon_t(1 - \varepsilon_t)}\right)\), exactly lesson 10’s normalizer step; then \(2\sqrt{\varepsilon(1 - \varepsilon)} \le \sqrt{1 - 4\gamma^2}\) for \(\varepsilon \le \frac12 - \gamma\).
