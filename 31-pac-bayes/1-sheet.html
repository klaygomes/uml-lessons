PAC-Bayes

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define a prior and a posterior over a hypothesis class, compute the true and empirical risk of a Gibbs (randomized) predictor, compute the relative entropy D(Q∥P)D(Q\|P)D(Q∥P) by hand, by formula and by simulation, prove the PAC-Bayes theorem step by step (one-sided Hoeffding, the exponential-moment lemma, the swap that needs the prior fixed in advance, Markov, the change-of-measure inequality, Jensen), recover the finite-class and description-length certificates as point-mass special cases, and run the PAC-Bayes rule over Gibbs posteriors with its cost.

Book: Chapter 31, Section 31.1 (PAC-Bayes bounds, pp. 415 to 417: prior, posterior, randomized prediction rule, ℓ(Q,z)\ell(Q, z)ℓ(Q,z), LD(Q)L_{\mathcal D}(Q)LD​(Q), LS(Q)L_S(Q)LS​(Q), Theorem 31.1 and its proof with Equations 31.1 to 31.6, Remark 31.1 on regularization, Equation 31.7), Section 31.2 (bibliographic remarks, p. 417: McAllester 1998 and later; not used in the prose), Section 31.3 (exercises, pp. 417 to 418). Tools: Markov's inequality (Appendix B.1, p. 422), Hoeffding's inequality (Appendix B.4, p. 425; one-sided form from its proof), Kraft inequality and the MDL bound (Chapter 7, §7.3), finite-class uniform convergence (Corollary 4.6), regularized risk minimization (Chapter 13). Relative entropy was introduced in lesson 24a (Chapter 24, as DRED_{\mathrm{RE}}DRE​); Jensen's inequality in lesson 14a.

Misconception 1: "a mix makes fewer mistakes than its best member". Why wrong: LD(Q)L_{\mathcal D}(Q)LD​(Q) is a weighted average of member risks, so at least the minimum; the mix lowers the certificate, not the error (committee 0.0296 vs perfect threshold 0.02; small committee 0.0430 vs h0.60h_{0.60}h0.60​'s 0.0392).

Misconception 2: "the posterior must be the Bayesian posterior". Why wrong: the theorem holds for every QQQ; Bayes' rule is never used.

Misconception 3: "fix the prior after seeing the data". Why wrong: step 3 pulls P(h)P(h)P(h) out of ES\mathbb E_SES​ because it is a constant.

Misconception 4: "PAC-Bayes always beats older bounds". Why wrong: for a point mass it pays log⁡(m/δ)\log(m/\delta)log(m/δ) vs log⁡(2/δ)\log(2/\delta)log(2/δ) and m−1m-1m−1 vs mmm (0.1525 vs 0.137 for a billion hypotheses at 650 labels).

Misconception 5: "a zero-prior hypothesis is merely expensive". Why wrong: any posterior weight on it makes D=∞D = \inftyD=∞.
