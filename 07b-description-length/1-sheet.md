# Description Length and Occam’s Razor

Objective: after this, you can explain why weights by size price a short list on a late feature like its whole size class; define description languages and prefix-free codes; prove the Kraft inequality and the MDL bound and run MDL; say when MDL beats SRM and when it loses; explain Occam's razor with its language catch; state MDL's stopping rule and cost; define consistency and explain why Memorize meets it and why that does not contradict No-Free-Lunch.

Book: Chapter 7, Sections 7.3 to 7.6 (PDF pp. 89 to 96). Recaps Sections 7.1 and 7.2 (lesson 7a). Uses Lemma 4.5 Hoeffding (p. 56), Theorem 5.1 (p. 61), Theorem 7.2 (p. 84), Theorem 7.4 (pp. 86 to 87), Remark 7.2 (p. 88). This lesson is the second half of the original lesson 7, from "Lists by the bit" on, with a new opening bug.

Terms:
- decision list, size, \(\mathcal H_n\), \(n(h)\), \(|\mathcal H_n|\), SRM bound, SRM, nonuniformly learnable: as in lesson 7a, recapped in "What you need from lesson 7a".
- description language \(d: \mathcal H \to \{0,1\}^*\), \(|h| = |d(h)|\); prefix-free: no \(d(h)\) is a prefix of \(d(h')\), \(h \ne h'\) (p. 89).
- list code: feature \(j\) in the zero-padded binary code (`1`, `010`, `011`, `00100`, ...; \(2\lfloor\log_2 j\rfloor + 1\) bits); a test is flag `1`, feature code, label bit; the end is `0` then the default bit.
- Kraft inequality (Lemma 7.6, p. 90): prefix-free \(S\) gives \(\sum_{\sigma \in S} 2^{-|\sigma|} \le 1\).
- MDL bound (Thm 7.7, p. 90): w.p. \(\ge 1 - \delta\), \(\forall h\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le L_S(h) + \sqrt{(|h| + \log(2/\delta))/(2m)}\); MDL rule: its argmin (p. 90).
- consistency (Def. 7.8, p. 92): sample size \(m^{\mathrm{CON}}(\varepsilon, \delta, h, \mathcal D)\); universally consistent when \(\mathcal P\) is all distributions. (The book's definition writes \(m^{\mathrm{NUL}}\) in the condition, a typo; the lesson writes \(m^{\mathrm{CON}}\).)
- missing mass: \(\sum_x \mathcal D(x)\,\mathbb 1[x \notin S]\), with mean \(\sum_x \mathcal D(x)(1 - \mathcal D(x))^m\).

Misconception: "shorter is intrinsically truer". Why wrong: length is relative to a language chosen in advance.
Second misconception: "pick the language that makes the winner short". Why wrong: a language, like weights, must be independent of \(S\), or the union bound's events are not the budgeted ones (the Occam language puzzle, p. 91).
Third misconception: "MDL is always better than SRM". Why wrong: on the main inbox at 1,000 labels SRM with the signed weights certifies \(f\) at 0.1938 while MDL's best is 0.2227 (the 15-bit three-test list); \(f\)'s 29 bits cost more than its size-5 share. On the help desk it is the reverse. Two priors, each wins on the streams it fits.

Derivation (proofs in the prose):
- Per-hypothesis SRM: singletons \(\{h_n\}\), Hoeffding gap \(\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2m)}\), share \(w(h)\delta\) adds \(-\log w(h)\).
- Kraft (Lemma 7.6): the book's coin argument, made finite (toss \(L\) coins, events \(E_\sigma\) disjoint by prefix-freeness), then partial sums for infinite \(S\); the binary-interval picture (codeword \(\sigma\) owns a stretch of [0, 1) of width \(2^{-|\sigma|}\)) added for the lab.
- MDL bound (Thm 7.7): Kraft gives weights \(2^{-|h|}\); singleton gap from Hoeffding; \(|h|\log 2 < |h|\); uses the SRM bound theorem of lesson 7a.
- Occam (§7.3.1, p. 91): razor, language puzzle (3 bits vs 100,000), resolution by commitment before data.
- Consistency (§7.4, pp. 92 to 93), Memorize (Example 7.4), expected missing mass by linearity and independence (noise-free intuition only; proof is Exercise 6), NFL revisited (§7.5.1, pp. 95 to 96), discussion (§7.5, pp. 93 to 95), consistency trick (p. 95).

MDL-ordered (enumerate by code length, stop when the gap alone reaches the best bound): at most \(2^{\ell^*+1}\) codewords, \(O(m\ell^*2^{\ell^*})\) time. Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. The sentence "if they did, both strings would be beginnings of the same toss sequence, so the shorter one would be a prefix of the longer one" uses prefix-freeness to make the \(E_\sigma\) disjoint. \(\{0, 01, 1\}\): \(1/2 + 1/4 + 1/2 = 1.25\); \(E_0\) and \(E_{01}\) overlap (tosses beginning 01). Without the end flag: \((();1)\) is `1`, and any list starting with a test, e.g. \(((1,0); 0)\) = `1 1 0 0`, begins with `1`: prefix clash.
2. \(\log(2/0.05) = 3.689\). 15 bits: \(0.12 + \sqrt{18.689/600} = 0.2965\); 29 bits: \(0.10 + \sqrt{32.689/600} = 0.3334\); MDL prefers the 15-bit list by 0.0369. Crossover: \(0.02 = (\sqrt{32.689/2} - \sqrt{18.689/2})/\sqrt m\), \(\sqrt m = 49.30\), \(m = 2{,}430.3\), so the smallest integer is \(m = 2{,}431\) (computed by search).

End-of-chapter problems (hints only, never the solution; book pp. 97 to 99):
- Exercise 4. Oracle inequality for MDL against \(h^*_B\), the best hypothesis of description length at most \(B\).
  1. Start from the MDL bound applied to the returned \(h_S\): it holds on a good event, simultaneously for all hypotheses.
  2. Compare the MDL objective at \(h_S\) with the objective at \(h^*_B\), and use \(|h^*_B| \le B\).
  3. You still need \(L_S(h^*_B)\) close to \(L_{\mathcal D}(h^*_B)\). Either use the same simultaneous event (two-sided) or a Hoeffding bound for the fixed \(h^*_B\) with part of the budget; account for the union.
  4. Aim for a bound of the form \(c\sqrt{(B + \log(c'/\delta))/m}\).
- Exercise 6. Memorize is consistent for every class of binary functions over a countable domain.
  1. Part 1: the tail of a convergent series of nonnegative terms goes to 0.
  2. Part 2: messages of tiny probability can only carry tiny total mass once you go far enough down the enumeration; choose the threshold from part 1.
  3. Part 3: a fixed message of probability above \(\eta\) is missed by \(m\) draws with probability at most \((1 - \eta)^m \le e^{-\eta m}\); there are at most \(n\) such messages; union bound.
  4. Parts 4 and 5: the unseen mass is the heavy unseen messages plus the light ones; bound each by \(\varepsilon/2\). Then relate Memorize's risk to the unseen mass (and, with noisy labels, to majority votes on the seen messages).
  Book slip in part 1 (p. 98): the enumeration is written with \(\mathcal D(\{x_i\}) \le \mathcal D(\{x_j\})\) for \(i \le j\), nondecreasing, which no infinite countable distribution allows; it should be nonincreasing. The hints do not depend on the order.
- Exercise 4 note: the book says "some fixed description language"; the MDL bound needs it prefix-free, so the hints assume a prefix-free language.
- Exercise 3 goes with lesson 7a. Exercises 1 and 2 (description length bounds VC dimension; no nondecreasing summable weights) are not set in either half; lesson 7a's sheet defers them here, so decide at the problems step. Exercise 5 is the deferred proof of lesson 7a's limit.
