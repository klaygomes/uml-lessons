Terms:
- convex set: contains \(\alpha\mathbf u + (1 - \alpha)\mathbf v\) for all \(\mathbf u, \mathbf v\) in it and \(\alpha \in [0, 1]\).
- convex function: \(f(\alpha\mathbf u + (1 - \alpha)\mathbf v) \le \alpha f(\mathbf u) + (1 - \alpha) f(\mathbf v)\); equivalently its epigraph is convex.
- local / global minimum: no lower point in some ball \(B(\mathbf u, r)\) / anywhere.
- tangent inequality: \(f(\mathbf u) \ge f(\mathbf w) + \langle \nabla f(\mathbf w), \mathbf u - \mathbf w \rangle\) for convex differentiable \(f\).
- \(\rho\)-Lipschitz: \(\|f(\mathbf w_1) - f(\mathbf w_2)\| \le \rho\|\mathbf w_1 - \mathbf w_2\|\).
- \(\beta\)-smooth: differentiable with \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient.
- self-bounded: \(\|\nabla f(\mathbf w)\|^2 \le 2\beta f(\mathbf w)\) (smooth and nonnegative).
- convex learning problem: \(\mathcal H\) convex and every \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) convex.
- hinge loss: \(\max\{0, 1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle\}\).
- certificate: \(f(\mathbf w) - f(\mathbf u) \le \|\nabla f(\mathbf w)\|(\|\mathbf w\| + B)\) for all \(\|\mathbf u\| \le B\), \(f\) convex and differentiable.

Misconception: "a zero gradient means you have reached the bottom". Why wrong: only for convex functions; the 0-1 risk has zero gradient on every terrace, at 0.8 and at 0.2.
Second: "smooth means convex". Why wrong: the sigmoid loss is smooth and fails the chord test: values 0.982 at \(z = -4\) and 0.5 at \(z = 0\), chord midpoint 0.741, curve at \(z = -2\) 0.8808.
Third: "a bigger step gets there faster, so it is better". Why wrong: \(3/\beta\) happened to work on the logistic risk (27 steps), but the quadratic upper bound allows an increase; on \(x^2\) it diverges (Problem 1).
Fourth: "a random chord test that finds no violation proves convexity". Why wrong: a test only finds violations; convexity is proved by an argument.
Fifth: "the hinge loss is convex, so the gradient certificate works for it". Why wrong: no gradient at the kink; the act 1 walker's certificate never drops below 0.4271 in 300 steps.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Convexity is used in the second display of the proof, \(f(\mathbf u + \alpha(\mathbf v - \mathbf u)) = f(\alpha\mathbf v + (1 - \alpha)\mathbf u) \le (1 - \alpha) f(\mathbf u) + \alpha f(\mathbf v)\). For the 0-1 risk with \(\mathbf u = -\mathbf w^\circ\), \(\mathbf v = \mathbf w^\circ\): convexity would force at most \((1 - \alpha) 0.8 + \alpha 0.2 = 0.8 - 0.6\alpha < 0.8\); the risk is really 0.8 for every \(\alpha < 0.218/6 \approx 0.0364\) (plateau radius over \(\|\mathbf v - \mathbf u\| = 6\)), and the walk table shows 0.8 even at \(\alpha = 0.25\) against the chord's 0.65. Local minimality holds; the convexity step fails, so the chain breaks.
2. The first step, the tangent inequality \(f(\mathbf u) \ge f(\mathbf w) + \langle \nabla f(\mathbf w), \mathbf u - \mathbf w \rangle\), needs convexity; the sigmoid loss fails the chord test (e.g. \(z = -4, 0\): curve 0.8808 at the midpoint above chord 0.741), and so does its risk. Ball vector: \(\mathbf w^\circ = (-1, 2, -2)\), \(\|\mathbf w^\circ\| = 3\), sigmoid risk 0.2687, more than 0.01 below 0.5011 (accept any ball vector with risk below 0.4911, e.g. found by search; the numerical ball minimum is about 0.2678). The reported weights outside the ball do not matter: the claim allows any \(\mathbf w\) and restricts only \(\mathbf u\).
3. \(0.02 \times (1.8 + 3) = 0.096\); next step lowers the risk by at least \(0.02^2/(2 \cdot 1.85) = 0.000108\).

- Problem 1 (how big a step is safe?). Hint ladder: (1) Put \(\mathbf v - \mathbf w = -\eta\nabla f(\mathbf w)\) into the quadratic upper bound; what do the inner product and the squared length become?
