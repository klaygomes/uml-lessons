From Gradient Descent to SGD

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can run gradient descent and prove its Bρ/TB\rho/\sqrt TBρ/T​ guarantee for convex-Lipschitz functions through the step lemma; compute subgradients (absolute value, pointwise maxima, the hinge loss) and connect Lipschitzness to bounded subgradients; state and prove the SGD guarantee in expectation; explain why one fresh example’s (sub)gradient is an unbiased estimate for the true risk, so that SGD learns convex-Lipschitz-bounded problems from B2ρ2/ε2B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2B2ρ2/ε2 examples in O(d)O(d)O(d) time per example; and certify a step size and a number of steps against a fresh-label budget. Variants (projection proof, variable step size, other averaging, strongly convex rate, smooth losses, SGD for regularized loss minimization) are lesson 14b.

Misconception: “SGD is a noisy approximation of gradient descent, so its learning guarantee must be worse.” · Why wrong: on fresh examples SGD minimizes LDL_{\mathcal D}LD​ itself, with B2ρ2/ε2B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2B2ρ2/ε2 examples, 8 times fewer than the RLM bound, while GD on LSL_SLS​ only optimizes the empirical risk.

Second misconception: “more passes over the archive make SGD better.” · Why wrong: the unbiasedness for ∇LD\nabla L_{\mathcal D}∇LD​ needs ztz_tzt​ independent of w(t)\mathbf w^{(t)}w(t); reused examples break it, and the procedure becomes an optimizer of LSL_SLS​.

Third: “the guarantee holds for each run.” · It is in expectation; Markov gives a crude high-probability version. The prose’s “What people get wrong” also covers “at a crease the method breaks” and “a bigger step size gets there faster”.
