# From Gradient Descent to SGD

Objective: after this, you can run gradient descent and prove its \(B\rho/\sqrt T\) guarantee for convex-Lipschitz functions through the step lemma; compute subgradients (absolute value, pointwise maxima, the hinge loss) and connect Lipschitzness to bounded subgradients; state and prove the SGD guarantee in expectation; explain why one fresh example’s (sub)gradient is an unbiased estimate for the true risk, so that SGD learns convex-Lipschitz-bounded problems from \(B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2\) examples in \(O(d)\) time per example; and certify a step size and a number of steps against a fresh-label budget. Variants (projection proof, variable step size, other averaging, strongly convex rate, smooth losses, SGD for regularized loss minimization) are lesson 14b.

Terms:
- gradient \(\nabla f(\mathbf w)\): vector of partial derivatives; direction of fastest increase.
- gradient descent: \(\mathbf w^{(1)} = \mathbf 0\), \(\mathbf w^{(t+1)} = \mathbf w^{(t)} - \eta\nabla f(\mathbf w^{(t)})\), output \(\bar{\mathbf w} = \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf w^{(t)}\).
- step lemma: for any \(\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_T\) and update \(\mathbf w^{(t+1)} = \mathbf w^{(t)} - \eta\mathbf v_t\) from \(\mathbf 0\), \(\sum_t \langle \mathbf w^{(t)} - \mathbf w^\star, \mathbf v_t \rangle \le \|\mathbf w^\star\|^2/(2\eta) + \frac\eta2\sum_t\|\mathbf v_t\|^2\).
- subgradient: \(\mathbf v\) with \(f(\mathbf u) \ge f(\mathbf w) + \langle \mathbf u - \mathbf w, \mathbf v \rangle\) for all \(\mathbf u\); \(\partial f(\mathbf w)\) the set of them.
- hinge subgradient: \(\mathbf 0\) if \(y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle \ge 1\), else \(-y\mathbf x\).
- SGD: step along random \(\mathbf v_t\) with \(\mathbb E[\mathbf v_t \mid \mathbf v_{1:t-1}] \in \partial f(\mathbf w^{(t)})\).
- unbiased estimate: a random vector whose conditional expectation given the past is the (sub)gradient at the current iterate.
- certified gap: \(B^2/(2\eta T) + \eta\rho^2/2\), minimized at \(\eta = B/(\rho\sqrt T)\) with value \(B\rho/\sqrt T\).
- projection onto the ball (SGD-Hinge): \(\mathbf w \min(1, B/\|\mathbf w\|)\); proof that it keeps the guarantee in lesson 14b.

Misconception: “SGD is a noisy approximation of gradient descent, so its learning guarantee must be worse.” · Why wrong: on fresh examples SGD minimizes \(L_{\mathcal D}\) itself, with \(B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2\) examples, 8 times fewer than the RLM bound, while GD on \(L_S\) only optimizes the empirical risk.
Second misconception: “more passes over the archive make SGD better.” · Why wrong: the unbiasedness for \(\nabla L_{\mathcal D}\) needs \(z_t\) independent of \(\mathbf w^{(t)}\); reused examples break it, and the procedure becomes an optimizer of \(L_S\).
Third: “the guarantee holds for each run.” · It is in expectation; Markov gives a crude high-probability version.
The prose’s “What people get wrong” also covers “at a crease the method breaks” and “a bigger step size gets there faster”.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. At \(y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle = 1\) both pieces of the max equal 0, and \(\partial\ell = \{-\alpha y\mathbf x : \alpha \in [0, 1]\}\), the segment from \(\mathbf 0\) to \(-y\mathbf x\). Inclusion: \(\ell(\mathbf u) \ge \alpha(1 - y\langle \mathbf u, \mathbf x \rangle) + (1-\alpha) \cdot 0 = \langle \mathbf u - \mathbf w, -\alpha y\mathbf x \rangle\), using \(1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle = 0\). (Reverse inclusion: a subgradient must be \(\mathbf 0\) along directions orthogonal to \(\mathbf x\), by testing \(\mathbf u = \mathbf w \pm s\mathbf e\), and its component along \(\mathbf x\) is pinned between the two one-sided slopes; accept a convincing sketch.) The choice does not matter: the proofs use only the supporting inequality and \(\|\mathbf v\| \le \rho\), and \(\|\alpha y\mathbf x\| \le \|\mathbf x\| \le \rho\). The pick must be a fixed rule of \((\mathbf w, z)\) so the conditional mean is a subgradient of \(L_{\mathcal D}\). The book’s Example 14.2 picks \(\mathbf 0\) at the crease.
2. Step 3: after conditioning on \(\mathbf v_{1:t-1}\) and pulling out \(\mathbf w^{(t)} - \mathbf w^\star\), the subgradient inequality is applied to \(\mathbb E[\mathbf v_t \mid \mathbf v_{1:t-1}]\). On the second pass, \(z_t\) was already used to build \(\mathbf w^{(t)}\), so given \(\mathbf w^{(t)}\) it is not distributed as \(\mathcal D\): the first equality \(\mathbb E[\mathbf v_t \mid \mathbf w^{(t)}] = \mathbb E_{z \sim \mathcal D}[\nabla \ell(\mathbf w^{(t)}, z)]\) fails (and its subgradient analogue), so \(\mathbb E[\mathbf v_t \mid \text{past}]\) need not be in \(\partial L_{\mathcal D}(\mathbf w^{(t)})\). (Sampling uniformly from the 10,000 with replacement would make it unbiased for \(L_S\) instead, which is exactly what lesson 14b does for the regularized objective.) Look for: independence of \(z_t\) from the past, not “the data are noisy”.
3. \(T = B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2 = 16 \cdot 4/0.01 = 6{,}400\), \(\eta = B/(\rho\sqrt T) = 4/(2 \cdot 80) = 0.025\). Doubling to \(\eta = 0.05\): \(16/(2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 6400) + 0.05 \cdot 4/2 = 0.025 + 0.1 = 0.125\). With 5,000 messages: best certified \(\varepsilon = B\rho/\sqrt{5000} = 8/70.71 \approx 0.113\), at \(\eta = 4/(2\sqrt{5000}) \approx 0.0283\). (Build-it assert `steps_needed(4, 2, 0.1) == 6400` matches.)

- Exercise 3 (Perceptron as subgradient descent), standard.
  1. \(f(\mathbf w^\star) \le 0\). If some \(\mathbf w\) with \(\|\mathbf w\| \le \|\mathbf w^\star\|\) had \(f(\mathbf w) < 0\), every \(y_i\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle > 1\); scale \(\mathbf w\) down by \(c = 1/\min_i y_i\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle < 1\) to get a feasible vector of smaller norm, a contradiction. \(f(\mathbf w) < 1\) means every \(y_i\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle > 0\).
  2. \(f\) is a max of affine functions: by the claim on maxima, \(-y_j\mathbf x_j\) with \(j \in \operatorname{argmin}_i y_i\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle\) is a subgradient; \(f\) is \(R\)-Lipschitz.
  3. Subgradient descent: \(\mathbf w \leftarrow \mathbf w + \eta y_j\mathbf x_j\) on the example with the smallest signed score. With \(B = \|\mathbf w^\star\|\), \(\rho = R\): \(f(\bar{\mathbf w}) \le R\|\mathbf w^\star\|/\sqrt T < 1\) once \(T > (R\|\mathbf w^\star\|)^2\), so \(\bar{\mathbf w}\) separates.
  4. Compare with lesson 9: the Perceptron updates on any mistake and stops after at most \((RB)^2\) updates; here the update uses the worst example even when there are no mistakes, and the guarantee is for the average. Starting from \(\mathbf 0\), \(\mathbf w^{(t)} = \eta \sum y_j\mathbf x_j\), and the argmin of signed scores is scale-invariant, so \(\eta\) does not change which examples are picked.
