Misconception: “98% accuracy means the important messages are found”. Why wrong: with 2% important, the all-no filter has zero-one loss 0.02, recall 0, F₁ 0.
Second: “precision at 5 can be trained by thresholding a classifier”. Why wrong: a classifier chooses its own threshold for the zero-one loss; precision at 5 fixes the number flagged and cares only about the top (lab: flagged-first 0.51 to 0.55 vs sorted 0.68).
Third (new in the split): “a lower Kendall tau loss means a better top of the list”. Why wrong: on the six messages, swapping only the top two gives Kendall 1/15 = 0.067 and NDCG loss 0.041; top three right with the bottom three reversed gives Kendall 0.2 and NDCG loss 0 (`nums.py`).
Fourth (new in the split): “dropping the 0/1 constraint gives a fractional order you cannot use”. Why wrong: the lemma; some optimal solution of the relaxed LP is a permutation matrix.

End-of-chapter problem (Section 17.8, PDF p. 249). In the prose it appears as the lesson’s own Problem 1 = Exercise 4 (Equation 17.7), extended with the NDCG bound \(G \le G(\mathbf y, \mathbf y)\) that the book calls easy to verify. Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Exercise 4 (sorting identity), standard.
  1. Take any maximizer \(\mathbf v\) and suppose \(v_i > v_j\) while \(y'_i < y'_j\). Swap \(v_i\) and \(v_j\). Compute the change: \((v_i - v_j)(y'_j - y'_i) > 0\).
  2. So a maximizer has no such inverted pair: \(\mathbf v\) orders positions as \(\mathbf y'\) orders scores, which is \(\pi(\mathbf y')\). Uniqueness: every other permutation has an inverted pair and can be strictly improved.
  3. For NDCG: \(G(\mathbf v, \mathbf y) = \sum_i D(v_i) y_i\). The same swap changes it by \((D(v_j) - D(v_i))(y_i - y_j)\), which is \(\ge 0\) when it puts the larger \(D\) on the larger \(y\) (\(D\) nondecreasing). Sort to reach \(\pi(\mathbf y)\) without ever decreasing \(G\); with ties in \(\mathbf y\), any tie order gives the same \(G\).
- Not chosen: Exercise 5 (the two definitions of \(b\)), mentioned in the prose as “a good exercise” for \(V_{\ge k}\), \(V_{\le k}\); offer it after the concept check if the learner wants more.

True and elementary; flagged as ours.
- The book rewrites the Kendall indicator as \(\mathbb 1[\operatorname{sign}(y_i - y_j)(y'_i - y'_j) \le 0]\) with equality; for pairs with \(y_i = y_j\) and \(y'_i = y'_j\) the left side is 0 and the right side 1, so the prose states it as \(\le\) (the surrogate bound is unaffected).
- The book writes the zero-one loss in the bipartite section as \((b + d)/(a + b + c + d)\) (p. 245); that is a typo, the prose uses \((b + c)/(a + b + c + d)\).
- The sign convention for thresholds (\(\operatorname{sign}(0) = -1\) at the threshold) and “precision at \(k\) with exactly the top \(k\) flagged” (the book requires \(a + b \ge k\)) are our concrete choices, stated in the prose.
- Ties in \(\pi(\mathbf y)\) when relevances repeat are broken by index (stable sort); the book does not specify.
- The certification step (lesson 11’s hold-out guarantee applied to one minus per-morning precision at 5, independent mornings as examples) is ours.
