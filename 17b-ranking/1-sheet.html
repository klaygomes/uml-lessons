Misconception: “98% accuracy means the important messages are found”. Why wrong: with 2% important, the all-no filter has zero-one loss 0.02, recall 0, F₁ 0.

Second: “precision at 5 can be trained by thresholding a classifier”. Why wrong: a classifier chooses its own threshold for the zero-one loss; precision at 5 fixes the number flagged and cares only about the top (lab: flagged-first 0.51 to 0.55 vs sorted 0.68). Third (new in the split): “a lower Kendall tau loss means a better top of the list”. Why wrong: on the six messages, swapping only the top two gives Kendall 1/15 = 0.067 and NDCG loss 0.041; top three right with the bottom three reversed gives Kendall 0.2 and NDCG loss 0 (nums.py). Fourth (new in the split): “dropping the 0/1 constraint gives a fractional order you cannot use”. Why wrong: the lemma; some optimal solution of the relaxed LP is a permutation matrix.
