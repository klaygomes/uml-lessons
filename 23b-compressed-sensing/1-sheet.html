Overview

Objective: after this, you can explain why no recovery matrix can undo a squeeze even for one-word messages, so the decoder must be nonlinear; define sparsity and the RIP, prove that RIP for 2s2s2s makes ℓ0\ell_0ℓ0​ decoding exact and that RIP with ε<1/(1+2)\varepsilon < 1/(1+\sqrt2)ε<1/(1+2​) makes ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ decoding (a linear program) exact for sparse and stable for almost-sparse vectors; prove that Gaussian matrices are RIP with n=O(sln⁡(d/ε)/ε2)n = O(s\ln(d/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^2)n=O(sln(d/ε)/ε2) rows; compute the RIP constant of a unit-column matrix for 2s=22s = 22s=2 as its largest column dot product; choose between PCA and compressed sensing by their assumptions; and certify a small desk by a result of the lesson rather than by test messages.

Misconception: "a better recovery matrix would fix the smear". Why wrong: right on every one-word message makes it right on every message (messages are sums of eje_jej​, matrices respect sums), and the null space forbids that.

Second: "exact recovery of a squeezed message is impossible". Why wrong: impossible for every message; possible for every sparse one when the null space has no sparse vector.

Third: "the linear program recovered my test messages, so the matrix is fine". Why wrong: success on tests is not a guarantee; in the build-it the program recovers the two-word (1,0,0,2,0,0)(1,0,0,2,0,0)(1,0,0,2,0,0) from three numbers, yet some two-word messages share their stored numbers (icosahedron: 30 of 30 in the lab and 0.447 above 0.414).

Fourth: "the RIP theorem's constants say how many measurements you need". Why wrong: 216 comes from a crude proof; at the mail room's size it asks for 2.49 million rows.

Derivation:

  • Why no matrix: linearity and the null space (the lesson's own, not in the book).
  • Johnson-Lindenstrauss recalled as lesson 23a states it: QQQ of nonzero vectors, ε=6ln⁡(2∣Q∣/δ)/n≤1/2\varepsilon = \sqrt{6\ln(2|Q|/\delta)/n} \le 1/2ε=6ln(2∣Q∣/δ)/n​≤1/2 (the book's Lemma 23.4, p. 329, allows ε≤3\varepsilon \le 3ε≤3; lesson 23a's sheet records why the range is cut). The subspace lemma uses it at accuracy ε/2≤1/2\varepsilon/2 \le 1/2ε/2≤1/2, so nothing here needs the wider range.
  • Theorem 23.6 as in the book (the 2s2s2s comes from the difference of two s-sparse vectors). Theorem 23.8 (and 23.7 as its special case) following the book's Candès proof step by step; Lemma 23.10 proved for both signs.
  • Lemma 23.11 replaced by the greedy packing net with ∣N∣≤(1+2/ε)s|N| \le (1 + 2/\varepsilon)^s∣N∣≤(1+2/ε)s; Lemma 23.12 as in the book with net points on the sphere; Theorem 23.9 proved with accuracy ε/3\varepsilon/3ε/3, confidence δ/(ds)\delta/\binom dsδ/(sd​), giving n≥216(ln⁡(2/δ)+sln⁡(36d/ε))/ε2n \ge 216(\ln(2/\delta) + s\ln(36d/\varepsilon))/\varepsilon^2n≥216(ln(2/δ)+sln(36d/ε))/ε2. See Assumptions.
  • RIP constant of a unit-column matrix for 2s=22s = 22s=2 (new): eigenvalues of (1cc1)\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{pmatrix}(1c​c1​) are 1±∣c∣1 \pm |c|1±∣c∣.
