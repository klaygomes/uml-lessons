The Runtime of Learning

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can state what it means for a learner to solve a learning task in time O(f)O(f)O(f) and to be efficient along a sequence of problems (including the prediction-time clause that defeats the lazy learner), explain why runtime is not measured against mmm, compute the cost of exhaustive ERM over a finite class, run and prove correct the realizable ERM algorithms for axis aligned rectangles (tightest box, O(nm)O(nm)O(nm)) and Boolean conjunctions (crossing out literals, O(nm)O(nm)O(nm)), prove that subset search is an exact agnostic ERM for rectangles in time mO(n)m^{O(n)}mO(n), state the hardness of agnostic ERM (unless P=NPP = NPP=NP) and of proper 3-term DNF learning (unless RP=NPRP = NPRP=NP), prove the 3-term DNF to 3-CNF identity and use the clause features ψ\psiψ to learn 3-term DNF efficiently, weigh the sample complexity against the runtime of a representation, and explain how trapdoor one-way functions give a class that is small but not efficiently learnable.

Misconception: "ERM over H\mathcal HH is NP-hard, so H\mathcal HH is not efficiently learnable". Why wrong: it only rules out proper learners (under RP≠NPRP \ne NPRP=NP); 3-term DNF is efficiently learnable through 3-CNF.

Second misconception: "realizable and agnostic are the same problem". Why wrong: statistically yes (finite VC dimension in both), computationally no: O(nm)O(nm)O(nm) against NP-hard for rectangles and conjunctions.

Third misconception: "a smaller class is always cheaper". Why wrong: 3-term DNF is smaller than 3-CNF and cheaper in labels (2,070 against 4,746,098 at n=30n = 30n=30) but intractable for proper learners.
