Misconception: “nearest neighbour has no inductive bias”. Why wrong: its bias is the metric, low dimension and a small Lipschitz constant; the lower bound shows that without them no learner succeeds below (c+1)d/2(c+1)^d/2(c+1)d/2 labels.

Second: “more scores can only help”. Why wrong: on invoices 30 scores give 0.32 against 0.14 for 2, with η\etaη and the Bayes risk unchanged.

Third: “1-NN converges to the Bayes risk”. Why wrong: the guarantee is 2×2\times2×; the measured limit is E[2η(1−η)]\mathbb E[2\eta(1-\eta)]E[2η(1−η)] (0.151 here, Bayes 0.115).

Fourth: “m−1/(d+1)m^{-1/(d+1)}m−1/(d+1) is the real rate”. Why wrong: box argument; in 2D the measured distance falls like m−1/2m^{-1/2}m−1/2. The exponential in ddd is real (lower bound).

Fifth: “larger kkk is always safer”. Why wrong: the k-NN certificate charges k m−1/(d+1)k\,m^{-1/(d+1)}km−1/(d+1); at this budget the best certified kkk is 1 (although k=15k = 15k=15 measures better).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):

  1. A c-Lipschitz η\etaη is continuous; on the connected cube a continuous function with values in {0,1}\{0, 1\}{0,1} is constant (along the segment between a 0-point and a 1-point it would have to pass through 1/2). So every message has the same label, the Bayes risk is 0, and the guarantee says E[LD(hS)]≤4cd m−1/(d+1)\mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(h_S)] \le 4c\sqrt d\, m^{-1/(d+1)}E[LD​(hS​)]≤4cd​m−1/(d+1); in fact the 1-NN rule’s risk is exactly 0, since every training label is the same. The lower-bound tasks are not a contradiction because their labels are deterministic only on the support CCC (a finite grid subset); the c-Lipschitz η\etaη on the cube is the extension η~\tilde\etaη~​, which takes values strictly between 0 and 1 off the grid, where the task puts no probability. Look for: continuity plus connectedness, and the support distinction.
  2.
