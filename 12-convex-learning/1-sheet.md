# Convex Learning Problems

Objective: after this, you can define convex sets, convex functions (chord and epigraph forms), Lipschitzness and smoothness; prove that local minima of convex functions are global, the tangent inequality, the one-variable convexity test, the composition, maximum and sum rules, the Lipschitz composition rule, the quadratic upper bound and self-boundedness of smooth functions, and smoothness through a linear function; say what a convex learning problem is and prove that its ERM problem is convex; prove that convexity alone (and convexity plus boundedness) does not give learnability; state the convex-Lipschitz-bounded and convex-smooth-bounded conditions and check them for the hinge, absolute and square losses; define a convex surrogate, prove the hinge loss is one for the 0-1 loss, and split the 0-1 risk of a surrogate learner into approximation, optimization and estimation error; run a smooth surrogate ERM with a certified stopping rule and state its cost.

Terms:
- convex set: contains \(\alpha\mathbf u + (1 - \alpha)\mathbf v\) for all \(\mathbf u, \mathbf v\) in it and \(\alpha \in [0, 1]\).
- convex function: \(f(\alpha\mathbf u + (1 - \alpha)\mathbf v) \le \alpha f(\mathbf u) + (1 - \alpha) f(\mathbf v)\); equivalently its epigraph is convex.
- \(\rho\)-Lipschitz: \(\|f(\mathbf w_1) - f(\mathbf w_2)\| \le \rho\|\mathbf w_1 - \mathbf w_2\|\).
- \(\beta\)-smooth: differentiable with \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient.
- self-bounded: \(\|\nabla f(\mathbf w)\|^2 \le 2\beta f(\mathbf w)\) (smooth and nonnegative).
- convex learning problem: \(\mathcal H\) convex and every \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) convex.
- convex-Lipschitz-bounded (\(\rho, B\)); convex-smooth-bounded (\(\beta, B\); loss also nonnegative).
- convex surrogate loss: convex and at least the original loss everywhere.
- hinge loss: \(\max\{0, 1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle\}\).
- optimization error: \(\min_{\mathcal H} L^{\text{hinge}}_{\mathcal D} - \min_{\mathcal H} L^{0-1}_{\mathcal D}\).

Misconception: "a convex loss makes the problem learnable". Why wrong: one-dimensional linear regression with the square loss is convex and not learnable (whatever \(\hat w\), one of two worlds charges excess at least 0.99 at \(m = 100\)).
Second: "bounding the weights fixes it". Why wrong: the bounded counterexample (\(|w| \le 1\), \(z_1 = (1/\mu, 0)\)) still fails; the loss's Lipschitz and smoothness constants are \(2/\mu^2\), unbounded.
Third: "minimizing the hinge loss minimizes mistakes". Why wrong: only an upper bound; optimization error 0.1738 in the lab world at \(B = 1.5\).
Fourth: "any convex loss is a surrogate". Why wrong: natural-log logistic is \(\log 2 < 1\) at score 0.
Fifth: "bigger \(B\) is always better". Why wrong: \(\rho B/\sqrt m\) grows with \(B\); hinge floor stops falling near \(B = 2.7\).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(g(a) = (\max\{0, a\})^2\) is convex: \(g\) is differentiable with \(g'(a) = 2\max\{0, a\}\) nondecreasing, and the lemma's proof of 2 ⇒ 1 uses only the mean value theorem, so it needs one derivative, not two (alternatively, check the chord directly on the cases \(a \le 0\), \(a > 0\)); composed with the affine \(1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x\rangle\) it is convex by the composition claim. Upper bound: for \(z \le 0\), \((1 - z)^2 \ge 1\); for \(z > 0\), \(\ge 0\). So a convex surrogate. Lipschitz over the ball: \(|g'| \le 2(1 + RB)\) on reachable scores (\(|y\langle\mathbf w,\mathbf x\rangle| \le RB\)), times \(\|\mathbf x\| \le R\): \(\rho = 2R(1 + RB)\) (72.17 for \(R = \sqrt{21}\), \(B = 1.5\)); convex-Lipschitz-bounded with \((2R(1 + RB), B)\). Smooth: \(g'\) is 2-Lipschitz, so \(\beta = 2\|\mathbf x\|^2 \le 2R^2\); nonnegative; convex-smooth-bounded with \((2R^2, B)\). Not Lipschitz over \(\mathbb R^d\) (grows quadratically). Look for: the learner noting the one-variable lemma as stated needs twice differentiability, which fails at \(a = 0\); accept the chord argument or the "max then square of a nonnegative" argument.
2. Determinism: "Because \(A\) is deterministic, on \(S_2\) it outputs one fixed number" (step 2): the same \(\hat w\) is produced in both worlds, so the adversary can pick the world after seeing \(\hat w\). The learner must succeed with probability \(\ge 1 - \delta = 1/2\); failure on the event \(E\) has probability \(\ge 0.99 > \delta\), so the guarantee is violated; if \(\mathbb P[E]\) were only positive but below \(\delta\), a learner could fail on \(E\) and still meet the definition. Bounded version: \(\mathcal H = [-1, 1]\) is convex and bounded (B = 1), the loss is convex, but \(\ell(w, z_1) = (w/\mu)^2\) has \(|\partial_w| = 2|w|/\mu^2 \le 2/\mu^2\): Lipschitz only with \(\rho = 2/\mu^2\), and \(\mu = \log(100/99)/(2m) \to 0\) as \(m\) grows, so no \(\rho\) fixed in advance works (equivalently, \(\|x\| = 1/\mu\) unbounded; also not smooth with fixed \(\beta\): \(\beta = 2/\mu^2\)). Also accept: a randomized learner needs footnote-level care (the book's footnote 1).
3. Hinge, \(B = 1.9\): \(\rho B/\sqrt{3000} = 4.5826 \cdot 1.9/54.77 = 0.159\); bound \(0.2003 + 0.159 = 0.3593 \le 0.36\); labels needed \(\lceil (8.707/0.1597)^2 \rceil = 2{,}973\). Base-2 logistic, \(B = 4\): \(\rho = R/\log 2 = 6.611\), \(\rho B/\sqrt{3000} = 0.4828\), bound 0.850; labels for 0.36: impossible, floor 0.3676 > 0.36. Hand over: hinge (and \(B = 1.5\), bound 0.349, is better still). Look for: noticing that the logistic's higher Lipschitz constant and higher floor both hurt, and that "smooth" does not help this bound.
