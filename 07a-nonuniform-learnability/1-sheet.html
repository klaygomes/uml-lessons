Nonuniform Learnability

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define nonuniform learnability and say how it differs from agnostic PAC learnability; prove the SRM bound (countable union bound over classes with weights), prove that SRM is a nonuniform learner and that nonuniform learnability is equivalent to being a countable union of agnostic PAC learnable classes; compute the price of not knowing the size; run SRM with a stopping rule and state its cost.

Book: Chapter 7, Sections 7.1 and 7.2 (PDF pp. 83 to 89). Uses Definition 3.3 (p. 46), Lemma 2.2 union bound (p. 39), Definition 4.3 (p. 55), Lemma 4.5 Hoeffding (p. 56), Corollary 4.6 (p. 57), Theorem 5.1 and Equation (5.1) (pp. 61 to 62), Theorems 6.7 and 6.8 (pp. 72 to 73). Sections 7.3 to 7.6 (MDL, Occam, consistency, discussion) are lesson 7b. This lesson is the first half of the original lesson 7, split at "Lists by the bit" so each sitting stays manageable.

Misconception: "nonuniformly learnable means PAC learnable, only slower". Why wrong: the sample size depends on the hypothesis you compete with, which you do not know in advance; decision lists of any length remain not PAC learnable.

Second misconception: "SRM ships the list with the smallest true risk". Why wrong: it ships the smallest certified bound; at 30 labels it ships "everything is junk" (true risk 0.375) though cap-3 ERM lists have 0.125 to 0.175.

Third misconception: "set the weights after seeing which list fits". Why wrong: weights must be independent of SSS, or the bad events BnB_nBn​ are not the ones whose shares were budgeted (the same point returns in lesson 7b as the Occam language puzzle, p. 91).
