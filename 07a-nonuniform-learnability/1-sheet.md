# Nonuniform Learnability

Objective: after this, you can define nonuniform learnability and say how it differs from agnostic PAC learnability; prove the SRM bound (countable union bound over classes with weights), prove that SRM is a nonuniform learner and that nonuniform learnability is equivalent to being a countable union of agnostic PAC learnable classes; compute the price of not knowing the size; run SRM with a stopping rule and state its cost.

Sections 7.3 to 7.6 (MDL, Occam, consistency, discussion) are lesson 7b. This lesson is the first half of the original lesson 7, split at "Lists by the bit" so each sitting stays manageable.

Key words: decision list (not "rule" for the hypothesis; "learning rule", "SRM rule", "ERM rule" are the book's names for learners and stay), test (one line of a list), size (not "length" in formal statements; "length" allowed in the story), \(\mathcal H_n\) (lists of size at most \(n\)), \(n(h)\), message, feature, junk / keep, example, training sequence (not "training set", "sample" for \(S\)), hypothesis, learner, hypothesis class, empirical risk, true risk, ERM, agnostic PAC learnable, uniform convergence property, VC dimension, shatter, fundamental theorem, accuracy \(\varepsilon\) (never \(\epsilon\)), confidence \(\delta\), sample size \(m\), confidence budget (the story word for \(\delta\) being split), weight function \(w\) (not "prior" as a noun), gap (\(\varepsilon_n(m, \delta)\); not "slack", "penalty"), bound (empirical risk plus gap; not "score", "objective" in prose), certificate (the story word for a shipped bound), competitive, nonuniformly learnable, SRM.

Terms:
- decision list: \(h = ((j_1, b_1), \dots, (j_k, b_k); b_0)\); returns \(b_i\) for the first \(i\) with \(x_{j_i} = 1\), else \(b_0\).
- size, \(\mathcal H_n\): \(\mathcal H_n\) = lists with at most \(n\) tests on distinct features among \(1..n\); size \(n(h) = \min\{n : h \in \mathcal H_n\} = \max(1, k, \max_i j_i)\); \(|\mathcal H_n| = 2\sum_{k=0}^n 2^k n!/(n-k)!\).
- \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-competitive: with probability at least \(1 - \delta\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le L_{\mathcal D}(h') + \varepsilon\) (p. 83; the book says "higher than", the lesson uses "at least" to match Definition 7.1, a choice).
- nonuniformly learnable (Def. 7.1, p. 84): \(\exists A, m^{\mathrm{NUL}}_{\mathcal H}: (0,1)^2 \times \mathcal H \to \mathbb N\), \(\forall \varepsilon, \delta, h\), \(m \ge m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(\varepsilon, \delta, h) \Rightarrow \forall \mathcal D\), w.p. \(\ge 1 - \delta\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(A(S)) \le L_{\mathcal D}(h) + \varepsilon\).
- weight function: \(w: \mathbb N \to [0,1]\), \(\sum_n w(n) \le 1\); \(6/(\pi^2 n^2)\) or \(2^{-n}\) (p. 86).
- gap \(\varepsilon_n(m, \delta) = \min\{\varepsilon \in (0,1) : m^{\mathrm{UC}}_{\mathcal H_n}(\varepsilon, \delta) \le m\}\) (7.1, p. 86); for finite \(\mathcal H_n\) the lesson uses \(\sqrt{\log(2|\mathcal H_n|/\delta)/(2m)}\).
- SRM: \(\operatorname{argmin}_h L_S(h) + \varepsilon_{n(h)}(m, w(n(h))\delta)\) (p. 87).

Misconception: "nonuniformly learnable means PAC learnable, only slower". Why wrong: the sample size depends on the hypothesis you compete with, which you do not know in advance; decision lists of any length remain not PAC learnable.
Second misconception: "SRM ships the list with the smallest true risk". Why wrong: it ships the smallest certified bound; at 30 labels it ships "everything is junk" (true risk 0.375) though cap-3 ERM lists have 0.125 to 0.175.
Third misconception: "set the weights after seeing which list fits". Why wrong: weights must be independent of \(S\), or the bad events \(B_n\) are not the ones whose shares were budgeted (the same point returns in lesson 7b as the Occam language puzzle, p. 91).

Derivation (proofs in the prose):
- Nonuniform from agnostic PAC: \(m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(\varepsilon,\delta,h) = m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon,\delta)\) (p. 84).
- SRM bound (Thm 7.4, pp. 86 to 87): bad events \(B_n\), \(\mathcal D^m(B_n) \le w(n)\delta\) by uniform convergence; finite union bound then continuity from below for the countable union; min over classes containing \(h\); convention \(\varepsilon_n(m, 0) = \infty\).
- SRM nonuniform learner (Thm 7.5, pp. 87 to 88), four steps on a single good event \(G\): bound applied to the data-dependent \(A(S)\) (simultaneity), SRM optimality, two-sidedness for \(h\), fact 2 on the sample size. The book draws step 3 from a separate uniform-convergence event; the lesson uses the same simultaneous event, which avoids a second \(\delta\).
- Characterization (Thm 7.2, p. 84): "if" via fundamental theorem plus SRM; "only if" with \(\mathcal H_n = \{h : m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(1/10, 1/10, h) \le n\}\) and the expectation form of No-Free-Lunch (5.1): \(\mathbb E \ge 1/4\) vs \(\le 1/10 + 1/10 = 1/5\), so \(\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal H_n) < 2n\). The book uses \((1/8, 1/7)\), where the probability form of NFL only matches at the boundary; the lesson's constants follow Exercise 5's (0.1, 0.1) and make the contradiction strict. Concept-check question 2 asks the learner to discover why (1/8, 1/7) fails with the expectation form.
- Decision lists: infinite VC (shatter \(e_1..e_k\) with \(((1,y_1),\dots,(k,y_k);0) \in \mathcal H_k\)); each \(\mathcal H_n\) finite; so nonuniformly but not PAC learnable (parallel to Example 7.1, polynomial classifiers, p. 85).
- Price of nonuniformity: finite-class version of the book's \(4C \cdot 2\log(2n)/\varepsilon^2\) (pp. 88 to 89).
- Remark 7.2 (p. 88) stated, proof deferred (Exercise 5).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(\sum 1/n\) diverges, so step 2 of the proof (union of bad events has probability at most \(\delta \sum w(n) \le \delta\)) fails: the failure probabilities may add to more than 1 and the theorem promises nothing (cannot be normalized either). Accept "the harmonic series diverges" with the step named. \(w(7) = 0\) with the others at \(6/(\pi^2 n^2)\): legal (sum is \(1 - 6/(49\pi^2) \le 1\)). \(\varepsilon_7(m, 0) = \infty\), so in the rule written with \(n(h)\) a list of size exactly 7 has an infinite bound and never ships. Full credit adds the subtlety: the classes are nested, so a size-7 list is also in \(\mathcal H_8\), and the general bound \(\min_{n : h \in \mathcal H_n}\) still certifies it through the size-8 gap; it pays size 8's price. (SRM-nested's stopping rule assumed nondecreasing gaps; \(g_7 = \infty\) breaks that, so the loop must skip size 7 rather than stop there.)
2. Split the expectation as in the proof: \(\mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D_i}(A(S))] \le \tfrac18 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot \tfrac17 = \tfrac{15}{56} \approx 0.268\). That is not below 1/4, so it does not contradict the expectation form of No-Free-Lunch (\(\mathbb E \ge 1/4\)): the argument fails. In general the bound is \(\varepsilon + \delta\), and the argument works exactly when \(\varepsilon + \delta < 1/4\) (the lesson's 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2; any \(\varepsilon = \delta < 1/8\)). Full credit notes that the \(\mathcal H_n\) are then defined with that pair, and that any fixed pair with \(\varepsilon + \delta < 1/4\) gives \(\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal H_n) < 2n\). Bonus: the book's (1/8, 1/7) goes with the probability form of No-Free-Lunch (Theorem 5.1: with probability at least 1/7, risk at least 1/8), where it only matches at the boundary.
