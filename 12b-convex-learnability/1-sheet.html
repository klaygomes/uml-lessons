Convex Learnability and Surrogates

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can prove that convexity alone (and convexity plus boundedness) does not give learnability; state the convex-Lipschitz-bounded and convex-smooth-bounded conditions and check them for the hinge, absolute and square losses; quote the ρB/m\rho B/\sqrt mρB/m​ rate and turn it into a label count; define a convex surrogate, prove the hinge loss is one for the 0-1 loss, and split the 0-1 risk of a surrogate learner into approximation, optimization and estimation error; run a smooth surrogate ERM with a certified stopping rule and state its cost; and choose a surrogate and a norm bound that certify a target 0-1 risk within a label budget.

Misconception: "a convex loss makes the problem learnable". Why wrong: one-dimensional linear regression with the square loss is convex and not learnable (whatever w^\hat ww^, one of two worlds charges excess at least 0.99 at m=100m = 100m=100; exactly 0.986).

Second: "bounding the weights fixes it". Why wrong: the bounded counterexample (∣w∣≤1|w| \le 1∣w∣≤1, z1=(1/μ,0)z_1 = (1/\mu, 0)z1​=(1/μ,0)) still fails; the loss's Lipschitz and smoothness constants are 2/μ22/\mu^22/μ2, unbounded.

Third: "minimizing the hinge loss minimizes mistakes". Why wrong: only an upper bound; optimization error 0.1738 in the lab world at B=1.5B = 1.5B=1.5.

Fourth: "any convex loss is a surrogate". Why wrong: natural-log logistic is log⁡2<1\log 2 < 1log2<1 at score 0.

Fifth: "bigger BBB is always better". Why wrong: ρB/m\rho B/\sqrt mρB/m​ grows with BBB; hinge floor stops falling near B=2.7B = 2.7B=2.7. Sixth (act 1): "more labels always help".
