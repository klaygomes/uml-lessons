# Strong Convexity and the Fitting-Stability Tradeoff

Terms:
- \(\sigma\)-strongly convex: \(f(\alpha\mathbf w + (1-\alpha)\mathbf u) \le \alpha f(\mathbf w) + (1-\alpha)f(\mathbf u) - \frac\sigma2\alpha(1-\alpha)\|\mathbf w - \mathbf u\|^2\).
- stability inequality: \(\lambda\|A(S^{(i)}) - A(S)\|^2 \le \frac1m[\ell(A(S^{(i)}), z_i) - \ell(A(S), z_i)] + \frac1m[\ell(A(S), z') - \ell(A(S^{(i)}), z')]\).
- Lipschitz rate: \(2\rho^2/(\lambda m)\); smooth rate: \(\frac{24\beta}{\lambda m}\mathbb E[L_S(A(S))]\) for \(\lambda \ge 2\beta/m\).
- oracle inequality: \(\mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(A(S))] \le L_{\mathcal D}(\mathbf w^*) + \lambda\|\mathbf w^*\|^2 + 2\rho^2/(\lambda m)\) for every \(\mathbf w^*\).
- certificate (lab): \((1 + 24\beta/(\lambda m)) \times \mathbb E[L_S(A(S))]\), valid when \(\lambda \ge 2\beta/m\).

Misconception (new in the split): "strong convexity is just convexity with a fancier name". Why wrong: convexity allows flat stretches (the valley); strong convexity forces a sag growing with the squared chord length, the floor the stability proof stands on; the penalty supplies it when the loss has none (concept-check 2: a single half square loss in \(\mathbb R^5\) has \(\sigma = 0\)).
Second: "more \(\lambda\) is always safer". Why wrong: the fit rises with \(\lambda\) for every \(S\) (proved); the certificate is U-shaped (one-word table: minimum 0.446 near \(\lambda = 0.1\)).
Third: "the bound's best \(\lambda\) is the true best". Why wrong: in the one-word world the bound prefers 0.1 while the true risk prefers the smallest \(\lambda\).
Fourth: "these are high-probability guarantees". Why wrong: they bound expectations; Problem 1 (Exercise 1) converts.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner):
1. Ridge as \(\lambda \to \infty\): \((2\lambda mI + A)^{-1}\mathbf b \to \mathbf 0\); fit \(\to L_S(\mathbf 0) = \frac12\cdot\text{mean}(y^2) = 0.5\); stability term \(\frac{24\beta}{\lambda m}\mathbb E[L_S] \to 0\); certificate \(\to 0.5\). The certificate is \((1 + \text{a positive number}) \times\) the expected fit, so it is never below the fit; no \(\lambda\) can certify less than what the learner fits. Look for "stable but does not fit".
2. In \(\mathbb R^5\): for any direction \(\mathbf u \perp \mathbf x\), \(t \mapsto \ell(\mathbf w + t\mathbf u)\) is constant, so the chord along \(\mathbf u\) lies on the graph and no positive margin is possible: \(\sigma = 0\) (convex, not strongly convex). In one dimension with \(x = \pm0.4\), \(\ell(w) = \frac12(xw - y)^2\) is a parabola with second derivative \(x^2 = 0.16\), and its chord sag is exactly \(\frac{0.16}{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)(w-u)^2\): 0.16-strongly convex (checked at random points, sags equal, e.g. 0.014576 = 0.014576). An average of losses \(L_S\) is strongly convex with modulus equal to its smallest curvature, which on night 1 of the campaign is at most 0.0019 (the shuffle directions), and for \(m < d\) is exactly 0. The penalty adds \(2\lambda\) in every direction regardless of the data (0.2019 at \(\lambda = 0.1\)), which is why the proof takes \(\sigma = 2\lambda\) from the penalty: it holds for every distribution.
3. Absolute loss on the unit ball: \(\rho = 1\). Lipschitz corollary: \(m \ge 8 \cdot 1 \cdot 4/0.01 = 3{,}200\), \(\lambda = \sqrt{2/(4 \cdot 3200)} = 0.0125\). Ridge: \(\beta = 1\), \(m \ge 150 \cdot 4/0.01 = 60{,}000\), \(\lambda = 0.1/12 \approx 0.00833 \ge 2/60{,}000 \approx 0.0000333\). (Computed by script.)
