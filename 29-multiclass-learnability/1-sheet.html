Multiclass Learnability

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define multiclass shattering and the Natarajan dimension, compute it for small classes by hand and by brute force, prove that it equals the VC dimension for two labels, prove Natarajan's lemma, state the multiclass fundamental theorem and prove its qualitative upper half and its lower bounds, bound the Natarajan dimension of one-versus-all, of any multiclass-to-binary reduction and of linear multiclass predictors (with the multivector sandwich), and explain and prove why two ERMs for the same class can need wildly different numbers of labels, so that learnability, uniform convergence and "every ERM learns" come apart with many labels.

Misconception: "with kkk labels, shattering means all k∣C∣k^{|C|}k∣C∣ filings" · Why wrong: urgency levels never file a higher score lower yet are learnable with Ndim k−1k - 1k−1; the definition asks only for mixtures of two candidates.

Second: "any ERM will do" · Why wrong: on 1,000 senders the bad ERM fails on every training sequence of size ≤ 499 under the claim's distribution (true risk ≥ 5%), the good ERM is certified at 60 labels; with infinitely many senders the bad ERM never learns.

Third: "learnable implies uniform convergence" · Why wrong: auto-folders over an infinite sender set are learnable (good ERM) but lack UC, since UC would make every ERM learn.

Fourth: "more folders means proportionally more labels" · Why wrong: kkk enters only as log⁡k\log klogk (300 to 3,000 folders multiplies log⁡k\log klogk by 1.40).
