# Multiclass Learnability

Objective: after this, you can define multiclass shattering and the Natarajan dimension, compute it for small classes by hand and by brute force, prove that it equals the VC dimension for two labels, prove Natarajan's lemma, state the multiclass fundamental theorem and prove its qualitative upper half and its lower bounds, bound the Natarajan dimension of one-versus-all, of any multiclass-to-binary reduction and of linear multiclass predictors (with the multivector sandwich), and explain and prove why two ERMs for the same class can need wildly different numbers of labels, so that learnability, uniform convergence and "every ERM learns" come apart with many labels.

Terms:
- multiclass shattering: \(C\) is shattered by \(\mathcal H \subseteq [k]^{\mathcal X}\) if there are \(f_0, f_1: C \to [k]\) with \(f_0(x) \ne f_1(x)\) everywhere and every mixture (\(f_0\) on \(B\), \(f_1\) on \(C \setminus B\), for every \(B \subseteq C\)) is achieved by some \(h \in \mathcal H\).
- Natarajan dimension: largest size of a shattered set (\(\infty\) if unbounded); equals VCdim when \(k = 2\).
- Natarajan's lemma: \(|\mathcal H| \le |\mathcal X|^{d} k^{2d}\), \(d = \mathrm{Ndim}(\mathcal H)\), \(\mathcal X\) finite.
- multiclass fundamental theorem: UC, agnostic and realizable sample complexities between \(C_1(d + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^{2\text{ or }1}\) and \(C_2(d\log k + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\), resp. \(C_2(d\log(kd/\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon\).
- reduction class \(\mathcal H^r_{\mathrm{bin}} = \{x \mapsto r(h_1(x), \dots, h_l(x))\}\); one-versus-all is \(l = k\), \(r\) = first 1 (ties to smallest, 1 if none).
- \(\mathcal H_\Psi = \{x \mapsto \operatorname{argmax}_i \langle \mathbf w, \Psi(x, i)\rangle : \mathbf w \in \mathbb R^d\}\), ties to the smallest folder number.
- auto-folders: \(h_A(x) = A\) if \(x \in A\), \(\ast\) otherwise, \(A\) finite or cofinite; \(A_{\mathrm{good}}(S) = h_\emptyset\), \(A_{\mathrm{bad}}(S) = h_{\{x_1..x_m\}^c}\) on all-inbox samples.

Misconception: "with \(k\) labels, shattering means all \(k^{|C|}\) filings" · Why wrong: urgency levels never file a higher score lower yet are learnable with Ndim \(k - 1\); the definition asks only for mixtures of two candidates.
Second: "any ERM will do" · Why wrong: on 1,000 senders the bad ERM fails on every training sequence of size ≤ 499 under the claim's distribution (true risk ≥ 5%), the good ERM is certified at 60 labels; with infinitely many senders the bad ERM never learns.
Third: "learnable implies uniform convergence" · Why wrong: auto-folders over an infinite sender set are learnable (good ERM) but lack UC, since UC would make every ERM learn.
Fourth: "more folders means proportionally more labels" · Why wrong: \(k\) enters only as \(\log k\) (300 to 3,000 folders multiplies \(\log k\) by 1.40).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Constant rules: Ndim 1. One message: candidates 1 and 2, achieved by the constants 1 and 2. Two messages \(x_1, x_2\): the mixture \(B = \{x_1\}\) needs \(f_0(x_1) = f_1(x_2)\) (one constant), \(B = \emptyset\) needs \(f_1(x_1) = f_1(x_2)\), so \(f_0(x_1) = f_1(x_1)\), contradicting distinct candidates (any two mixtures that differ only at \(x_1\) give the contradiction). All functions \(\mathcal X \to [k]\), \(|\mathcal X| = n\): Ndim \(n\), with \(f_0 \equiv 1\), \(f_1 \equiv 2\). Look for the learner naming the failing mixture, not just "constants can't vary".
2. A set \(C\) shattered by \(\mathcal H_{ij}\) with \(f_0, f_1\), extended by \(f_0(x_0) = i\), \(f_1(x_0) = j\) (distinct because \(i < j\)); for \(B \subseteq C \cup \{x_0\}\), a trace \(g\) realizes the mixture on \(C\), and because \(g\) is completable both ways, the completion with \(i\) (if \(x_0 \in B\)) or \(j\) (otherwise) is in \(\mathcal H\): so \(\mathcal H\) shatters \(|C| + 1\) points, hence \(|C| \le d - 1\). With "or", a trace completable only with \(i\) would be included; the mixture needing \(j\) at \(x_0\) would have no hypothesis, the shattering of \(C \cup \{x_0\}\) fails, and \(\mathcal H_{ij}\) could have dimension \(d\) (for instance, it could be all of \(\mathcal H_{\mathcal X'}\)); also the splitting step's count would still hold but the induction would not close.
3. (a) \(\lceil \log(100)/0.05 \rceil = \lceil 92.1 \rceil = 93\). (b) \(m \le (600-1)/6 = 99.8\), so up to 99 (and true risk ≥ \(\varepsilon\) for \(m \le 299\)).
