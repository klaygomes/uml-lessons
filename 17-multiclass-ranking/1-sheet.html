Bug: users want four folders (work, personal, promotions, junk) and an inbox ranked by importance. The team bolts four yes/no halfspaces together with one-versus-all: messages get two yeses or none, and a whole kind of work mail is never filed right; an “important?” filter is 98% accurate by saying no to everything (2% important).

Fix: a linear multiclass predictor with a class-sensitive feature map, trained with the generalized hinge loss and the users’ cost table by multiclass SGD; a linear ranker trained and judged by the measure users care about (precision at 5), certified on fresh mail. Act 2: the learner chooses the folder learner (one-versus-all, multiclass zero-one, multiclass with the users’ cost) and the ranking measure (accuracy, F₁ at 0, AUC, precision at 5), splits a fresh-label budget, and must certify folder cost ≤ 0.06 and precision at 5 ≥ 0.6 jointly with 95% confidence.

Misconception: “if every yes/no filter is optimal, the combination is optimal”. Why wrong: in the three-disc world every filter is the best halfspace for its own question, yet one-versus-all misfiles all work mail (20%), while three weight vectors classify perfectly.

Second misconception: “98% accuracy means the important messages are found”. Why wrong: with 2% important, the all-no filter has zero-one loss 0.02, recall 0, F₁ 0.

Third: “many labels need many examples”. Why wrong: the multiclass SVM and SGD bounds have no explicit ∣Y∣|\mathcal Y|∣Y∣; it enters only through ρ\rhoρ and BBB (Remark 17.3).

Fourth: “ties are a corner case” and “generalized hinge is one hinge per class” (prose, What people get wrong).
