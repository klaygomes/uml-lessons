Overview

Misconception: “the kernel trick makes the feature space small”. Why wrong: the space is unchanged (possibly infinite); storage and computation move to mmm coefficients and the m×mm \times mm×m Gram matrix (2.048 GB at m=16,000m = 16{,}000m=16,000).

Second misconception: “any similarity function is a kernel”. Why wrong: max⁡{x,x′}\max\{x, x'\}max{x,x′} on {1,2}\{1, 2\}{1,2} has Gram [[1,2],[2,2]][[1,2],[2,2]][[1,2],[2,2]] and (1,−1)(1,-1)(1,−1) gives −1-1−1.

Third misconception: “the Gaussian kernel can separate anything, so it is a safe default”. Why wrong: as σ→0\sigma \to 0σ→0 it becomes the memorizer, with B2=NB^2 = NB2=N over NNN messages (concept check 1); on pair-marked junk the best Gaussian certificate the writer found needs 1.85×1081.85 \times 10^81.85×108 labels.

Fourth misconception: “the representer theorem means few training messages matter”. Why wrong: it gives a combination of all mmm; sparsity comes from the loss (support vectors, hard-SVM).
