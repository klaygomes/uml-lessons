# The Growth Function

Objective: after this, you can compute the growth function of thresholds and intervals, prove Sauer's lemma through its stronger claim (labellings at most shattered subsets) and its polynomial form \((em/d)^d\), prove uniform convergence from a small growth function (Markov, ghost sample, random signs, Hoeffding, union bound, tail lemma), close the fundamental theorem's last arrow (finite VC dimension gives uniform convergence) with an explicit sample size, and turn that theorem into a certified number of noisy labels for intervals, explaining why thresholds and unions of intervals cannot be certified to the same target.

Notation: \(\varepsilon\) for accuracy (series convention), \(\delta\), \(m\).

Terms:
- growth function: \(\tau_{\mathcal H}(m) = \max_{|C| = m} |\mathcal H_C|\).
- Sauer's lemma: VC dimension at most \(d\) implies \(\tau_{\mathcal H}(m) \le \sum_{i \le d}\binom{m}{i} \le (em/d)^d\).
- stronger claim: \(|\mathcal H_C| \le |\{B \subseteq C : \mathcal H \text{ shatters } B\}|\).
- partner class: \(\mathcal H'\), the hypotheses with a twin that differs only at \(c_1\).
- ghost sample: an imaginary second training sequence \(S' \sim \mathcal D^m\) used only in the proof.
- uniform convergence from a small growth function: with probability at least \(1 - \delta\), \(|L_{\mathcal D}(h) - L_S(h)| \le (4 + 2\sqrt{\log \tau_{\mathcal H}(2m)})/(\delta\sqrt{2m})\) for every \(h\).
- click-log risk: in the act-2 world, \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) = 0.05 + 0.9w\), where \(w\) is the probability of the scores on which \(h\) disagrees with [0.55, 0.8].

Misconception 1: “the proven bound is a label budget”. Why wrong: Markov's 1/δ and loose constants give 662,296 labels for thresholds at ε = δ = 0.1, against 30 from lesson 6a's two-strip argument. The fix section shows the same gap on the click log: 4,623,683 clicks are certified, while runs of 1,000 clicks already reach 0.053 at worst.
Misconception 2: “uniform convergence needs a finite class”. Why wrong: once \(S\) and \(S'\) are fixed only the at most \(\tau_{\mathcal H}(2m)\) labellings matter; thresholds on 400 scores leave 401 loss vectors.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Grouping by tail: a tail in \(Y_0 \setminus Y_1\) gives one labelling, a tail in \(Y_1\) two; \(Y_1 \subseteq Y_0\), so the total is \(|Y_0| + |Y_1|\). For B shattered by \(\mathcal H'\): realize the B-part with g ∈ \(\mathcal H'\); its partner g' agrees on C' ⊇ B and flips \(c_1\); one of them gives the wanted \(c_1\) label; both are in \(\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H\). With \(\mathcal H\) in place of \(\mathcal H'\): a hypothesis realizing the B-part may have no partner, so \(c_1\) may be stuck at one label and B ∪ {c_1} not shattered; e.g. \(\mathcal H_C = \{(0,0), (0,1)\}\): \(\mathcal H\) shatters {c_2} but not {c_1, c_2}. Also, \(Y_1\) is the restriction of \(\mathcal H'\), not of \(\mathcal H\), so the induction must be applied to \(\mathcal H'\).
2. \(\tau(5) = 31\) (every labelling except 10101); \(\tau(10) = 1 + \binom{11}{2} + \binom{11}{4} = 386\) (count by choosing run boundaries among 11 gaps); Sauer's \(\sum_{i \le 4}\binom{m}{i}\): 31 and 386, tight both times (s14; rechecked by `n03_misc.py`). \((em/d)^d\): at m = 10, \((10e/4)^4 \approx 2{,}133\) (2132.7); at m = 5, \((5e/4)^4 \approx 133\) (133.3) (the lesson's bound holds for 1 ≤ d ≤ m, so both are valid; the book's version asks m > d + 1). Accept a brute-force count with explanation.
3. No. The theorem's bound is an upper bound; a vacuous upper bound proves nothing about the true gap. What proves it: unions of intervals have infinite VC dimension, so by lesson 6a's theorem they are not PAC learnable (statement 4 fails), and since 1 implies 2 implies 3 implies 4, uniform convergence (statement 1) fails too. Computation: \(\tau_\cup(2m) = 2^{2m}\), bound \(= 4/(\delta\sqrt{2m}) + 2\sqrt{\log 2}/\delta \ge 16.6511\) at δ = 0.1 (`n01_cert.py`). Look for the learner separating “the bound is useless” from “the property fails”.
