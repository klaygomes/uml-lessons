The Growth Function

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can compute the growth function of thresholds and intervals, prove Sauer's lemma through its stronger claim (labellings at most shattered subsets) and its polynomial form (em/d)d(em/d)^d(em/d)d, prove uniform convergence from a small growth function (Markov, ghost sample, random signs, Hoeffding, union bound, tail lemma), close the fundamental theorem's last arrow (finite VC dimension gives uniform convergence) with an explicit sample size, and turn that theorem into a certified number of noisy labels for intervals, explaining why thresholds and unions of intervals cannot be certified to the same target.

Book: Chapter 6, The VC-Dimension, PDF pp. 73 to 78. §6.5 (proof of Theorem 6.7, 6→1: Definition 6.9, Lemma 6.10 with proof, Theorem 6.11 with proof, Remark 6.4, pp. 73 to 77), §6.6 summary, §6.7 bibliographic remarks (p. 78), §6.8 exercise 4 (p. 79). Appendix lemmas used: A.1, A.2 (log lemma, p. 419), A.4 (tail lemma, p. 419 to 420), A.5 (binomial sum, p. 420 to 421), Markov (B.1, p. 422), Hoeffding (Lemma B.6, p. 425). Recalled from lesson 6a: Definitions 6.2, 6.3, 6.5, Theorem 6.7 statement (pp. 68 to 72); from earlier chapters: Corollary 4.4 (p. 55), Definitions 4.1 and 4.3 (p. 54 to 55), Corollary 4.6 (p. 57).
