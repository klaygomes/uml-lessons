Overview

Objective: after this, you can define decision stumps, weighted risk and γ\gammaγ-weak learnability (and say why weak learning is statistically as hard as strong learning but may be computationally easier), prove that ERM over stumps is a 112\tfrac1{12}121​-weak learner for three-piece hypotheses on the line (and has an edge of 16\tfrac1661​ on every weighting of a finite sample), implement ERM over stumps in O(dm)O(dm)O(dm) per call after sorting, run AdaBoost by hand and in code, prove the bound LS(hs)≤e−2γ2TL_S(h_s) \le e^{-2\gamma^2 T}LS​(hs​)≤e−2γ2T step by step (and its sharper product form), and turn a guaranteed edge into a number of rounds that promises zero empirical risk on every training sequence.

Split: first half of the original lesson 10 (10-boosting/), up to and including "AdaBoost as code". The second half is lesson 10b (10b-boosting-generalization/).

Misconception: "AdaBoost's empirical risk goes down every round". Why wrong: on the ten messages it stays at 0.2 after round 2; on the desk's night 0 it is 0 after round 3 and 0.16 after round 4; only ZtZ_tZt​ is guaranteed to fall.

Second misconception: "a weak learner is a weak assumption". Why wrong: the edge must hold on every reweighting, which is what each round of the proof uses (and what the desk's schedule relies on).

Third misconception: "tonight's run was perfect after three rounds, so three rounds is a schedule". Why wrong: round 4 undoes it on 41% of simulated nights; simulated nights are not every night; tonight's ZT<1/300Z_T < 1/300ZT​<1/300 certifies tonight only after the run; only the ceiling e−T/18e^{-T/18}e−T/18 is known in advance for every night (103 rounds).
