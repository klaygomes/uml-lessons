Terms:
- RLM: \(A(S) \in \operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf w}(L_S(\mathbf w) + R(\mathbf w))\); Tikhonov: \(R = \lambda\|\mathbf w\|^2\).
- ridge regression: RLM with Tikhonov and the half square loss; \(\mathbf w = (2\lambda m I + A)^{-1}\mathbf b\), \(A = \sum \mathbf x_i\mathbf x_i^\top\), \(\mathbf b = \sum y_i\mathbf x_i\).
- replace-one sequence \(S^{(i)}\): \(S\) with \(z_i\) replaced by an independent \(z' \sim \mathcal D\).
- on-average-replace-one-stable with rate \(\varepsilon(m)\) (decreasing): \(\mathbb E[\ell(A(S^{(i)}), z_i) - \ell(A(S), z_i)] \le \varepsilon(m)\) for every \(\mathcal D\).
- \(\sigma\)-strongly convex: \(f(\alpha\mathbf w + (1-\alpha)\mathbf u) \le \alpha f(\mathbf w) + (1-\alpha)f(\mathbf u) - \frac\sigma2\alpha(1-\alpha)\|\mathbf w - \mathbf u\|^2\).
- oracle inequality: \(\mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(A(S))] \le L_{\mathcal D}(\mathbf w^*) + \lambda\|\mathbf w^*\|^2 + 2\rho^2/(\lambda m)\) for every \(\mathbf w^*\).

Misconception: "regularization is good because small weights are good". Why wrong: the penalty adds curvature \(2\lambda\) in every direction; it matters in the flat valley (0.0019 to 0.2019) and barely in steep directions (0.814 to 1.014); what it buys is stability, which equals a bound on overfitting.
Second: "a stable learner is a good learner". Why wrong: the constant learner \(\mathbf 0\) has rate 0 and production error 0.5.
Third: "more \(\lambda\) is always safer". Why wrong: the fit rises with \(\lambda\) for every \(S\) (proved); the certificate is U-shaped (one-word table: minimum 0.446 near \(\lambda = 0.1\)).
Fourth: "the bound's best \(\lambda\) is the true best". Why wrong: in the one-word world the bound prefers 0.1 while the true risk prefers the smallest \(\lambda\).
Fifth: "these are high-probability guarantees". Why wrong: they bound expectations; Exercise 1 converts.

Derivation:
- Ridge: gradient \(2\lambda\mathbf w + \frac1m\sum(\langle\mathbf w, \mathbf x_i\rangle - y_i)\mathbf x_i\); invertibility via \(\mathbf v^\top(2\lambda mI + A)\mathbf v \ge 2\lambda m\|\mathbf v\|^2\) (the book: "eigenvalues bounded below by \(2\lambda m\)"); exact expansion shows uniqueness (ours).
- Theorem 13.2: book's two-line proof; ours spells out conditioning on \(S\) and the exchange of positions \(i\) and \(m+1\) of an i.i.d. sequence.
- Lemma 13.5: part 1 by the exact identity (book: "follow directly from the definition"); part 3 proved without derivatives (left side ≥ 0 by minimality for every \(\alpha\), then \(\alpha \to 0\)); the book takes a derivative of \(g(\alpha)\) at 0. Existence and uniqueness of the RLM minimizer are ours.
- Equations 13.7 to 13.10 as in the book. Corollary 13.6 as in the book.
- Smooth case: the book's chain gives \(\frac{48\beta}{\lambda m}\mathbb E[L_S]\) (Corollary 13.7) using \(8\beta^2/(\lambda m)^2\) and \((a+b)^2 \le 3(a^2+b^2)\). The prose keeps the exact coefficient \(4\beta^2/(\lambda m)^2\), bounds it by \(2\beta/(\lambda m)\), and uses \((p+q)^2 \le 2(p^2+q^2)\), giving 24; this implies the book's 48. Downstream: smooth oracle with 24 (book's Corollary 13.10 has 48 and the example \(\lambda = 48\beta/m\); prose uses \(\lambda = 24\beta/m\)).
- Monotonicity of fit in \(\lambda\) (claim "more penalty, worse fit"): ours; the book states "the empirical risk increases with \(\lambda\)".
- Corollary 13.9 proof (minimize \(\lambda B^2 + c/\lambda\)): ours, standard.
- Corollary 13.11: the book states it without proof. With the book's constant 48 the stated \(m \ge 150\beta B^2/\varepsilon^2\) does not follow along these routes (excess \(\varepsilon/3 + 0.96\varepsilon\)); with 24 it does: excess \(\le \lambda B^2 + 24\beta C/(\lambda m) \le \varepsilon/3 + 0.48\varepsilon < 0.82\varepsilon\), using \(C = 1\) and the \(\ell(\mathbf 0, z) \le C\) remark. Theorem 13.1 follows with \(\beta = \|\mathbf x\|^2 \le 1\) for the half square loss (the book's 13.3 has the \(\frac12\)).

2. The \(m+1\) examples are i.i.d., so their joint law is a product of identical factors and is invariant under exchanging positions \(i\) and \(m+1\); hence \((S^{(i)}, z_i) \sim (S, z')\). Independence of \(z'\) from \(S\) gives \(\mathbb E[\ell(A(S), z')] = \mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(A(S))]\). If \(z' \sim \mathcal D'\): the first step gives \(\mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D'}(A(S))]\) instead, and the exchange step fails because \(S^{(i)}\) is no longer \(\mathcal D^m\)-distributed; the right side then measures the sensitivity of \(A\) to inserting one \(\mathcal D'\) example, not the overfitting gap.

- Exercise 3 (stability + AERM ⇒ learning).
  1. Write \(L_{\mathcal D}(A(S)) - \min_h L_{\mathcal D}(h)\) as a sum of three differences through \(L_S(A(S))\) and \(\min_h L_S(h)\).
