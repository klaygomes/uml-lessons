Misconceptions (all four in "What people get wrong"):

  • "The corollary's mmm is necessary." Why wrong: it is sufficient; it must hold for the worst D\mathcal DD. In act 1's uniform world, 0.88% of sequences are misleading at m=30m = 30m=30, against 215 asked for at (0.05, 0.01).
  • "Cost grows with ∣H∣|\mathcal H|∣H∣." Why wrong: with log⁡∣H∣\log |\mathcal H|log∣H∣; 30 to 465 hypotheses moves 161 to 215.
  • "The guarantee is about my hypothesis." Why wrong: the probability is over the draw of SSS.
  • "ε\varepsilonε and δ\deltaδ cost the same." Why wrong: mmm scales with 1/ε1/\varepsilon1/ε but only with log⁡(1/δ)\log(1/\delta)log(1/δ): 215 to 430 vs 215 to 261.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):

  1. Realizability fails: no one-feature hypothesis equals "F and U". Under uniform D\mathcal DD the best one-feature hypotheses (hFh_FhF​, hUh_UhU​) have true risk 0.25 and the rest 0.5, so ERM returns a hypothesis with true risk at least 0.25 whatever mmm is (other D\mathcal DD change the number, never to 0). The corollary promises nothing; its hypothesis is false. Step 1 breaks: it needs LS(hS)=0L_S(h_S) = 0LS​(hS​)=0, which came from realizability. Diagnostic: if ERM's minimum empirical risk is above 0, no hypothesis in the class is consistent, so realizability (or noise-free labels) has failed. With 161 labels this almost surely happens: each example has probability 1/4 of contradicting hFh_FhF​ (F on, U off) and 1/4 of contradicting hUh_UhU​.
