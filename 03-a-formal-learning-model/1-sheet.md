Terms:
- accuracy parameter \(\varepsilon\): how much true risk the guarantee forgives ("approximately correct").
- confidence parameter \(\delta\): the allowed probability that the guarantee fails over the draw of \(S\) ("probably").
- PAC learnable (Definition 3.1): there exist \(m_{\mathcal H}: (0,1)^2 \to \mathbb N\) and an algorithm such that for all \(\varepsilon, \delta\), all \(\mathcal D\) over \(\mathcal X\), all \(f\) with realizability, on \(m \ge m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon, \delta)\) i.i.d. examples it returns \(h\) with \(L_{(\mathcal D, f)}(h) \le \varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1 - \delta\).
- sample complexity: the minimal such \(m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon, \delta)\).
- joint distribution: \(\mathcal D\) over \(\mathcal X \times \mathcal Y\) = marginal \(\mathcal D_x\) over messages, then conditional probability of the label given the message; \(\eta(x) = \mathbb P[y = 1 \mid x]\).
- true risk (agnostic): \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) = \mathbb P_{(x,y) \sim \mathcal D}[h(x) \ne y]\) (3.1); in general \(\mathbb E_{z \sim \mathcal D}[\ell(h, z)]\) (3.3).
- Bayes optimal predictor: \(f_{\mathcal D}(x) = 1\) iff \(\mathbb P[y = 1 \mid x] \ge 1/2\); Bayes risk \(L_{\mathcal D}(f_{\mathcal D})\), the floor under every hypothesis.
- agnostic PAC learnable (Definition 3.3): as above but for every \(\mathcal D\) over \(\mathcal X \times \mathcal Y\), no realizability, and the target is \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le \min_{h' \in \mathcal H} L_{\mathcal D}(h') + \varepsilon\).
- loss function: \(\ell: \mathcal H \times Z \to \mathbb R_+\); 0-1 loss \(\mathbb 1[h(x) \ne y]\); square loss \((h(x) - y)^2\); Definition 3.4 is agnostic PAC with a general loss, returning \(h \in \mathcal H\).
- proper vs representation-independent learning (Remark 3.2): must return \(h \in \mathcal H\), or may return \(h' \in \mathcal H' \supseteq \mathcal H\).

Misconception: "agnostic PAC with \(\varepsilon = 0.01\) means the filter is wrong at most 1% of the time". Why wrong: the guarantee is relative to the best in the class; for the one-feature class in the noisy mail room that allows 0.185.
Second misconception: "with enough data, true risk goes to zero". Why wrong: the Bayes risk (0.125 here) is a floor under every hypothesis when labels disagree.
Third misconception: "minimizing empirical risk is the goal". Why wrong: ERM over all 65,536 hypotheses gets \(L_S = 1/12\) on the worked example and true risk 0.35, double \(h_F\)'s 0.175.

Derivation:
- Definition 3.1, why \(\varepsilon, \delta > 0\) (book's two reasons), sample complexity as the minimal function, Corollary 3.2 from Corollary 2.3 with the ceiling.
- Joint distribution, (3.1), \(L_S = L_{\mathcal D(\text{uniform over } S)}\).
- Bayes optimality (the book's Exercise 7, proved in the prose for finite \(\mathcal X\)): law of total probability, conditional risk is \(\eta\) or \(1 - \eta\), so at least \(\min\{\eta, 1 - \eta\}\), attained by \(f_{\mathcal D}\), multiply by \(\mathcal D_x(x) \ge 0\) and sum.
- Definition 3.3; reduction to PAC under realizability (left to Exercise 6).
- (3.2), loss functions, (3.3), (3.4), \(\mathbb E[\alpha] = \mathbb P[\alpha = 1]\) for 0/1-valued \(\alpha\), Definition 3.4, Remarks 3.1, 3.2.
- No appendix is needed in Chapter 3. Hoeffding's inequality (Appendix B.4) enters in lesson 4.

Algorithms: a PAC learner = algorithm \(A\) plus \(m_{\mathcal H}\); the user picks \(\varepsilon, \delta\), collects \(m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon, \delta)\) labels, runs \(A\), which never sees \(\varepsilon, \delta\). Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Every message has \(\min\{\eta, 1 - \eta\} = 1/2\), so the Bayes risk is 0.5. Every hypothesis has true risk exactly 0.5 (each message's conditional risk is 1/2 whatever it says), so every hypothesis is Bayes optimal; the rule \(f_{\mathcal D}\) itself says junk everywhere by the \(\ge\) convention. The best in the one-feature class is 0.5, and the agnostic promise \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le 0.5 + \varepsilon\) holds for every hypothesis and every training sequence: kept trivially, worthless as a filter. Nothing can be learned because the features carry no information about the labels; the theory is not failing. Look for: "every \(g\) ties", and the distinction between a promise kept and a useful one.
2. Mistakes: (a) the guarantee is relative, \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le \min_{h' \in \mathcal H} L_{\mathcal D}(h') + 0.02\), not "at most 2%"; (b) "guaranteed" should be "with probability at least 0.99 over the draw of the training sequence"; (c) "enough examples" means \(m \ge m_{\mathcal H}(0.02, 0.01)\), a number fixed before seeing \(\mathcal D\); (d) true risk is a probability over fresh messages from \(\mathcal D\), measured against labels people give, not a count of messages in the inbox. Correct statement in the noisy mail room: with probability at least 0.99 over \(S\) of \(m \ge m_{\mathcal H}(0.02, 0.01)\) examples, \(L_{\mathcal D}(h_S) \le 0.175 + 0.02 = 0.195\); and no hypothesis can go below the Bayes risk 0.125.
3. \(\mathbb E[(c - y)^2 \mid x] = \eta (1 - c)^2 + (1 - \eta) c^2 = (c - \eta)^2 + \eta(1 - \eta)\), minimized at \(c = \eta = 0.7\), minimum \(0.21\) (values: \(c = 0\): 0.7; \(c = 0.5\): 0.25; \(c = 0.7\): 0.21; \(c = 1\): 0.3). Under the 0-1 loss with answers in \(\{0,1\}\): say junk (1), risk 0.3, as \(f_{\mathcal D}\). The square loss rewards reporting the probability itself; the 0-1 loss only rewards the right side of 1/2. With answers restricted to \(\{0,1\}\) the square loss gives 0.3 for junk, matching the 0-1 loss. Accept a derivative argument instead of completing the square.
