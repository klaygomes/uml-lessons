Generative Models

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can contrast the generative and discriminative approaches; define the likelihood, log-likelihood and maximum likelihood estimator and derive it for Bernoulli and Gaussian models (with the maximum checked, edge cases included); show that maximum likelihood is ERM with the log loss and split its true risk into entropy plus relative entropy (and prove the relative entropy is nonnegative); compute the excess risk of the Gaussian-mean MLE (1/(2m)1/(2m)1/(2m) in expectation) and prove that the Bernoulli MLE has infinite true risk with probability at least 1/2 when m≤log⁡2/(2θ⋆)m \le \log 2/(2\theta^\star)m≤log2/(2θ⋆); state and prove the pseudo-count guarantee for the add-α\alphaα estimator; derive Laplace’s rule from a uniform prior and compute MAP estimates; derive naive Bayes and LDA (LDA is a halfspace) and give their costs; define EM, prove it is alternate maximization of GGG and that it never decreases the likelihood; write soft k-means and its cost; certify a naive Bayes filter with a union bound.

Misconception: “a probability of 0 is just a small probability”. Why wrong: in a product it vetoes all other evidence and in a log it is −∞-\infty−∞; MLE produces it with probability ≥1/2\ge 1/2≥1/2 for m≤log⁡2/(2θ⋆)m \le \log 2/(2\theta^\star)m≤log2/(2θ⋆).

Second: “smoothing is a hack”. Why wrong: add-α\alphaα is regularized MLE, add-one is the uniform-prior predictive, and it has a theorem.

Third: “more pseudo-counts are always safer”. Why wrong: bias α/(m+2α)\alpha/(m+2\alpha)α/(m+2α); α=20\alpha = 20α=20 fails the accuracy certificate at 600 labels.

Fourth: “naive Bayes assumes the words are independent”. Why wrong: independent given the label.

Fifth: “EM finds the MLE”. Why wrong: only monotone; local maxima; identical starting centres never separate.

Sixth: “MLE is not ERM”. Why wrong: it is ERM with the log loss.
