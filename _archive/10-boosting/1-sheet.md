# Boosting

Objective: after this, you can define decision stumps, weighted risk and \(\gamma\)-weak learnability (and say why weak learning is statistically as hard as strong learning but may be computationally easier), prove that ERM over stumps is a \(\tfrac1{12}\)-weak learner for three-piece hypotheses on the line, implement ERM over stumps in \(O(dm)\) per call after sorting, run AdaBoost by hand and in code, prove the bound \(L_S(h_s) \le e^{-2\gamma^2 T}\) step by step (and its sharper product form), define \(L(B, T)\), show that it expresses staircases and prove the upper bound \(T(d+1)(3\log(T(d+1)) + 2)\) on its VC dimension, turn \(T\) into a certified bound on true risk for a finite domain, and describe the Viola and Jones base hypotheses.

Key words: message, word count (a feature; not "frequency", "term count"), junk \(+1\) / keep \(-1\) (not spam / ham), example, training sequence, hypothesis (not "classifier", "model"; "rule" only in the story's opening and in "rules of thumb"), decision stump (short form "stump"; not "split", "threshold rule"), base class \(B\) (not "weak class"), weak learner, strong learning (the lesson's name for PAC learning), edge \(\gamma\) (not "advantage" in formal text), distribution over the examples \(D\), weight \(D_i\) (belongs to an example), vote \(w_t\) (belongs to a hypothesis; never "weight" for \(w_t\)), weighted risk \(L_D(h)\) (not "weighted error"), round (not "iteration"), empirical risk (not "training error"), true risk, \(Z_t\) (no nickname), certified risk (\(L_S + \operatorname{gap}(T)\)), gap\((T)\), fresh error (measured on 50,000 fresh messages in the lab; never called true risk), approximation error, estimation error, VC dimension, shatter. Accuracy is \(\varepsilon\), confidence \(\delta\), sample size \(m\); AdaBoost's weighted risks are \(\varepsilon_t\) (same symbol family, as in the book).

Terms:
- decision stump: \(h_{j,\theta,b}(x) = b \cdot \operatorname{sign}(x_j - \theta)\), with \(\operatorname{sign}(u) = +1\) iff \(u > 0\).
- weighted risk: \(L_D(h) = \sum_i D_i \mathbb 1[h(x_i) \ne y_i]\) for a distribution \(D\) over the \(m\) examples; equals \(L_S\) when \(D\) is uniform.
- \(\gamma\)-weak learner (Definition 10.1): like PAC but with accuracy fixed at \(\tfrac12 - \gamma\) and \(m_{\mathcal H}: (0,1) \to \mathbb N\) depending on \(\delta\) only.
- AdaBoost: rounds \(t = 1..T\); \(h_t = \mathrm{WL}(D^{(t)}, S)\), \(\varepsilon_t = L_{D^{(t)}}(h_t)\), \(w_t = \tfrac12\log(1/\varepsilon_t - 1)\), \(D^{(t+1)} \propto D^{(t)} e^{-w_t y h_t}\), output \(\operatorname{sign}(\sum w_t h_t)\).
- \(Z_t = \frac1m\sum_i e^{-y_i f_t(x_i)}\), \(f_t = \sum_{p \le t} w_p h_p\).
- \(L(B, T)\) (Equation 10.4): halfspaces over \(\psi(x) = (h_1(x), \dots, h_T(x))\).
- \(G_r\): piecewise constant hypotheses on the line with at most \(r\) pieces.

Misconception: "AdaBoost's empirical risk goes down every round". Why wrong: on the ten messages it stays at 0.2 after round 2; only \(Z_t\) is guaranteed to fall.
Second misconception: "more rounds means a better filter". Why wrong: the class and its VC dimension grow with \(T\); certified risk 0.175 at 40 rounds, 0.227 at 100.
Third misconception: "a weak learner is a weak assumption". Why wrong: the edge must hold on every reweighting, which is what each round of the proof uses.

Derivation:
- Weak learnability and its statistical equivalence to strong learning via the lower bound of Theorem 6.8 with \(\varepsilon = \tfrac12 - \gamma\).
- Example 10.1 with the \(\eta\) repair (see Assumptions), \(\varepsilon = \eta = 1/24\).
- ERM for stumps: \(\Theta_j\), update, both signs via \(1 - F\).
- Theorem 10.2: Step 1 \(\mathbb 1 \le e^{-yf}\); Step 2 telescoping, \(Z_0 = 1\); Step 3 induction for \(D^{(t+1)}\); Step 4 ratio \(= 2\sqrt{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}\); Step 5 monotonicity of \(a(1-a)\), \(1 - a \le e^{-a}\). Plus: the vote minimizes the ratio; product form \(Z_T = \prod 2\sqrt{\varepsilon_t(1-\varepsilon_t)} \le e^{-2\sum\gamma_t^2}\).
- Remark 10.2: union bound, \(1 - \delta T\).
- \(G_T \subseteq L(B,T)\) on finite sets, VC \(\ge T\); Lemma 10.3 with full proof including Lemma A.1 (proved in the prose) and the small-\(m\) case.
- Certificate: Corollary 4.6-style uniform convergence on the finite domain plus a union bound over \(T = 1..100\).

Algorithms: ERM-Stumps (mirror of the book's pseudocode, see Assumptions): \(O(dm\log m)\) per call with sorting, \(O(dm)\) per call with presorted indices (\(O(dm)\) space). AdaBoost: \(O(T(m + c_{WL}))\); with stumps \(O(dm\log m + Tdm)\) time, \(O(dm + m + T)\) space, \(O(T)\) per prediction. Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(\varepsilon_t = 0.7\): \(w_t = \tfrac12\log(1/0.7 - 1) = \tfrac12\log(3/7) = -0.424\). A negative vote means the final vote uses \(-h_t\), whose weighted risk is 0.3, with vote 0.424. The ratio is \(2\sqrt{0.7 \cdot 0.3} = 0.917 < 1\), so \(Z\) still shrinks: Step 4 never used \(\varepsilon \le \tfrac12\); only Step 5 did, and \(2\sqrt{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}\) is symmetric about \(\tfrac12\). \(\varepsilon_t = 0.5\): \(w_t = 0\), ratio 1, weights unchanged (multiplied by \(e^0\)), so no progress, and a deterministic weak learner returns the same hypothesis forever. \(\varepsilon_t = 0\): \(w_t\) is infinite; stop and return \(h_t\), which has empirical risk 0. Look for: the symmetry, and that a 30%-right hypothesis is as useful as a 70%-right one.
2. Step 4, the last two lines (plugging \(e^{\pm w}\)). With \(w = 1\), \(\varepsilon = 0.25\): \(e^{-1}(0.75) + e^{1}(0.25) = 0.9555\); AdaBoost's vote: \(2\sqrt{0.25 \cdot 0.75} = 0.8660\). The ratio \(e^{-w}(1-\varepsilon) + e^{w}\varepsilon\) is a strictly convex function of \(w\) minimized exactly at AdaBoost's \(w\) (derivative zero at \(e^{2w} = (1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\)), so any other vote gives a ratio at least as large in that round. Accept: "greedy per round", not "globally optimal over all rounds".
3. \(e^{-2\gamma^2 T} < 1/m\) iff \(T > \log(m)/(2\gamma^2) = \log(20{,}000)/0.005 = 1980.7\), so \(T = 1{,}981\) (then \(L_S\) is a multiple of \(1/m\) below \(1/m\), hence 0). The VC bound at \(T = 1{,}981\), \(d = 3\): \(1981 \cdot 4 \cdot (3\log 7924 + 2) \approx 229{,}265 > 20{,}000\). Any VC-based guarantee on fresh mail needs \(m\) well above the VC dimension, so at that \(T\) the bound promises nothing: the vote may simply be memorizing. Look for: realizing that driving the empirical risk to 0 is not the goal (lesson 1's memorizer, lesson 5's estimation error).

- Exercise 3 (\(h_t\) has risk exactly 1/2 under \(D^{(t+1)}\)), standard.
  1. Before normalizing, what does the update do to the weight of an example \(h_t\) gets right, and to one it gets wrong? Write the two unnormalized totals in terms of \(\varepsilon_t\) and \(w_t\).
  2. The totals are \((1-\varepsilon_t)e^{-w_t}\) and \(\varepsilon_t e^{w_t}\). Substitute \(e^{w_t} = \sqrt{(1-\varepsilon_t)/\varepsilon_t}\).
  3. Both become \(\sqrt{\varepsilon_t(1-\varepsilon_t)}\). Normalizing divides by their sum, so each side gets exactly half. (Check: the ten messages after round 1: 4 and 5 hold 1/4 + 1/4.)
- Exercise 2 (Equation 10.5 equals the piecewise constant hypothesis), standard.
  1. Fix \(x \in (\theta_{s-1}, \theta_s]\). Which stumps \(\operatorname{sign}(x - \theta_{t-1})\) say \(+1\), and which \(-1\)? (Those with \(t \le s\), and \(t > s\); \(t = 1\) is always \(+1\).)
  2. So the sum is \(2P_s - W\), with \(P_s = \sum_{t \le s} w_t\) and \(W = P_T\). Compute \(P_s\) for \(s\) even and odd.
  3. \(P_s = \tfrac{(-1)^T}{2} + \mathbb 1[s \text{ even}]\). Plug in \(W\) for \(T\) even and odd: the sum is \(\tfrac32\) for even \(s\) and \(-\tfrac12\) for odd \(s\), never 0, so the sign is \((-1)^s\).

- Orientation and sign convention are ours: stumps are \(b \cdot \operatorname{sign}(x_j - \theta)\) with \(\operatorname{sign}(0) = -1\), so \(b = +1\) says junk above the threshold. The book's ERM section writes \(\operatorname{sign}(\theta - x_i) \cdot b\) and fixes \(b = 1\), predicting \(+1\) when \(x \le \theta\), with update \(F - y_i D_i\); our pseudocode is its mirror image (update \(F + y_i D_i\), start at the total keep weight) and handles both signs at once via \(1 - F\), which the book leaves implicit. The book does not fix \(\operatorname{sign}(0)\); Equation 10.5 needs \(\operatorname{sign}(0) = -1\) at the cut points.
- Example 10.1 repair: the book says a stump agrees with any pair of the three regions; for the two outer rays no stump on \(\mathbb R\) does exactly (stumps are never constant on \(\mathbb R\)), so the prose uses "within \(\eta\)" by continuity of probability and sets \(\varepsilon = \eta = 1/24\) (the book uses \(\varepsilon = 1/12\) and a sample size \(\Omega(\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon^2)\)). VCdim 2 for stumps on the line is the book's claim; the prose leaves the check to the reader.
- Equation 10.5 correction: with the book's \(w_1 = 0.5\) the vote equals the alternating staircase only for even \(T\); the prose and Problem 2 use \(w_1 = (-1)^T/2\). Also \(\theta_0 = -\infty\) is not a real threshold; the prose notes that on finite sets (and on counts) a constant stump exists.
