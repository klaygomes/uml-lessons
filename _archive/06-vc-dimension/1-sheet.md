# The VC Dimension

Objective: after this, you can compute the restriction of a class to a finite set, decide whether a class shatters it, prove the VC dimension of thresholds (1), intervals (2), axis-aligned rectangles (4), finite classes (at most log2 |H|) and unions of intervals (infinite); state the fundamental theorem of statistical learning in both versions; prove Sauer's lemma and uniform convergence from a small growth function; and certify a label budget for thresholds and intervals on the score line.

Terms:
- spam score: a number in [0, 1] the scoring service assigns a message; the domain is now \(\mathcal X = [0, 1]\).
- threshold: \(h_t(x) = \mathbb 1[x > t]\), \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{thr}} = \{h_t : t \in \mathbb R\}\).
- restriction: \(\mathcal H_C = \{(h(c_1), \dots, h(c_m)) : h \in \mathcal H\}\), the labellings \(\mathcal H\) produces on \(C\).
- shatter: \(\mathcal H\) shatters \(C\) when \(|\mathcal H_C| = 2^{|C|}\).
- VC dimension: the size of the largest set \(\mathcal H\) shatters; infinite if there is no largest.
- growth function: \(\tau_{\mathcal H}(m) = \max_{|C| = m} |\mathcal H_C|\).
- Sauer's lemma: VC dimension at most \(d\) implies \(\tau_{\mathcal H}(m) \le \sum_{i \le d}\binom{m}{i} \le (em/d)^d\).
- fundamental theorem of statistical learning: six equivalent properties (UC, ERM agnostic PAC, agnostic PAC, PAC, ERM PAC, finite VC), and the quantitative bounds in \(d\).

Misconception 1: “an infinite class cannot be learned”. Why wrong: thresholds are infinite and PAC learnable with \(\lceil \log(2/\delta)/\varepsilon \rceil\) examples; VC dimension, not size, decides.
Misconception 2: “VCdim ≥ d needs every d-set shattered”. Why wrong: one shattered set suffices; the universal quantifier belongs to the upper bound (every d+1 set fails).
Misconception 3: “VC dimension = number of parameters”. Why wrong: \(\lceil 0.5\sin(\theta x)\rceil\) has one parameter and infinite VC dimension.
Misconception 4: “the proven bound is a label budget”. Why wrong: Markov's 1/δ and loose constants give 662,296 labels for thresholds at ε = δ = 0.1, against 30 from the direct argument.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. {always keep, always junk}: on one point both labellings occur, so one point is shattered; on two points only (0,0) and (1,1), so no pair is shattered: VC dimension 1, equal to \(\log_2 |\mathcal H| = 1\) (the finite-class bound is tight here). Thresholds with t ∈ [0, 1] on [0, 1]: \(h_t(0) = \mathbb 1[0 > t] = 0\) for every \(t \ge 0\), so {0} is not shattered; every \(\{c\}\) with \(0 < c \le 1\) is (t = 0 gives junk, t = c gives keep). No pair is shattered (the (1, 0) argument is unchanged). VC dimension 1: one shattered set suffices. Look for the quantifier asymmetry stated explicitly.
2. Grouping by tail: a tail in \(Y_0 \setminus Y_1\) gives one labelling, a tail in \(Y_1\) two; \(Y_1 \subseteq Y_0\), so the total is \(|Y_0| + |Y_1|\). For B shattered by \(\mathcal H'\): realize the B-part with g ∈ \(\mathcal H'\); its partner g' agrees on C' ⊇ B and flips \(c_1\); one of them gives the wanted \(c_1\) label; both are in \(\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H\). With \(\mathcal H\) in place of \(\mathcal H'\): a hypothesis realizing the B-part may have no partner, so \(c_1\) may be stuck at one label and B ∪ {c_1} not shattered; e.g. \(\mathcal H_C = \{(0,0), (0,1)\}\): \(\mathcal H\) shatters {c_2} but not {c_1, c_2}. Also, \(Y_1\) is the restriction of \(\mathcal H'\), not of \(\mathcal H\), so the induction must be applied to \(\mathcal H'\).
3. VC = 4: any 4 points, every labelling has at most 2 runs of junk, so realizable by at most two intervals (one per run); 5 points \(c_1 < \dots < c_5\), labelling (1,0,1,0,1) has 3 runs, impossible (a single interval covering two junk points covers the keep point between them, so each interval covers at most one run). \(\tau(5) = 31\) (every labelling except 10101); \(\tau(10) = 1 + \binom{11}{2} + \binom{11}{4} = 386\) (count by choosing run boundaries among 11 gaps); Sauer's \(\sum_{i \le 4}\binom{m}{i}\): 31 and 386, tight both times (s14). \((em/d)^d\): at m = 10, \((10e/4)^4 \approx 2{,}133\); at m = 5, \((5e/4)^4 \approx 133\) (the lesson's bound holds for 1 ≤ d ≤ m, so both are valid; the book's version asks m > d + 1). Assumptions:
- Not checked yet by the book-check agents (gate 2). Every claim was written from the PDF extract of pp. 67 to 82, 33 to 66 and 419 to 425 in this session.
- Orientation: the book's thresholds are \(\mathbb 1[x < a]\); the lesson uses \(\mathbb 1[x > t]\) (junk above the line). Lemma 6.1's proof is mirrored; strips are half-open intervals to match.
- No-atom assumption: the lesson assumes no single score has positive probability, so strips of probability exactly ε exist. The book's proof of Lemma 6.1 uses such points without stating the assumption.
- Intervals are closed, \([a, b]\) with \(a \le b\); the book uses open \((a, b)\) with \(a < b\). VC dimension is 2 either way; closed intervals make the tightest-interval learner a member of the class. The s05 growth snippet uses open intervals with half-integer ends, which give the same labellings.
- Domain points of thresholds on {1..k}: the lesson counts k + 1 distinct thresholds; the book says |H| = k for its \(\mathbb 1[x < a]\) version. Either way VC = 1.
- Not in the book: the unions-of-intervals class \(\mathcal H_\cup\) and its infinite VC dimension; the two-intervals class of concept check 3; the decision-list cliffhanger (decision lists are not in Chapters 6 or 7; the claims about them are standard and stated loosely as story).
- Not in the book: the interval certificate (tightest-interval learner, \(m \ge 2\log(2/\delta)/\varepsilon\)). It adapts Lemma 6.1's strip argument and Exercise 2.3's tightest-rectangle idea.
- Correction to the book: in the proof of Theorem 6.11 the book applies Hoeffding to terms in [−1, 1] with \(2\exp(-2m\rho^2)\); the correct range factor gives \(2\exp(-m\rho^2/2)\). The lesson carries the correct constant, so its Theorem reads \((4 + 2\sqrt{\log\tau_{\mathcal H}(2m)})/(\delta\sqrt{2m})\) instead of the book's \((4 + \sqrt{\log\tau_{\mathcal H}(2m)})/(\delta\sqrt{2m})\), and the final \(m^{\mathrm{UC}}\) bound has \(\alpha = 8d/(\delta\varepsilon)^2\) (book: 2d/(δε)² before Lemma A.2). The conclusion (finite VC implies UC) is unchanged. Worth confirming against the book's errata at book-check time.
- The book's last step uses Lemma A.2 with \(b = 2d\log(2e/d)/(\delta\varepsilon)^2\), which is negative for d ≥ 6, where A.2 requires b > 0; the lesson splits the cases with max{·, 0}.
- Hoeffding: lesson 4 is assumed to state Lemma B.6 for i.i.d. variables; the lesson notes the proof uses only independence (the book's own proof of B.6 multiplies per-variable bounds) and applies it to independent, non-identical terms, as the book does in Theorem 6.11's proof.
- Tail lemma and binomial bound are proved by shorter standard arguments than Appendix A's; results are at least as strong as A.4 and A.5.
- The sine example uses the book's §6.3.5 form \(\lceil 0.5\sin(\theta x)\rceil\); Exercise 8 writes \(\lceil\sin(\theta x)\rceil\); not chosen as a problem, so its proof is not given.
- Vapnik and Chervonenkis 1970 is from the chapter introduction (p. 67); the bibliographic remarks cite their 1971 paper. The lesson says 1970.
