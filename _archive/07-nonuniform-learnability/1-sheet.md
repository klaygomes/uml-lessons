# Nonuniform Learnability

Objective: after this, you can define nonuniform learnability and say how it differs from agnostic PAC learnability; prove the SRM bound (countable union bound over classes with weights), prove that SRM is a nonuniform learner and that nonuniform learnability is equivalent to being a countable union of agnostic PAC learnable classes; prove the Kraft inequality and the MDL bound and explain Occam's razor with its language catch; run SRM and MDL with a stopping rule and state their cost; define consistency and explain why Memorize meets it and why that does not contradict No-Free-Lunch.

Terms:
- decision list: \(h = ((j_1, b_1), \dots, (j_k, b_k); b_0)\); returns \(b_i\) for the first \(i\) with \(x_{j_i} = 1\), else \(b_0\).
- size, \(\mathcal H_n\): \(\mathcal H_n\) = lists with at most \(n\) tests on distinct features among \(1..n\); size \(n(h) = \min\{n : h \in \mathcal H_n\} = \max(1, k, \max_i j_i)\); \(|\mathcal H_n| = 2\sum_{k=0}^n 2^k n!/(n-k)!\).
- \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-competitive: with probability higher than \(1 - \delta\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) \le L_{\mathcal D}(h') + \varepsilon\) (p. 83).
- nonuniformly learnable (Def. 7.1, p. 84): \(\exists A, m^{\mathrm{NUL}}_{\mathcal H}: (0,1)^2 \times \mathcal H \to \mathbb N\), \(\forall \varepsilon, \delta, h\), \(m \ge m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(\varepsilon, \delta, h) \Rightarrow \forall \mathcal D\), w.p. \(\ge 1 - \delta\), \(L_{\mathcal D}(A(S)) \le L_{\mathcal D}(h) + \varepsilon\).
- weight function: \(w: \mathbb N \to [0,1]\), \(\sum_n w(n) \le 1\); \(6/(\pi^2 n^2)\) or \(2^{-n}\) (p. 86).
- gap \(\varepsilon_n(m, \delta) = \min\{\varepsilon \in (0,1) : m^{\mathrm{UC}}_{\mathcal H_n}(\varepsilon, \delta) \le m\}\) (7.1, p. 86); for finite \(\mathcal H_n\) the lesson uses \(\sqrt{\log(2|\mathcal H_n|/\delta)/(2m)}\).
- SRM: \(\operatorname{argmin}_h L_S(h) + \varepsilon_{n(h)}(m, w(n(h))\delta)\) (p. 87).
- description language \(d: \mathcal H \to \{0,1\}^*\), \(|h| = |d(h)|\); prefix-free: no \(d(h)\) is a prefix of \(d(h')\), \(h \ne h'\) (p. 89).
- Kraft inequality (Lemma 7.6, p. 90); MDL bound (Thm 7.7, p. 90); MDL rule (p. 90).
- consistency (Def. 7.8, p. 92): sample size \(m^{\mathrm{CON}}(\varepsilon, \delta, h, \mathcal D)\); universally consistent when \(\mathcal P\) is all distributions. (The book's definition writes \(m^{\mathrm{NUL}}\) in the condition, a typo; the lesson writes \(m^{\mathrm{CON}}\).)

Misconception: "nonuniformly learnable means PAC learnable, only slower". Why wrong: the sample size depends on the hypothesis you compete with, which you do not know in advance; decision lists of any length remain not PAC learnable.
Second misconception: "SRM ships the list with the smallest true risk". Why wrong: it ships the smallest certified bound; at 30 labels it ships "everything is junk" (true risk 0.375) though cap-3 ERM lists have 0.125 to 0.175.
Third misconception: "set the weights after seeing which list fits". Why wrong: weights must be independent of \(S\) or the union bound's events are not the budgeted ones (the Occam language puzzle, p. 91).
Fourth: "shorter is intrinsically truer". Why wrong: length is relative to a language chosen in advance.

Derivation (proofs in the prose):
- Nonuniform from agnostic PAC: \(m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(\varepsilon,\delta,h) = m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon,\delta)\) (p. 84).
- SRM bound (Thm 7.4, pp. 86 to 87): bad events \(B_n\), \(\mathcal D^m(B_n) \le w(n)\delta\) by uniform convergence; finite union bound then continuity from below for the countable union; min over classes containing \(h\); convention \(\varepsilon_n(m, 0) = \infty\).
- SRM nonuniform learner (Thm 7.5, pp. 87 to 88), four steps on a single good event \(G\): bound applied to the data-dependent \(A(S)\) (simultaneity), SRM optimality, two-sidedness for \(h\), fact 2 on the sample size. The book draws step 3 from a separate uniform-convergence event; the lesson uses the same simultaneous event, which avoids a second \(\delta\).
- Characterization (Thm 7.2, p. 84): "if" via fundamental theorem plus SRM; "only if" with \(\mathcal H_n = \{h : m^{\mathrm{NUL}}(1/10, 1/10, h) \le n\}\) and the expectation form of No-Free-Lunch (5.1): \(\mathbb E \ge 1/4\) vs \(\le 1/10 + 1/10 = 1/5\), so \(\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal H_n) < 2n\). The book uses \((1/8, 1/7)\), where the probability form of NFL only matches at the boundary; the lesson's constants follow Exercise 5's (0.1, 0.1) and make the contradiction strict.
- Decision lists: infinite VC (shatter \(e_1..e_k\) with \(((1,y_1),\dots,(k,y_k);0) \in \mathcal H_k\)); each \(\mathcal H_n\) finite; so nonuniformly but not PAC learnable (parallel to Example 7.1, polynomial classifiers, p. 85).
- Price of nonuniformity: finite-class version of the book's \(4C \cdot 2\log(2n)/\varepsilon^2\) (pp. 88 to 89).
- Remark 7.2 (p. 88) stated, proof deferred (Exercise 5).
- Kraft (Lemma 7.6): the book's coin argument, made finite (toss \(L\) coins, events \(E_\sigma\) disjoint by prefix-freeness), then partial sums for infinite \(S\); the stop-when-in-\(S\) process given as the intuition.
- MDL bound (Thm 7.7): Kraft gives weights \(2^{-|h|}\); singleton gap from Hoeffding; \(|h|\log 2 < |h|\).
- Occam (§7.3.1, p. 91): razor, language puzzle (3 bits vs 100,000), resolution by commitment before data.
- Consistency (§7.4, pp. 92 to 93), Memorize (Example 7.4), expected missing mass by linearity and independence (noise-free intuition only; proof is Exercise 6), NFL revisited (§7.5.1, pp. 95 to 96), discussion (§7.5, pp. 93 to 95), consistency trick (p. 95).

Algorithms: SRM and MDL pseudocode as on pp. 87 and 90. Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(\sum 1/n\) diverges, so step 2 of the proof (union of bad events has probability at most \(\delta \sum w(n) \le \delta\)) fails: the failure probabilities may add to more than 1 and the theorem promises nothing (cannot be normalized either). Accept "the harmonic series diverges" with the step named. \(w(7) = 0\) with the others at \(6/(\pi^2 n^2)\): legal (sum is \(1 - 6/(49\pi^2) \le 1\)). \(\varepsilon_7(m, 0) = \infty\), so in the rule written with \(n(h)\) a list of size exactly 7 has an infinite bound and never ships. Full credit adds the subtlety: the classes are nested, so a size-7 list is also in \(\mathcal H_8\), and the general bound \(\min_{n : h \in \mathcal H_n}\) still certifies it through the size-8 gap; it pays size 8's price. (SRM-nested's stopping rule assumed nondecreasing gaps; \(g_7 = \infty\) breaks that, so the loop must skip size 7 rather than stop there.)
2. The sentence "if they did, both strings would be beginnings of the same toss sequence, so the shorter would be a prefix of the longer" uses prefix-freeness to make the \(E_\sigma\) disjoint. \(\{0, 01, 1\}\): \(1/2 + 1/4 + 1/2 = 1.25\); \(E_0\) and \(E_{01}\) overlap (tosses beginning 01). Without the end flag: \((();1)\) is `1`, and any list starting with a test, e.g. \(((1,0); 0)\) = `1 1 0 0`, begins with `1`: prefix clash.
3. \(\log(2/0.05) = 3.689\). 15 bits: \(0.12 + \sqrt{18.689/600} = 0.2965\); 29 bits: \(0.10 + \sqrt{32.689/600} = 0.3334\); MDL prefers the 15-bit list by 0.0369. Crossover: \(0.02 = (\sqrt{32.689/2} - \sqrt{18.689/2})/\sqrt m\), \(\sqrt m = 49.30\), \(m = 2{,}430.3\), so the smallest integer is \(m = 2{,}431\) (computed by search).

End-of-chapter problems (hints only, never the solution; book pp. 97 to 99):
- Exercise 3 (both bullets; second starred). Union of finite \(\mathcal H_n\): find \(w(h)\) depending only on \(n(h)\) and \(|\mathcal H_{n(h)}|\) with \(\sum_h w(h) \le 1\); then countable \(\mathcal H_n\).
  1. Think of a two-level budget: first share out among classes, then within a class.
  2. A hypothesis can lie in many classes. Charge it once: which class owns it?
  3. Write \(\sum_h w(h) = \sum_n \sum_{h : n(h) = n} w(h)\) and bound the inner sum using \(|\{h : n(h) = n\}| \le |\mathcal H_n|\).
  4. (Starred part) An infinite \(\mathcal H_n\) cannot be split evenly. Enumerate it and reuse a summable sequence inside the class.
- Exercise 4. Oracle inequality for MDL against \(h^*_B\), the best hypothesis of description length at most \(B\).
  1. Start from the MDL bound applied to the returned \(h_S\): it holds on a good event, simultaneously for all hypotheses.
  2. Compare the MDL objective at \(h_S\) with the objective at \(h^*_B\), and use \(|h^*_B| \le B\).
  3. You still need \(L_S(h^*_B)\) close to \(L_{\mathcal D}(h^*_B)\). Either use the same simultaneous event (two-sided) or a Hoeffding bound for the fixed \(h^*_B\) with part of the budget; account for the union.
  4. Aim for a bound of the form \(c\sqrt{(B + \log(c'/\delta))/m}\).
- Exercise 6. Memorize is consistent for every class of binary functions over a countable domain.
  1. Part 1: the tail of a convergent series of nonnegative terms goes to 0.
  2. Part 2: messages of tiny probability can only carry tiny total mass once you go far enough down the enumeration; choose the threshold from part 1.
  3. Part 3: a fixed message of probability above \(\eta\) is missed by \(m\) draws with probability at most \((1 - \eta)^m \le e^{-\eta m}\); there are at most \(n\) such messages; union bound.
  4. Parts 4 and 5: the unseen mass is the heavy unseen messages plus the light ones; bound each by \(\varepsilon/2\). Then relate Memorize's risk to the unseen mass (and, with noisy labels, to majority votes on the seen messages).
