Nonuniform Learnability

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define nonuniform learnability and say how it differs from agnostic PAC learnability; prove the SRM bound (countable union bound over classes with weights), prove that SRM is a nonuniform learner and that nonuniform learnability is equivalent to being a countable union of agnostic PAC learnable classes; prove the Kraft inequality and the MDL bound and explain Occam's razor with its language catch; run SRM and MDL with a stopping rule and state their cost; define consistency and explain why Memorize meets it and why that does not contradict No-Free-Lunch.

Book: Chapter 7, Sections 7.1 to 7.6 (PDF pp. 83 to 96). Uses Definition 3.3 (p. 46), Lemma 2.2 union bound (p. 39), Definition 4.3 (p. 55), Lemma 4.5 Hoeffding (p. 56), Corollary 4.6 (p. 57), Theorem 5.1 and Equation (5.1) (pp. 61 to 62), Theorems 6.7 and 6.8 (pp. 72 to 73).

Misconception: "nonuniformly learnable means PAC learnable, only slower". Why wrong: the sample size depends on the hypothesis you compete with, which you do not know in advance; decision lists of any length remain not PAC learnable.

Second misconception: "SRM ships the list with the smallest true risk". Why wrong: it ships the smallest certified bound; at 30 labels it ships "everything is junk" (true risk 0.375) though cap-3 ERM lists have 0.125 to 0.175.

Third misconception: "set the weights after seeing which list fits". Why wrong: weights must be independent of SSS or the union bound's events are not the budgeted ones (the Occam language puzzle, p. 91).

Fourth: "shorter is intrinsically truer". Why wrong: length is relative to a language chosen in advance.
