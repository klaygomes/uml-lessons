Objective: after this, you can state the online protocol and explain why no guarantee from lessons 2 to 20 survives an adaptive adversary; define mistake bounds and online learnability; prove the mistake bounds of Consistent (\(|\mathcal H| - 1\)) and Halving (\(\log_2|\mathcal H|\)); define shattered trees and the Littlestone dimension, prove the tree lower bound, the root split and SOA’s bound, and so the exact characterization \(M_{\text{SOA}}(\mathcal H) = \mathrm{Ldim}(\mathcal H)\); prove \(\mathrm{VCdim} \le \mathrm{Ldim}\) with an infinite gap; define regret and prove Cover’s impossibility for deterministic learners; prove Weighted-Majority’s regret \(\sqrt{2\log(d)T}\) step by step and turn it into regret bounds for finite classes and for classes of finite Littlestone dimension; prove Online Gradient Descent’s regret \(B\rho\sqrt T\); derive the online Perceptron from OGD on round-dependent hinge surrogates and prove its mistake bound; and give each learner’s time and space per round.

Terms:
- round: one pass of receive \(x_t\), predict \(p_t\), see \(y_t\), update.
- online learner: an algorithm that predicts each round from the past rounds only.
- adversary: whoever chooses the messages (and, without realizability, the labels), possibly after seeing all past predictions.
- realizable sequence: some \(h^\star \in \mathcal H\) has \(y_t = h^\star(x_t)\) for all \(t\).
- mistake bound: \(M_A(\mathcal H) = \sup_S |\{t : p_t \neq y_t\}| \le B < \infty\) over realizable \(S\); online learnable: some \(A\) has one.
- version space \(V_t\): the hypotheses consistent with rounds \(1, \dots, t-1\).
- shattered tree: complete binary tree of depth \(d\) of messages such that every label path is followed by some \(h \in \mathcal H\).
- Littlestone dimension: the largest depth of a shattered tree (\(\infty\) if unbounded).
- regret: \(\sup\) over sequences and \(h \in \mathcal H\) of the learner’s mistakes (expected, if randomized) minus \(h\)’s.
- expert / cost: an adviser; \(v_{t,i} \in [0,1]\) is what following expert \(i\) costs on round \(t\).
- online convex optimization: convex \(\mathcal H \subseteq \mathbb R^d\), \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) convex, the learner commits \(\mathbf w^{(t)}\) before \(z_t\) is revealed.

Misconception: “online learning is batch learning one example at a time.” · Why wrong: what changes is who chooses the examples; thresholds have VC dimension 1 (easy in batch) and Littlestone dimension \(\infty\) (impossible online).
Second: “any consistent hypothesis will do.” · Consistent can be forced into \(|\mathcal H| - 1\) mistakes, Halving at most \(\log_2|\mathcal H|\).
Third: “randomizing only adds noise.” · Without it, regret \(T/2\) is forced even for two constant hypotheses (Cover).
Fourth: “WM’s bound needs random costs.” · It holds for every cost sequence; the expectation is only over the learner’s coin.
Fifth: “the Perceptron learns any separable stream with few mistakes.” · Only with margin; Ldim of halfspaces is infinite for \(d \ge 2\).

Derivation (every result proved in the prose except the regret lower bound):
- Consistent \(\le |\mathcal H| - 1\); Halving \(\le \log_2|\mathcal H|\) via \(1 \le |V_{T+1}| \le |\mathcal H|2^{-M}\).
- Tree lower bound (Lemma 21.6); finite classes \(\mathrm{Ldim} \le \log_2|\mathcal H|\); singletons 1; thresholds \(\infty\).
- Root split \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V) = \max_x (1 + \min\{\mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(0)}), \mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(1)})\})\) and monotonicity under subsets: stated and proved by us; the book’s proof of Lemma 21.7 uses the construction in one sentence. SOA’s bound (Lemma 21.7) then by contradiction; Corollary 21.8.
- VCdim \(\le\) Ldim (Theorem 21.9).
- Cover’s impossibility; randomized loss \(|p_t - y_t|\).
- Theorem 21.11 in six steps, with \(e^{-a} \le 1 - a + a^2/2\) proved by the derivative of \(g\) and \(\log(1-b) \le -b\) from \(1 - b \le e^{-b}\); the general-\(\eta\) form \(\log d/\eta + \eta T/2\) (the line before the book plugs in \(\eta\)); remark that \(T \le 2\log d\) makes the bound trivial (ours).
- Corollary 21.12 via the sign identity; Lemma 21.13 by induction on version spaces; expert count via lesson 6’s polynomial bound; Theorem 21.10 upper bound.
- Theorem 21.15 (OGD), all three forms.
- Theorem 21.16 (Perceptron), with \(\eta\) chosen after the run and the quadratic claim solved in full.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Steps 2 and 3 use \(\eta < 1\): Step 2 needs \(a = \eta v_{t,i} \in [0, 1]\) for \(e^{-a} \le 1 - a + a^2/2\) as proved (the inequality is in fact true for all \(a \ge 0\), so accept that observation), and Step 3 needs \(b < 1\) so that \(\log(1 - b)\) is defined and \(\le -b\); \(b \le \eta\) and \(\eta = \sqrt{2\log(d)/T} < 1\) iff \(T > 2\log d\). Without it, \(b\) could reach 1 and the log is undefined. The final inequality still holds when \(T \le 2\log d\): then \(\sqrt{2\log(d)T} \ge T\), and the regret is at most \(T\) because each round’s cost difference is at most 1 (\(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf v\rangle \le 1\), costs \(\ge 0\)). Look for the trivial-bound argument, not “the theorem fails”.
2. The prediction rule is used in exactly one place: “SOA predicted \(p_t\) because its side had the larger (or tied) dimension, so \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(p_t)}) \ge \mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(y_t)}) = d\)”; this is what puts both parts at dimension \(d\) and lets the root split give \(d + 1\). For the smaller-side variant, a mistake leaves the larger part, whose dimension can equal \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V_t)\), so the potential need not drop. Example: the A∪B class (Ldim 2): at \(x_0\) the variant predicts with A (keep, dimension 1); the adversary says junk and keeps B, which has dimension 2 and forces 2 more: 3 mistakes \(> 2\). Accept any class with an unbalanced split; on \(\mathcal H_1\) every useful split is balanced, so it does not work there.
3. \(\eta = \sqrt{2\ln 100/5000} \approx 0.0429\); regret \(\le \sqrt{2\ln(100)\cdot 5000} \approx 214.6\); per round \(\approx 0.043\) (4.3%). Perceptron: \(C = 9\), \(b = R\|\mathbf w^\star\| = 4\): \(9 + 4\cdot 3 + 16 = 37\) mistakes at most. Accept \(\le 37\); the answer must use the shortfall form, not \(R^2\|\mathbf w^\star\|^2 = 16\), since \(\mathbf w^\star\) does not separate with margin.

End-of-chapter problems (§21.7, PDF pp. 305 to 306). In the prose: Problem 1 = Exercise 2, Problem 2 = Exercise 4 (doubling trick), Problem 3 = Exercise 5 (online-to-batch). Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Exercise 2 (Halving tight), standard.
  1. You want every mistake to remove exactly half the version space, so you want every vote to be a tie. Which class splits evenly on every new message?
  2. Take \(\mathcal X = \{1, \dots, k\}\) and \(\mathcal H\) = all \(2^k\) functions from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\{0,1\}\). After rounds on \(1, \dots, t-1\), the version space is all functions with those labels fixed: \(2^{k-t+1}\) of them.
  3. On message \(t\), exactly half say 1: a tie, so Halving predicts 1. Label it 0. Each round is a mistake; after \(k\) rounds one function remains, and it is in \(\mathcal H\), so the sequence is realizable. \(k = \log_2|\mathcal H|\) mistakes. Works for every power of two.
- Exercise 4 (doubling trick), standard.
  1. Regret against a fixed \(h\) over all rounds is the sum, over periods, of (learner’s loss in the period minus \(h\)’s); each such term is at most the period’s regret against the best hypothesis of that period. Why is \(\sum_m \min_h(\cdot) \le \min_h \sum_m(\cdot)\) the right direction?
  2. If the last period started is \(m = M\), then \(2^M \le T\). The total is at most \(\alpha\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sqrt{2^m}\), a geometric series with ratio \(\sqrt 2\).
  3. \(\sum_{m=0}^M (\sqrt 2)^m = \frac{(\sqrt2)^{M+1} - 1}{\sqrt2 - 1} \le \frac{\sqrt 2\,\sqrt{2^M}}{\sqrt 2 - 1} \le \frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2 - 1}\sqrt T\). A partial last period has regret at most its bound too (the algorithm told \(2^M\) rounds and stopped early: check the original bound still applies, or note that regret over a prefix of a period is at most the period’s bound for the algorithms of this lesson since their proofs hold for any \(T' \le T\)).
- Exercise 5 (online-to-batch), standard; hint in the book points at the SGD proof (Theorem 14.8).
  1. \(\mathbb E_r[L_{\mathcal D}(h_r)] = \frac1T\sum_t L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)\) for fixed messages. Take expectation over the messages too.
  2. \(h_t\) depends only on \(x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}\) (and their labels by \(h^\star\)); \(x_t\) is independent of them. So \(\mathbb E[\mathbb 1[h_t(x_t) \ne h^\star(x_t)] \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}] = L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)\).
  3. Sum over \(t\): \(\sum_t \mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)] = \mathbb E[\text{number of mistakes}] \le M_A(\mathcal H)\), since the sequence is realizable. Divide by \(T\).
