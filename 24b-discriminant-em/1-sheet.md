# Discriminant Analysis and EM

Terms:
- Gaussian cloud: density \(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\Sigma|^{1/2}}\exp(-\frac12(x-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu))\); covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) (variances on the diagonal, covariances off it).
- LDA: equal priors, Gaussian classes, shared \(\Sigma\); \(w = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1 - \mu_0)\), \(b = \frac12(\mu_0^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu_0 - \mu_1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu_1) = -\frac12\langle w, \mu_0 + \mu_1\rangle\).
- latent variable \(Y\); mixture \(\mathbb P[X = x] = \sum_y c_y \mathcal N(x; \mu_y, \Sigma_y)\); log-likelihood with the sum inside the log.
- EM: E step \(Q_{i,y} = \mathbb P_{\theta^{(t)}}[Y = y \mid X = x_i]\), M step \(\theta^{(t+1)} \in \operatorname{argmax}_\theta F(Q^{(t+1)},\theta)\); \(F(Q,\theta) = \sum_{i,y} Q_{i,y}\log\mathbb P_\theta[x_i, y]\); \(G = F - \sum Q\log Q\).
- soft k-means: \(\Sigma_y = I\); \(\mu_y = \sum_i Q_{i,y}x_i/\sum_i Q_{i,y}\), \(c_y = \sum_i Q_{i,y}/m\).
- hard assignment \(Q^{\mathrm{hard}}\); rounding tax \(\tau(\theta) = \sum_i \log(1/\max_y\mathbb P_\theta[Y=y\mid x_i]) \ge 0\); \(L = G(Q^{\mathrm{hard}},\theta) + \tau\); gain \(\gamma \ge 0\).

Misconception: “EM finds the maximum likelihood estimate”. Why wrong: only monotone; local maxima; identical starting centres never separate (fixed point: \(Q_{i,y} = c_y\), both centres go to the overall average).
Second: “a hard assignment is just a cheaper soft one”. Why wrong: \(G\) no longer touches \(L\); a round promises only \(L - \tau + \gamma\); on the test archive the loop fell from \(-422.2\) to \(-433.7\) while the soft loop climbed to \(-364.0\).
Third: “LDA weighs each score by how far apart its averages are”. Why wrong: \(w = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1 - \mu_0) = (2, 0)\), not \((2, 1)\); the link score carries no further evidence given the capitals score.

Derivation:
- LDA: log-likelihood ratio, expansion with symmetry of \(\Sigma^{-1}\), Equation 24.8; \(b = -\frac12\langle w, \mu_1 + \mu_0\rangle\).
- EM: mixture density, log-likelihood with the sum inside the log, \(F\), E and M steps; Lemma (alternate maximization of \(G\)) with Jensen replaced by \(\log t \le t - 1\) per message; Theorem (monotone) as a chain of two equalities and two inequalities.
- Hard assignment (ours): for a one-hot row the entropy term of \(G\) is 0, so message \(i\)’s part of \(G\) is \(\log\mathbb P_\theta[x_i, \hat y_i] = \log\mathbb P_\theta[x_i] + \log\max_y\mathbb P_\theta[Y = y\mid x_i]\), giving \(L = G(Q^{\mathrm{hard}},\theta) + \tau\). With \(\gamma\) the gain of the M step in \(G(Q^{\mathrm{hard}}, \cdot)\) (nonnegative because the old parameters are a candidate) and \(G \le L\) for every \(Q\): \(L(\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge G(Q^{\mathrm{hard}},\theta^{(t+1)}) = G(Q^{\mathrm{hard}},\theta^{(t)}) + \gamma = L(\theta^{(t)}) - \tau + \gamma\). Soft: \(\tau = 0\).
- Identical centres (ours): equal bells cancel in Bayes’ rule, \(Q_{i,y} = c_y\); M step gives \(\mu_y = \sum_i c_y x_i/\sum_i c_y\) = overall average, \(c_y\) unchanged; a fixed point.
- Soft k-means: E step with \(Z_i\), M step objective 24.13, \(\mu_y\) by gradient (concave quadratic), \(c_y\) stated (Problem 1).
- Safe M steps (sheet only, not in the prose; the lab’s certificate part 1 uses it): for fixed \(Q\) the M-step objective is a sum of a concave function of the shares (\(\sum_y\nu_y\log c_y\)) and a concave quadratic in the centres, so it is concave; the half-way point between the old parameters and the maximizer has \(F\) at least the average of the two values, hence at least the old \(F\). Centres only: maximizing over a subset that contains the old values never lowers \(F\). Jitter can lower \(F\). All three “safe” variants satisfy the lemma’s requirement (concept check 1: only “at least as good as \(\theta^{(t)}\)” is used).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Approximate M step: the link \(G(Q^{(t+1)}, \theta^{(t+1)}) \ge G(Q^{(t+1)}, \theta^{(t)})\) used that \(\theta^{(t+1)}\) maximizes \(G(Q^{(t+1)}, \cdot)\); it only needs “at least as good as \(\theta^{(t)}\)”, and since \(G\) and \(F\) differ by a term independent of θ, \(F(Q^{(t+1)},\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge F(Q^{(t+1)},\theta^{(t)})\) is exactly that. Theorem holds (this is “generalized EM”, a name outside the book). Approximate E step: the first equality \(L(\theta^{(t+1)}) = G(Q^{(t+2)}, \theta^{(t+1)})\) becomes \(\ge\) (still fine, \(L \ge G\) always), but the last equality \(G(Q^{(t+1)}, \theta^{(t)}) = L(\theta^{(t)})\) becomes \(\le\), and the chain breaks: the likelihood can decrease. Look for: the E step’s exactness is what anchors \(G\) to \(L\). A good answer also connects this to the lesson’s bug (the hard assignment is the approximate E step).
2. (a) \(\Sigma = I\) gives \(w = \mu_1 - \mu_0 = (2, 1)\) and \(b = -\frac12\langle w, \mu_0 + \mu_1\rangle = -\frac12(2 \cdot 4 + 1 \cdot 3) = -5.5\) (equivalently \(\frac12(\|\mu_0\|^2 - \|\mu_1\|^2) = \frac12(2 - 13)\)). (b) At (2.3, 0.5): her score is \(2 \cdot 2.3 + 0.5 - 5.5 = -0.4\), keep; LDA’s is \(2 \cdot 2.3 - 4 = 0.6\), junk. The Bayes optimal predictor under the true clouds is the LDA rule (junk). (c) The independent rule counts the link score as evidence, but given the capitals score it carries none (LDA’s weight 0): the correlation makes it double-count, so the boundary tilts; expect more mistakes. Exact error rates (equal priors): LDA 0.1587 (\(\Phi(-1)\), the best possible), independent 0.1724, about 1.4 points worse. Look for: using \(\Sigma^{-1}\), not \(\mu_1 - \mu_0\), and for the “double counting” explanation.
3. \(L(\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge L(\theta^{(t)}) - 2 + 0.5 = L(\theta^{(t)}) - 1.5\): the likelihood may fall by up to 1.5 nats (or rise or stay); the bound promises nothing better. With tax 0.3: \(\ge L - 0.3 + 0.5 = L + 0.2\): it rises by at least 0.2, so a hard round is safe whenever \(\gamma \ge \tau\). The soft loop pays no tax because the posterior makes \(G(Q, \theta) = L(\theta)\) exactly (lemma’s equality: \(t = 1\) in \(\log t \le t - 1\) for every \(y\)), so \(\tau = 0\) and the chain gives \(L(\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge L(\theta^{(t)}) + \gamma\).

End-of-chapter problem (§24.8, PDF p. 356). In the prose: Problem 1 = Exercise 3 (the original lesson called it Problem 2). Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Problem 1 hint ladder:
  1. (i) In the M step objective, the terms with \(c\) are \(\sum_i\sum_y Q_{i,y}\log c_y = \sum_y (\sum_i Q_{i,y})\log c_y\).
  2. (ii) \(c^\star_y \ge 0\) and \(\sum_y c^\star_y = 1\) by construction.
  3. (iii) \(\sum_y \nu_y \log c_y = (\sum_{y'}\nu_{y'})\sum_y c^\star_y\log c_y\), and \(\sum_y c^\star_y\log c_y = -D_{\mathrm{RE}}(c^\star\|c) + \sum_y c^\star_y\log c^\star_y\); the last sum does not depend on \(c\).
  4. (iv) Relative entropy is \(\ge 0\) with equality iff \(c = c^\star\) (proved in lesson 24a). If some \(\nu_y = 0\), \(c^\star_y = 0\) violates \(c_y > 0\): the supremum is approached but not attained; note this edge case (the book’s statement ignores it).
