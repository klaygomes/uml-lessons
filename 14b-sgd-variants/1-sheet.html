Misconception: “projecting the iterates breaks the guarantee.” · Why wrong: projection onto a closed convex set never moves you further from w⋆∈H\mathbf w^\star \in \mathcal Hw⋆∈H, so the per-step identity becomes an inequality in the right direction.

Second misconception: “any shrinking step size gets the log⁡(T)/T\log(T)/Tlog(T)/T rate.” · Why wrong: it needs strong convexity and ηt=1/(λt)\eta_t = 1/(\lambda t)ηt​=1/(λt) with a λ\lambdaλ no larger than the true one; claiming more curvature (λ′>λ\lambda' > \lambdaλ′>λ) leaves terms (λ′−λ)at/2≥0(\lambda' - \lambda)a_t/2 \ge 0(λ′−λ)at​/2≥0 after the telescoping, and B/(ρt)B/(\rho\sqrt t)B/(ρt​) on a convex function gives only order 1/T1/\sqrt T1/T​.

Third: “the fast rate means SGD learns from fewer fresh messages.” · It is an optimization rate for a strongly convex objective; the true-risk count for convex-Lipschitz-bounded problems stays B2ρ2/ε2B^2\rho^2/\varepsilon^2B2ρ2/ε2.

Fourth: “the measured gap is far below the certificate, so fewer steps would do.” · The measurement is one world; the certificate holds for every training sequence.
