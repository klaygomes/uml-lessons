PCA and Random Projections

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define linear compression and recovery and explain why exact recovery of every vector is impossible when n<dn < dn<d; state and prove that PCA (top eigenvectors of the scatter matrix, W=U⊤W = U^\topW=U⊤) minimizes the total squared reconstruction error, with leftover error equal to the discarded eigenvalues; compute PCA by the Gram-matrix route when d≫md \gg md≫m and give its costs; prove chi-squared concentration by Chernoff's method (for ε≤1/2\varepsilon \le 1/2ε≤1/2), the one-vector random-projection lemma and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma, and compute the JL dimension; and choose between PCA and a random projection by their assumptions, certifying a squeeze rather than measuring one draw.

Misconception: "PCA is the optimal dimensionality reduction". Why wrong: optimal only for linear recovery, squared error, on the archive it was fitted to; sparse data is recovered far better by compressed sensing (lesson 23b; standard-basis example: PCA loses (d−n)/d(d-n)/d(d−n)/d).

Second: "random is worse than fitted". Why wrong: only the random projection has a guarantee for unseen messages, with nnn free of ddd.

Third: "measured on this draw = certified". Why wrong: n=500n = 500n=500 measures 0.243 but the JL bound there is 22.

Fourth: "JL dimension grows with the vocabulary". Why wrong: no ddd in the formula.
