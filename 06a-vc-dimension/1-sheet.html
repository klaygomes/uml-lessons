The VC Dimension

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can compute the restriction of a class to a finite set, decide whether a class shatters it, prove the VC dimension of thresholds (1), intervals (2), axis-aligned rectangles (4), finite classes (at most log2 |H|) and unions of intervals (infinite); prove that infinite VC dimension rules out PAC learning; state the fundamental theorem of statistical learning in both versions and prove its easy arrows; run ERM for thresholds and intervals in O(m log m); and certify a label budget for thresholds and intervals on the score line. The hard arrow (finite VC dimension gives uniform convergence) is lesson 6b.

Book: Chapter 6, The VC-Dimension, PDF pp. 67 to 73. §6.1 (Example 6.1, Lemma 6.1, p. 67 to 68), §6.2 (Definitions 6.2, 6.3, Example 6.2, Corollary 6.4, Definition 6.5, Theorem 6.6, pp. 68 to 70), §6.3 (thresholds, intervals, rectangles with Figure 6.1, finite classes, parameters and the sine class, pp. 70 to 72), §6.4 (Theorems 6.7 and 6.8, Remark 6.3, pp. 72 to 73), the opening of §6.5 (easy arrows and the two-part plan, p. 73), §6.8 exercises 5 and 9 (pp. 79 to 80). Recalled from earlier chapters: Corollary 2.3 (p. 40 to 41), Definitions 3.1 and 3.3 (pp. 43, 46), Definitions 4.1, 4.3, Lemma 4.2, Corollaries 4.4 and 4.6 (pp. 54 to 57), discretization trick (p. 57 to 58), Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 (pp. 61 to 64).

The class of thresholds is infinite, so every finite-class guarantee from lessons 2 to 4 is vacuous; the 64-bit discretization gives 948 labels but depends on the hardware, and lesson 5 showed some infinite classes are unlearnable. Fix: what matters is how many ways a class can label points: VC dimension. Thresholds have VC dimension 1 and a direct two-strip argument certifies 74 labels at ε = δ = 0.05. In act 2 the truth becomes an interval (newsletters take the top scores and are kept); the learner picks intervals (VC 2, certified at 148 labels by the tightest-interval argument, budget 160) over thresholds (miss the truth) and unions of intervals (VC infinite, no certificate).

Misconception 1: “an infinite class cannot be learned”. Why wrong: thresholds are infinite and PAC learnable with ⌈log⁡(2/δ)/ε⌉\lceil \log(2/\delta)/\varepsilon \rceil⌈log(2/δ)/ε⌉ examples; VC dimension, not size, decides.

Misconception 2: “VCdim ≥ d needs every d-set shattered”. Why wrong: one shattered set suffices; the universal quantifier belongs to the upper bound (every d+1 set fails).

Misconception 3: “VC dimension = number of parameters”. Why wrong: ⌈0.5sin⁡(θx)⌉\lceil 0.5\sin(\theta x)\rceil⌈0.5sin(θx)⌉ has one parameter and infinite VC dimension.
