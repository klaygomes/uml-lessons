Overview

Objective: after this, you can explain why a single contradictory label breaks every mistake-bound learner of lesson 21a; define regret and prove Cover’s impossibility for deterministic learners; explain the learner’s coin and P[y^t≠yt]=∣pt−yt∣\mathbb P[\hat y_t \neq y_t] = |p_t - y_t|P[y^​t​=yt​]=∣pt​−yt​∣; prove Weighted-Majority’s regret 2log⁡(d)T\sqrt{2\log(d)T}2log(d)T​ step by step, with the general-η\etaη form log⁡d/η+ηT/2\log d/\eta + \eta T/2logd/η+ηT/2, and turn it into regret bounds for finite classes and for classes of finite Littlestone dimension; prove Online Gradient Descent’s regret BρTB\rho\sqrt TBρT​; derive the online Perceptron from OGD on round-dependent hinge surrogates and prove its mistake bound; and give each learner’s time and space per round.

Misconception: “randomizing only adds noise.” · Without it, regret T/2T/2T/2 is forced even for two constant hypotheses (Cover).

Second: “WM’s bound needs random costs.” · It holds for every cost sequence; the expectation is only over the learner’s coin.

Third: “the Perceptron learns any separable stream with few mistakes.” · Only with margin; Ldim of halfspaces is infinite for d≥2d \ge 2d≥2. Fourth (new in the split, shown by act 1): “a bigger step size always learns faster, so it is always better.” · Against the punisher a bold η\etaη lurches; the bound log⁡d/η+ηT/2\log d/\eta + \eta T/2logd/η+ηT/2 has a learning-slowly term and an overreacting term, and the safe η\etaη balances them.
