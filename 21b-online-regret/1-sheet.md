Objective: after this, you can explain why a single contradictory label breaks every mistake-bound learner of lesson 21a; define regret and prove Cover’s impossibility for deterministic learners; explain the learner’s coin and \(\mathbb P[\hat y_t \neq y_t] = |p_t - y_t|\); prove Weighted-Majority’s regret \(\sqrt{2\log(d)T}\) step by step, with the general-\(\eta\) form \(\log d/\eta + \eta T/2\), and turn it into regret bounds for finite classes and for classes of finite Littlestone dimension; prove Online Gradient Descent’s regret \(B\rho\sqrt T\); derive the online Perceptron from OGD on round-dependent hinge surrogates and prove its mistake bound; and give each learner’s time and space per round.

Terms:
- regret: \(\sup\) over sequences and \(h \in \mathcal H\) of the learner’s mistakes (expected, if randomized) minus \(h\)’s.
- sublinear: \(\mathrm{Regret}_A(\mathcal H, T)/T \to 0\).
- expert / cost: an adviser; \(v_{t,i} \in [0,1]\) is what following expert \(i\) costs on round \(t\).
- total trust \(Z_t = \sum_i \tilde w_i^{(t)}\).
- online convex optimization: convex \(\mathcal H \subseteq \mathbb R^n\), \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) convex, the learner commits \(\mathbf w^{(t)}\) before \(z_t\) is revealed.
- shortfall \(C = \sum_{t \in \mathcal M}[1 - y_t\langle \mathbf w^\star, \mathbf x_t\rangle]_+\).
- Recapped from lesson 21a (one line each in “What you need from lesson 21a”): round, prediction, mistake, \(T\), \([T]\), adversary and its opening trick, realizability, \(M_A(\mathcal H)\), \(\sup\), version space, \(V^{(r)}\), Littlestone dimension and shattered tree, tree lower bound, SOA, \(\mathcal H_1\), rogue sender, thresholds.

Misconception: “randomizing only adds noise.” · Without it, regret \(T/2\) is forced even for two constant hypotheses (Cover).
Second: “WM’s bound needs random costs.” · It holds for every cost sequence; the expectation is only over the learner’s coin.
Third: “the Perceptron learns any separable stream with few mistakes.” · Only with margin; Ldim of halfspaces is infinite for \(d \ge 2\).
Fourth (new in the split, shown by act 1): “a bigger step size always learns faster, so it is always better.” · Against the punisher a bold \(\eta\) lurches; the bound \(\log d/\eta + \eta T/2\) has a learning-slowly term and an overreacting term, and the safe \(\eta\) balances them.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Steps 2 and 3 use \(\eta < 1\): Step 2 needs \(a = \eta v_{t,i} \in [0, 1]\) for \(e^{-a} \le 1 - a + a^2/2\) as proved (the inequality is in fact true for all \(a \ge 0\), so accept that observation), and Step 3 needs \(b < 1\) so that \(\log(1 - b)\) is defined and \(\le -b\); \(b \le \eta\) and \(\eta = \sqrt{2\log(d)/T} < 1\) iff \(T > 2\log d\). Without it, \(b\) could reach 1 and the log is undefined. The final inequality still holds when \(T \le 2\log d\): then \(\sqrt{2\log(d)T} \ge T\), and the regret is at most \(T\) because each round’s cost difference is at most 1 (\(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf v\rangle \le 1\), costs \(\ge 0\)). Look for the trivial-bound argument, not “the theorem fails”.
2. \(\eta = \sqrt{2\ln 100/5000} \approx 0.0429\); regret \(\le \sqrt{2\ln(100)\cdot 5000} \approx 214.6\); per round \(\approx 0.043\) (4.3%). Perceptron: \(C = 9\), \(b = R\|\mathbf w^\star\| = 4\): \(9 + 4\cdot 3 + 16 = 37\) mistakes at most. Accept \(\le 37\); the answer must use the shortfall form, not \(R^2\|\mathbf w^\star\|^2 = 16\), since \(\mathbf w^\star\) does not separate with margin. The total shortfall over the stream bounds the sum over update rounds, so using 9 is safe.

End-of-chapter problem (§21.7, PDF p. 305). In the prose: Problem 1 = Exercise 4 (doubling trick). Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Exercise 4 (doubling trick), standard.
  1. Regret against a fixed \(h\) over all rounds is the sum, over periods, of (learner’s loss in the period minus \(h\)’s); each such term is at most the period’s regret against the best hypothesis of that period. Why is \(\sum_m \min_h(\cdot) \le \min_h \sum_m(\cdot)\) the right direction?
  2. If the last period started is \(m = M\), then \(2^M \le T\). The total is at most \(\alpha\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sqrt{2^m}\), a geometric series with ratio \(\sqrt 2\).
  3. \(\sum_{m=0}^M (\sqrt 2)^m = \frac{(\sqrt2)^{M+1} - 1}{\sqrt2 - 1} \le \frac{\sqrt 2\,\sqrt{2^M}}{\sqrt 2 - 1} \le \frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2 - 1}\sqrt T\). A partial last period has regret at most its bound too (the algorithm told \(2^M\) rounds and stopped early: check the original bound still applies, or note that regret over a prefix of a period is at most the period’s bound for the algorithms of this lesson since their proofs hold for any \(T' \le T\)).
