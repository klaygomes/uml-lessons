Terms:
- distance from \(\mathbf x\) to the hyperplane of \((\mathbf w, b)\): \(\min\{\|\mathbf x - \mathbf v\| : \langle \mathbf w, \mathbf v \rangle + b = 0\}\), equal to \(|\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle + b|\) when \(\|\mathbf w\| = 1\).
- margin of a separating \((\mathbf w, b)\), \(\|\mathbf w\| = 1\), on \(S\): \(\min_i y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b)\).
- hard-SVM: the separating halfspace of largest margin; equivalently \(\operatorname{argmin} \|\mathbf w\|^2\) s.t. \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b) \ge 1\), output normalized.
- separable with a \((\gamma, \rho)\)-margin: some unit \((\mathbf w^\star, b^\star)\) has \(y(\langle \mathbf w^\star, \mathbf x \rangle + b^\star) \ge \gamma\) and \(\|\mathbf x\| \le \rho\) with probability 1.
- slack variable \(\xi_i \ge 0\): how much constraint \(i\) is violated.
- soft-SVM: \(\min \lambda\|\mathbf w\|^2 + \frac1m \sum \xi_i\) s.t. \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i\), \(\xi_i \ge 0\); equals \(\min \lambda \|\mathbf w\|^2 + L_S^{\text{hinge}}\).
- hinge loss: \(\max\{0, 1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle\}\); ramp loss: \(\min\{1, \text{hinge}\}\).
- support vectors: training messages with \(|\langle \mathbf w_0, \mathbf x_i \rangle| = 1\).
- dual problem: \(\max_{\boldsymbol\alpha \ge 0} \sum \alpha_i - \frac12 \sum\sum \alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j \langle \mathbf x_j, \mathbf x_i \rangle\).

Misconception: “every consistent halfspace is as good as any other”. Why wrong: they differ in margin, hence in robustness to perturbation and in the dimension-free guarantee; Perceptron 0.243 vs hard-SVM 1.342 on the lesson’s eight messages.
Second misconception: “scale the data to get a bigger margin”. Why wrong: margin and radius scale together; only \(\rho/\gamma\) (or \(\rho B\)) matters (Definition 15.3 and Remark 15.2).
Third misconception: “the margin bound always beats the VC bound”. Why wrong: it wins when \(\rho^2B^2 \ll d\); the large-margin preference is an inductive bias that can raise approximation error, and the hinge approximation term can exceed the 0-1 one (p. 209).
Fourth: “every message on the margin has a nonzero coefficient”. Why wrong: in S10 at \(\lambda = 0.1\), A has signed score exactly 1 and dual coefficient 0.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Scaling all lifted vectors by 10: lifted margin 0.8018 → 8.018, \(\rho\) 5.477 → 54.77, \((\rho/\gamma)^2\) stays 46.67. The program “min \(\|\mathbf w\|^2\) s.t. \(y_i\langle \mathbf w, 10\mathbf x_i\rangle \ge 1\)” is solved by \(\mathbf w_0/10\) (substitute \(\mathbf w' = 10\mathbf w\)), so the labels on the scaled messages are the same as before on the originals: same hypothesis up to the change of units. The hard-SVM bound depends only on \(\rho/\gamma\), so it is unchanged. \(\rho\) is needed because margin alone is not scale-invariant (any separable data can be given any margin); for soft-SVM \(\rho\) is also the Lipschitz constant of the hinge loss (Remark 15.2). Watch for: scaling only the counts but not the constant 1 is not a pure scaling of the lifted vectors.
2. For \(i \notin I\) the gap \(y_i\langle \mathbf w_0, \mathbf x_i\rangle - 1\) is positive; \(\mathbf w_t\) stays feasible if \(t|\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle|\) is at most that gap, so \(t\) must be at most the minimum of gap/\(|\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle|\) over those \(i\) with \(\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle \ne 0\). Finitely many examples make that minimum positive; with infinitely many constraints the infimum could be 0. \(t \le 1\) (any \(t < 2\) works) makes \(2t - t^2 > 0\), so the norm strictly drops; at \(t = 2\) the norm is unchanged (reflection of \(\mathbf r\)), beyond it grows. \(I\) nonempty: otherwise \(V = \{\mathbf 0\}\), so \(\mathbf w_0 = \mathbf r\) and the argument forces \(\mathbf w_0 = \mathbf 0\), which violates every constraint (when \(m \ge 1\)).
3. \(\lambda = \sqrt{2\rho^2/(B^2m)} = \sqrt{50/80{,}000} = 0.025\); bound \(0.04 + \sqrt{8 \cdot 25 \cdot 4/20{,}000} = 0.04 + 0.2 = 0.24\). For 0.15: \(\sqrt{800/m} \le 0.11 \Rightarrow m \ge 800/0.0121 = 66{,}115.7\), so 66,116 labels. Accept the bound as an expectation over \(S\), and look for the learner saying so.

Problems (presented in the prose as the lesson’s own; hints one at a time, never a solution):
- Problem 1 = Exercise 15.1 (p. 214).
  1. Separability means some unit \((\mathbf w, b)\) has all signed scores positive. What does that say about the value of \(\max \min_i y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i\rangle + b)\)?
  2. Show every maximizer of the second form lies in \(G\): a point outside \(G\) has smallest signed score \(\le 0\), below the maximum.
  3. For \((\mathbf w, b) \in G\), compare \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i\rangle + b)\) with \(|\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i\rangle + b|\) using \(y_i \in \{\pm 1\}\).
  4. The two objectives agree on \(G\), the first problem is restricted to \(G\), and the second’s maximizers are in \(G\): conclude the argmax sets are equal.
- Problem 2 = Exercise 15.3 (p. 214). The claim is false.
  1. Try the smallest possible case: one dimension, \(m = 2\).
  2. Take \(x_1 = a\), \(y_1 = +1\) and \(x_2 = -a\), \(y_2 = -1\) for a small number \(a > 0\). What does hard-SVM return?
  3. Both examples have \(y x = a\), so soft-SVM minimizes \(\lambda w^2 + \max\{0, 1 - a w\}\). Where is its minimum on \(0 \le w \le 1/a\)?
  4. The minimizer is \(a/(2\lambda)\) whenever \(a^2 < 2\lambda\); hard-SVM gives \(1/a\). For any fixed \(\lambda\), pick \(a\) small enough.
- Problem 3 = Exercise 15.4 (p. 214).
  1. Fix any \(x' \in \mathcal X\) and \(y' \in \mathcal Y\). Compare \(f(x', y')\) with \(\min_x f(x, y')\) and with \(\max_y f(x', y)\).
  2. You get \(\min_x f(x, y') \le \max_y f(x', y)\). Which side depends on \(x'\), which on \(y'\)?
  3. Take the max over \(y'\) on the left and the min over \(x'\) on the right. (If extrema are not attained, the same argument works with inf and sup.)
- Not chosen: Exercise 15.2 (Batch Perceptron at most \((\rho/\gamma)^2\) updates) is lesson 9’s radius-and-margin claim, already proved there.
