# Covering Numbers

Objective: after this, you can define an \(r\)-cover and the covering number \(N(r, A)\) of a set of score vectors, build a grid cover of a flat set and prove its size, prove the shift-and-stretch and Lipschitz-loss properties of covering numbers, turn one cover into a Rademacher bound with Massart's lemma, prove the chaining bound step by step, derive its infinite-chain corollary and the flat-class example, and choose the chain's depth by the rule the bound itself implies.

Terms:
- Euclidean norm \(\|a - a'\|\): straight-line distance between score vectors.
- \(r\)-cover: \(A'\) with every \(a \in A\) within \(r\) of some \(a' \in A'\); representatives need not lie in \(A\).
- covering number \(N(r, A)\): size of the smallest \(r\)-cover; non-increasing in \(r\); equals 1 once \(r \ge c\).
- \(\rho\)-Lipschitz: \(|\varphi(t) - \varphi(t')| \le \rho |t - t'|\).
- Rademacher complexity \(R(A) = \frac1m \mathbb E_\sigma \sup_{a\in A} \langle\sigma, a\rangle\) (lesson 26).
- chaining: telescoping \(a^*\) through nearest representatives at radii \(c, c/2, \dots, c2^{-M}\).

Misconception: "a cover's representatives must be members of the class". Why wrong: the definition allows any vectors of \(\mathbb R^m\); the grid points and the clump example's \((0,0)\) are not score vectors.
Second misconception: "chaining always gives a smaller number than a single cover". Why wrong: at every practical \(m\) in this lesson the one-scale bound is smaller (normalized 4.33 to 8.14 versus 11.02); chaining wins only on dependence on \(m\).
Third misconception: "the measured Rademacher complexity certifies the filter". Why wrong: it is a random estimate (0.0126 at \(m = 9{,}900\)), not a bound.

Derivation:
- Flat-set proposition: midpoint grid with \(n = \lceil c\sqrt d / r\rceil\) per coordinate, error \(\le c/n\) per coordinate, total \(\le \sqrt d\,c/n \le r\); \(\lceil t\rceil \le 2t\) for \(t \ge 1\) gives the book's \((2c\sqrt d/r)^d\).
- Shift and stretch, Lipschitz-loss lemma: transform the cover with the set.
- Massart (uncentered form) reproved: Jensen, independence, \(\cosh t \le e^{t^2/2}\), \(\lambda = \sqrt{2\log N}/L\).
- One-scale bound: \(R(A) \le r/\sqrt m + (c + r)\sqrt{2\log N(r,A)}/m\) by Cauchy–Schwarz plus Massart on the pruned cover.
- Chaining bound: nine steps (center; covers \(B_0 = \{0\}, B_k\); \(\eta\)-maximizer; links \(\le 3c2^{-k}\); \(|\hat B_k| \le N^2\); telescope; Cauchy–Schwarz on the last rounding; Massart per level; add and let \(\eta \to 0\)).
- Depth rule: \(f(M) - f(M-1) = \frac{c2^{-M}}{\sqrt m}(6\sqrt{\log N_M}/\sqrt m - 1)\), so refine while \(6\sqrt{\log N_M} \le \sqrt m\).
- Corollary: \(\sum 2^{-k} = 1\), \(\sum k2^{-k} = 2\). Flat example: \(\alpha = \sqrt{d\log(2\sqrt d)}\), \(\beta = \sqrt d\).

Algorithms: GridCover (\(O(d\,n^d)\) time and space; size alone \(O(1)\); nearest grid point \(O(d)\) by rounding), CoveringRadius (\(O(|A||B|d)\), \(O(|A|d)\) for a grid; a test on a sample, not a proof), ChainingBound and BestDepth (\(O(M)\)). Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(A\) itself is a cover at every radius, so \(N(r,A) \le N\), with equality once \(r\) is below half the smallest distance between members. As \(M \to \infty\) the chaining bound is at most \(\frac{6c}{m}\sqrt{\log N}\sum_k 2^{-k} = 6c\sqrt{\log N}/m\), while Massart gives \(c\sqrt{2\log N}/m\) (using \(\max\|a\| \le c\) after centering), smaller by a factor \(6/\sqrt 2 \approx 4.24\). No contradiction: both are upper bounds; chaining pays constants to handle sets where Massart cannot be applied at all.
2. Massart's lemma bounds the expected maximum over a fixed finite set; the link \(b^{(k)} - b^{(k-1)}\) depends on \(\sigma\), so its expected inner product can't be bounded by its length alone except through Cauchy–Schwarz, which costs \(\sqrt m\) per level and destroys the bound. Placing every possible link in \(\hat B_k\), fixed before \(\sigma\) is drawn, lets Massart bound the expected max. The 6 is \(3\) (link length \(c2^{-k} + c2^{-(k-1)} = 3c2^{-k}\)) times \(\sqrt{2\log N^2}/\sqrt{\log N} = 2\) (pairs of representatives).
3. \(\alpha = \sqrt{3\log(2\sqrt3)} = 1.931\), \(\beta = \sqrt3 = 1.732\), \(C = 6(\alpha + 2\beta) = 32.37\); \(32.37/\sqrt m \le 0.1\) needs \(m \ge 104{,}772\).

Problems written for this lesson (the chapter has no exercises; marked as the lesson's own), hint ladders:
1. Volume lower bound \(N(r, A) \ge (c/r)^d\) for the full disc.
   - Hint 1: the union of the \(N\) balls of radius \(r\) around the representatives must contain the disc; project the representatives onto the subspace first (projection never increases distance).
   - Hint 2: compare \(d\)-dimensional volumes: \(N \cdot r^d V_d \ge c^d V_d\), where \(V_d\) is the unit ball's volume.
   - Hint 3: for \(d = 2\) the upper bound \(\lceil c\sqrt2/r\rceil^2\) is about twice the lower bound \((c/r)^2\) (plus ceiling effects); at \(r = c/8\): 144 versus 64.
2. Chain the loss vectors directly.
   - Hint 1: ramp-loss vectors lie in \([0,1]^m\), within \(\sqrt m/2\) of the vector of all \(1/2\); so use \(c' = \sqrt m / 2\).
   - Hint 2: the Lipschitz lemma with \(\rho = 1\) gives \(N(c'2^{-k}, \varphi\circ A) \le N(c'2^{-k}, A) \le \lceil 2^{k+1}\sqrt2\rceil^2\) (radius in units of \(A\)'s \(c = \sqrt m\)).
   - Hint 3: the infinite chain gives \(R \le (3/\sqrt m)\sum_k 2^{-k}\sqrt{\log\lceil 2^{k+1}\sqrt2\rceil^2} = 6.572/\sqrt m\); gap at 12,000 is 0.1448, versus 0.226 from chaining the scores (the loss set is half as wide). Expected answer: 0.145.
3. One-feature filter.
   - Hint 1: \(\alpha = \sqrt{\log 2} = 0.833\), \(\beta = 1\), constant 16.995.
   - Hint 2: gap \((2 \cdot 16.995 + \sqrt{2\log 40})/\sqrt m \le 0.25\) needs \(m \ge 21{,}559\).
   - Hint 3: a segment of length \(2c\) has \(N(r) = \lceil c/r\rceil\), so \(N(c2^{-k}) = 2^k\); the chained constant is \(6\sum_k 2^{-k}\sqrt{k\log 2} = 6.73\), and the gap needs only \(m \ge 4{,}187\).
