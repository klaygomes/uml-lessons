Covering Numbers

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define an rrr-cover and the covering number N(r,A)N(r, A)N(r,A) of a set of score vectors, build a grid cover of a flat set and prove its size, prove the shift-and-stretch and Lipschitz-loss properties of covering numbers, turn one cover into a Rademacher bound with Massart's lemma, prove the chaining bound step by step, derive its infinite-chain corollary and the flat-class example, and choose the chain's depth by the rule the bound itself implies.

Uses Chapter 26: definition of R(A)R(A)R(A) (eq. 26.5, p. 376), Lemma 26.6 (p. 379), Massart Lemma 26.8 and its proof (pp. 380 to 381), contraction Lemma 26.9 (p. 381), Theorem 26.5 (p. 378). Terms:

  • Euclidean norm ∥a−a′∥\|a - a'\|∥a−a′∥: straight-line distance between score vectors.
  • rrr-cover: A′A'A′ with every a∈Aa \in Aa∈A within rrr of some a′∈A′a' \in A'a′∈A′; representatives need not lie in AAA.
  • covering number N(r,A)N(r, A)N(r,A): size of the smallest rrr-cover; non-increasing in rrr; equals 1 once r≥cr \ge cr≥c.
  • ρ\rhoρ-Lipschitz: ∣φ(t)−φ(t′)∣≤ρ∣t−t′∣|\varphi(t) - \varphi(t')| \le \rho |t - t'|∣φ(t)−φ(t′)∣≤ρ∣t−t′∣.

Misconception: "a cover's representatives must be members of the class". Why wrong: the definition allows any vectors of Rm\mathbb R^mRm; the grid points and the clump example's (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) are not score vectors.

Second misconception: "chaining always gives a smaller number than a single cover". Why wrong: at every practical mmm in this lesson the one-scale bound is smaller (normalized 4.33 to 8.14 versus 11.02); chaining wins only on dependence on mmm.

Third misconception: "the measured Rademacher complexity certifies the filter". Why wrong: it is a random estimate (0.0126 at m=9,900m = 9{,}900m=9,900), not a bound.
