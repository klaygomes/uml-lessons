# Hard-SVM and the Margin

Objective: after this, you can define the margin of a separating hyperplane and compute it (distance to a hyperplane), state hard-SVM in its max-min and quadratic-program forms and prove the program’s output has the largest margin, explain what regularizing the bias term changes in the homogeneous form, explain why margins are only meaningful relative to the radius, state the dimension-free sample complexity of hard-SVM and tie its ratio \(\rho/\gamma\) to the Perceptron’s mistake bound, and prove that a margin is a safety distance: every training message survives every edit shorter than the margin.

Key words: message, word count, junk / keep (labels \(+1\) / \(-1\)), example, training sequence (the book’s “training set” not named), hypothesis, learner, halfspace, weight vector, bias term (never bare “bias” for \(b\)), score, signed score (\(y(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x\rangle + b)\), lesson 9a’s phrase), homogeneous, lift, separable, Perceptron, update, distance (to a hyperplane), margin (geometric: the smallest distance from the hyperplane to a training message; not “gap”; lesson 11 used “margin” for the \(\varepsilon\) of a reported number), unit (vector), hard-SVM (not “maximum-margin classifier”), quadratic program (not “QP problem”), regularize (the bias term), radius \(\rho\) (lesson 9a’s \(R\); the prose says so once), separable with a \((\gamma, \rho)\)-margin, 0-1 true risk, edit (an arrow \(\mathbf e\) added to a message; a one-word edit has length 1), safety distance, promise (the written requirement in the fix). Notation: \(\varepsilon\) accuracy, \(\delta\) confidence, \(m\) sample size, \(\log\) natural.

Terms:
- halfspace \((\mathbf w, b)\): labels \(\mathbf x\) with \(\operatorname{sign}(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle + b)\); hyperplane \(\{\mathbf v : \langle \mathbf w, \mathbf v \rangle + b = 0\}\).
- distance from \(\mathbf x\) to the hyperplane of \((\mathbf w, b)\): \(\min\{\|\mathbf x - \mathbf v\| : \langle \mathbf w, \mathbf v \rangle + b = 0\}\), equal to \(|\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle + b|\) when \(\|\mathbf w\| = 1\).
- margin of a separating \((\mathbf w, b)\), \(\|\mathbf w\| = 1\), on \(S\): \(\min_i y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b)\).
- hard-SVM: the separating halfspace of largest margin; equivalently \(\operatorname{argmin} \|\mathbf w\|^2\) s.t. \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b) \ge 1\), output normalized.
- homogeneous halfspace: no bias term; lifting \(\mathbf x \mapsto (1, \mathbf x)\) makes the bias term the first weight, and the homogeneous program then regularizes it.
- separable with a \((\gamma, \rho)\)-margin: some unit \((\mathbf w^\star, b^\star)\) has \(y(\langle \mathbf w^\star, \mathbf x \rangle + b^\star) \ge \gamma\) and \(\|\mathbf x\| \le \rho\) with probability 1.
- safety distance (the lesson’s claim): for a unit separator with margin \(\gamma\), \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i + \mathbf e \rangle + b) \ge \gamma - \|\mathbf e\|\) for every edit \(\mathbf e\).

Misconception: “every consistent halfspace is as good as any other”. Why wrong: they differ in margin, hence in robustness to edits and in the dimension-free guarantee; Perceptron 0.243 vs hard-SVM 1.342 on the lesson’s eight messages.
Second misconception: “scale the data to get a bigger margin”. Why wrong: margin and radius scale together; only \(\rho/\gamma\) matters (Definition 15.3).
Third misconception: “if none of the edits I tried flips a message, the line is safe”. Why wrong: with the weight vector at \(-50^\circ\) and the line centred, no axis one-word edit of any of the eight messages crosses, yet A and E sit 0.841 from it; only the margin certifies edits in every direction.

Derivations (all in the prose, complete):
- Claim 15.1 (distance), book’s proof with the cross term spelled out; snippet tests 10,001 grid points of the line.
- Lemma 15.2, restated as “the hard-SVM output’s margin is at least every separator’s margin”, which avoids assuming the max-min problem has a solution; adds \(\mathbf w_0 \ne 0\) when both labels appear.
- Theorem 15.4 stated with its interpretation and numbers; proof deferred to lesson 26 (as the book defers to Ch. 26). Remark 15.1 tied to lesson 9a’s mistake bound.
- Safety-distance claim (the lesson’s own): linearity plus Cauchy–Schwarz; tightness noted (an edit of length \(\gamma\) along \(-y_i\mathbf w\) lands on the hyperplane).

Algorithms: Hard-SVM as a quadratic program (pseudocode in the prose; output normalized); solvers run in polynomial time; the concrete solver (dual coordinate ascent, \(O(md)\) per sweep) and the build-it challenge are in lesson 15b. Brute-force max-margin search over directions (the 100,000-direction snippet) is \(O(Nm)\) for \(N\) directions in the plane.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. Scaling all lifted vectors by 10: lifted margin 0.8018 → 8.018, \(\rho\) 5.477 → 54.77, \((\rho/\gamma)^2\) stays 46.67. The program “min \(\|\mathbf w\|^2\) s.t. \(y_i\langle \mathbf w, 10\mathbf x_i\rangle \ge 1\)” is solved by \(\mathbf w_0/10\) (substitute \(\mathbf w' = 10\mathbf w\)), so the labels on the scaled messages are the same as before on the originals: same hypothesis up to the change of units. The hard-SVM bound depends only on \(\rho/\gamma\), so it is unchanged. \(\rho\) is needed because margin alone is not scale-invariant (any separable data can be given any margin). Watch for: scaling only the counts but not the constant 1 is not a pure scaling of the lifted vectors.
2. Swapping one “FREE” for a “meeting” is the edit \((-1, +1)\), length \(\sqrt2 \approx 1.414 > 1.342\), so the claim promises nothing. B becomes \((2, 1)\), score \((2 \cdot 2 - 1)/3 - 1 = 0\): exactly on the line, a mistake by the convention (`split15/a2c.py`; G with “meeting” swapped for “free” also lands on \((2,1)\)). A margin can never promise more than its own length: an edit of length \(\gamma\) along \(-y_i\mathbf w\) reaches the hyperplane, and here an edit only 0.07 longer than the margin does. Look for the learner computing the edit’s length, not just the new score.
3. All junk, e.g. \(S = (((3,0), +1))\): \(\mathbf w = \mathbf 0\), \(b = 1\) satisfies \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i\rangle + b) = 1 \ge 1\) with \(\|\mathbf w\|^2 = 0\), the smallest possible, so \(\mathbf w_0 = \mathbf 0\) and the output \(\mathbf w_0/\|\mathbf w_0\|\) divides by zero (hard-SVM has no direction to return; every separator’s margin can be made as large as you like by moving \(b\)). \(\gamma > 0\) is used to divide the competitor’s inequalities by \(\gamma\) without flipping them and to make \((\mathbf w/\gamma, b/\gamma)\) defined; with \(\gamma \le 0\) the competitor does not separate.
