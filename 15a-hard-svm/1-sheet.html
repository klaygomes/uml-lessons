Hard-SVM and the Margin

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can define the margin of a separating hyperplane and compute it (distance to a hyperplane), state hard-SVM in its max-min and quadratic-program forms and prove the program’s output has the largest margin, explain what regularizing the bias term changes in the homogeneous form, explain why margins are only meaningful relative to the radius, state the dimension-free sample complexity of hard-SVM and tie its ratio ρ/γ\rho/\gammaρ/γ to the Perceptron’s mistake bound, and prove that a margin is a safety distance: every training message survives every edit shorter than the margin.

Book: Chapter 15, pp. 202 to 206: intro (p. 202); 15.1 Margin and Hard-SVM, Claim 15.1, Lemma 15.2, Eqs. 15.1 and 15.2 (pp. 202 to 205); 15.1.1 homogeneous case, Eq. 15.3 (p. 205); 15.1.2 sample complexity, Definition 15.3, Theorem 15.4, Remark 15.1 (pp. 205 to 206); 15.8 Exercise 15.1 (p. 214). Tools from earlier chapters: Theorem 26.13 (Section 26.3, proof of Theorem 15.4, deferred to lesson 26); Cauchy–Schwarz and the Perceptron mistake bound (Chapter 9, lesson 9a); the VC dimension of halfspaces and the fundamental theorem (Chapters 6 and 9, lessons 6a and 9a). Split from lesson 15 (15-support-vector-machines, original kept there) on 2026-11-20; sections 15.2 to 15.6 and Exercises 15.3 and 15.4 went to lesson 15b.

Bug: on separable mail the Perceptron returns a separator that grazes the lunch invitation G (distance 0.243); one extra “free” flips it, and also “see you tomorrow” and the pizza invitation, to junk.

Fix: act 2 of the lab: facilities and operations agree on a written promise, “every labelled message sits more than one word from the boundary”. Over three labelled weeks the learner steers a separator (direction and offset) until it separates with plane margin strictly greater than 1, the length of a one-word edit, which by the safety-distance claim certifies that no edit of length at most 1 can carry any labelled message across. Passing is decided by the exact margin, never by the edits that happen to be drawn.

Terms:

  • margin of a separating (w,b)(\mathbf w, b)(w,b), ∥w∥=1\|\mathbf w\| = 1∥w∥=1, on SSS: min⁡iyi(⟨w,xi⟩+b)\min_i y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b)mini​yi​(⟨w,xi​⟩+b).
  • hard-SVM: the separating halfspace of largest margin; equivalently argmin⁡∥w∥2\operatorname{argmin} \|\mathbf w\|^2argmin∥w∥2 s.t. yi(⟨w,xi⟩+b)≥1y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b) \ge 1yi​(⟨w,xi​⟩+b)≥1, output normalized.
  • homogeneous halfspace: no bias term; lifting x↦(1,x)\mathbf x \mapsto (1, \mathbf x)x↦(1,x) makes the bias term the first weight, and the homogeneous program then regularizes it.
  • separable with a (γ,ρ)(\gamma, \rho)(γ,ρ)-margin: some unit (w⋆,b⋆)(\mathbf w^\star, b^\star)(w⋆,b⋆) has y(⟨w⋆,x⟩+b⋆)≥γy(\langle \mathbf w^\star, \mathbf x \rangle + b^\star) \ge \gammay(⟨w⋆,x⟩+b⋆)≥γ and ∥x∥≤ρ\|\mathbf x\| \le \rho∥x∥≤ρ with probability 1.
  • safety distance (the lesson’s claim): for a unit separator with margin γ\gammaγ, yi(⟨w,xi+e⟩+b)≥γ−∥e∥y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i + \mathbf e \rangle + b) \ge \gamma - \|\mathbf e\|yi​(⟨w,xi​+e⟩+b)≥γ−∥e∥ for every edit e\mathbf ee.

Misconception: “every consistent halfspace is as good as any other”. Why wrong: they differ in margin, hence in robustness to edits and in the dimension-free guarantee; Perceptron 0.243 vs hard-SVM 1.342 on the lesson’s eight messages.

Second misconception: “scale the data to get a bigger margin”. Why wrong: margin and radius scale together; only ρ/γ\rho/\gammaρ/γ matters (Definition 15.3).

Third misconception: “if none of the edits I tried flips a message, the line is safe”. Why wrong: with the weight vector at −50∘-50^\circ−50∘ and the line centred, no axis one-word edit of any of the eight messages crosses, yet A and E sit 0.841 from it; only the margin certifies edits in every direction.

Derivations (all in the prose, complete):

  • Claim 15.1 (distance), book’s proof with the cross term spelled out; snippet tests 10,001 grid points of the line.
  • Lemma 15.2, restated as “the hard-SVM output’s margin is at least every separator’s margin”, which avoids assuming the max-min problem has a solution; adds w0≠0\mathbf w_0 \ne 0w0​=0 when both labels appear.
  • Theorem 15.4 stated with its interpretation and numbers; proof deferred to lesson 26 (as the book defers to Ch. 26). Remark 15.1 tied to lesson 9a’s mistake bound.
  • Safety-distance claim (the lesson’s own): linearity plus Cauchy–Schwarz; tightness noted (an edit of length γ\gammaγ along −yiw-y_i\mathbf w−yi​w lands on the hyperplane).

Algorithms: Hard-SVM as a quadratic program (pseudocode in the prose; output normalized); solvers run in polynomial time; the concrete solver (dual coordinate ascent, O(md)O(md)O(md) per sweep) and the build-it challenge are in lesson 15b.
