A promise nobody can sign

The boosted filter shipped at the end of lesson 10a with a cheerful release note: “wrong on 0.9% of messages.” A week later a customer’s legal team asks to put that number into their contract, and then asks the obvious question. Which messages?

The 0.9% was measured on the twenty thousand messages the vote was trained on. It is the empirical risk. Every guarantee lesson 10a proved about the vote is about the empirical risk: the theorem drives it down exponentially, and says nothing whatsoever about the next message to arrive. You have met a hypothesis with a spotless empirical risk before. Lesson 1’s memorizer was right on every message it had seen and no better than a coin on every other.

So the manager rewrites the request. With probability at least 95%, the filter’s true risk on fresh mail must be at most 0.20. Not 0.9%. Just 0.20, but in writing, computed from the labelled messages alone, with nothing measured on mail you do not have.

The team’s first answer is more rounds, since more rounds kept pushing the empirical risk down. But every round also makes the vote a member of a bigger class, and lesson 4 taught you that the bigger the class, the further its empirical risks can stray from its true risks. So what does a vote of TTT stumps cost you on fresh mail, and which TTT can you sign for? That is this lesson’s hunt.

What the vote can express

AdaBoost does not return a stump. It returns a vote of TTT stumps, like the three-stump vote that got all ten messages right, and that vote lives in a bigger class. For a base class BBB, define

L(B,T)={x↦sign⁡ ⁣(∑t=1Twtht(x)):w∈RT, h1,…,hT∈B}.L(B, T) = \left\{ x \mapsto \operatorname{sign}\!\left( \sum_{t=1}^T w_t h_t(x) \right) : w \in \mathbb R^T,\ h_1, \dots, h_T \in B \right\}.L(B,T)={x↦sign(t=1∑T​wt​ht​(x)):w∈RT, h1​,…,hT​∈B}.

So TTT stumps can draw a staircase with TTT steps. That has a consequence for the VC dimension (lesson 6a: the size of the largest set of points a class can shatter, that is, label in every possible way). Take any TTT distinct points on the line and any of their 2T2^T2T labellings. Cut between neighbours whose labels differ: that gives at most TTT pieces whose labels alternate. If the first piece is −1-1−1, the staircase with that many stumps draws it; if it is +1+1+1, flip the sign of every vote; and any stumps left over get vote 0. So on those points every labelling is a vote of TTT stumps. Hence

VCdim⁡(L(B,T))≥T.\operatorname{VCdim}(L(B, T)) \ge T.VCdim(L(B,T))≥T.

VCdim⁡(⋅)\operatorname{VCdim}(\cdot)VCdim(⋅) is the VC dimension of the class in the brackets. In plain words: votes of TTT stumps on the line can label some TTT points in every possible way. Three stumps can label any three points on the line in all 23=82^3 = 823=8 ways. The exact value turns out to depend on the parity of TTT: it is T+1T + 1T+1 when TTT is odd and TTT when TTT is even. You will see why in a moment, and it is a nice puzzle on its own. What matters is the trend: every extra round buys the vote more expressive power.

That power is a double-edged sword, and lesson 5 named both edges. A richer class lowers the approximation error, the true risk of the best hypothesis in the class. A richer class also raises the estimation error, the gap between that best and what the learner finds from finitely many examples. AdaBoost gives you a single dial for this tradeoff: the number of rounds TTT. The empirical risk falls as TTT grows. How fast does the class grow?

How big the class gets

Each round adds one base hypothesis, with its own ddd degrees of freedom, plus one vote, so expect about T(d+1)T(d + 1)T(d+1), a little more for the logarithm.

Now the parity puzzle from the last section. Put TTT stumps on the line, all with genuine thresholds between T+1T + 1T+1 points. A promise about fresh mail

The empirical risk falls with TTT. The class grows with TTT. To turn that into a promise about fresh mail, we need a bound with real numbers in it, and our mail room has a feature that makes one easy: there are only finitely many possible messages. What people get wrong

“More rounds means a better filter.” More rounds means a lower empirical risk and a bigger class, whose VC dimension grows roughly linearly with TTT. In the lab’s run, 100 rounds have a smaller empirical risk than 40, and a certified risk of 0.227 instead of 0.175.

“The theorem says AdaBoost learns.” The theorem from lesson 10a is about the empirical risk only. It says nothing about fresh mail. That part needs the size of L(B,T)L(B, T)L(B,T), and the two together make the tradeoff you just navigated.

“The certified risk is what the filter will score on fresh mail.” It is a ceiling, not a forecast. At 80 rounds the certified risk is 0.203 and the fresh error in the lab is 0.006. The certificate has to hold for every distribution and every vote in the class, so it charges you for the class’s size even when the extra rounds happen not to hurt.

Faces: boosting in the wild

The same recipe, counts plus stumps plus boosting, made one of the best-known detectors in computer vision: the face detector of Viola and Jones. The task is to tell whether a small grey image, 24 by 24 pixels, shows a human face. So X\mathcal XX is the set of 24×2424 \times 2424×24 real matrices of grey levels, and the goal is a hypothesis h:X→{±1}h: \mathcal X \to \{\pm 1\}h:X→{±1}.

Each base hypothesis has the form h(x)=f(g(x))h(x) = f(g(x))h(x)=f(g(x)), where fff is a decision stump on a single number and ggg turns the image into that number. Every ggg is described by two things:

  • an axis-aligned rectangle RRR inside the image, and
  • a type, one of four masks, A to D.

To compute ggg, stretch the mask to fill RRR, add up the pixel values in each of the mask’s regions, and take a difference. Types A and B split the rectangle into two equal halves, side by side or one above the other, and subtract one sum from the other. Type C splits it into three strips and subtracts the two outer sums from the centre one. Type D splits it into four quarters and takes the difference between the diagonal pairs.

[diagram:haar: built on the lesson page]

How many functions ggg are there? A rectangle is fixed by its two horizontal and two vertical edges, so there are at most 24424^4244 rectangles and 4⋅2444 \cdot 24^44⋅244 functions. Counting more carefully, the edges sit on 25 grid lines in each direction:

rectangles = (25 * 24 // 2) ** 2
print(rectangles, 24 ** 4, 4 * rectangles)

It prints 90000 331776 360000. So the weak learner is the stump learner of lesson 10a, run over a few hundred thousand features: compute every ggg on every training image once, then find the best stump on the weighted images in each round. The first step is made fast by a preprocessing trick, the integral image, which lets every rectangle sum be computed in constant time. Problem 2 asks you to invent it.

And what does AdaBoost pick? The first feature it chooses compares the band across the eyes with the band across the upper cheeks just below: the eye region is often darker than the cheeks. The second compares the eye regions with the bridge of the nose between them. Nobody told the learner that faces have eyes. It found the two cheapest rules of thumb that beat a coin, then the next one, on the faces the first two missed.
