Terms:
- slack variable \(\xi_i \ge 0\): how much constraint \(i\) is violated.
- soft-SVM: \(\min \lambda\|\mathbf w\|^2 + \frac1m \sum \xi_i\) s.t. \(y_i(\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x_i \rangle + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i\), \(\xi_i \ge 0\); equals \(\min \lambda \|\mathbf w\|^2 + L_S^{\text{hinge}}\).
- hinge loss: \(\max\{0, 1 - y\langle \mathbf w, \mathbf x \rangle\}\); ramp loss: \(\min\{1, \text{hinge}\}\).
- support vectors: training messages with \(|\langle \mathbf w_0, \mathbf x_i \rangle| = 1\).
- dual problem: \(\max_{\boldsymbol\alpha \ge 0} \sum \alpha_i - \frac12 \sum\sum \alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j \langle \mathbf x_j, \mathbf x_i \rangle\).
- From lesson 15a, recalled in “What you need from lesson 15a”: halfspace, score, signed score, distance, margin, hard-SVM and its program, homogeneous and lifted forms, separable with a \((\gamma, \rho)\)-margin, radius, hard-SVM’s guarantee, the VC route.

Misconception: “the margin bound always beats the VC bound”. Why wrong: it wins when \(\rho^2B^2 \ll d\); the large-margin preference is an inductive bias that can raise approximation error, and the hinge approximation term can exceed the 0-1 one (p. 209).
Second misconception: “every message on the margin has a nonzero coefficient”. Why wrong: in S10 at \(\lambda = 0.1\), A has signed score exactly 1 and dual coefficient 0.
Third misconception: “soft-SVM with a tiny \(\lambda\) is hard-SVM”. Why wrong: on some samples it is (S10 at \(\lambda \le 0.02\) returns the hard-SVM separator of the eight), but no single \(\lambda\) works for every separable sample (Problem 1).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. For \(i \notin I\) the gap \(y_i\langle \mathbf w_0, \mathbf x_i\rangle - 1\) is positive; \(\mathbf w_t\) stays feasible if \(t|\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle|\) is at most that gap, so \(t\) must be at most the minimum of gap/\(|\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle|\) over those \(i\) with \(\langle \mathbf r, \mathbf x_i\rangle \ne 0\). Finitely many examples make that minimum positive; with infinitely many constraints the infimum could be 0. \(t \le 1\) (any \(t < 2\) works) makes \(2t - t^2 > 0\), so the norm strictly drops; at \(t = 2\) the norm is unchanged (reflection of \(\mathbf r\)), beyond it grows. \(I\) nonempty: otherwise \(V = \{\mathbf 0\}\), so \(\mathbf w_0 = \mathbf r\) and the argument forces \(\mathbf w_0 = \mathbf 0\), which violates every constraint (when \(m \ge 1\)).
2. \(\lambda = \sqrt{2\rho^2/(B^2m)} = \sqrt{50/80{,}000} = 0.025\); bound \(0.04 + \sqrt{8 \cdot 25 \cdot 4/20{,}000} = 0.04 + 0.2 = 0.24\). For 0.15: \(\sqrt{800/m} \le 0.11 \Rightarrow m \ge 800/0.0121 = 66{,}115.7\), so 66,116 labels. Accept the bound as an expectation over \(S\), and look for the learner saying so.

Problems (presented in the prose as the lesson’s own; hints one at a time, never a solution):
- Problem 1 = Exercise 15.3 (p. 214). The claim is false.
  1. Try the smallest possible case: one dimension, \(m = 2\).
  2. Take \(x_1 = a\), \(y_1 = +1\) and \(x_2 = -a\), \(y_2 = -1\) for a small number \(a > 0\). What does hard-SVM return?
  3. Both examples have \(y x = a\), so soft-SVM minimizes \(\lambda w^2 + \max\{0, 1 - a w\}\). Where is its minimum on \(0 \le w \le 1/a\)?
  4. The minimizer is \(a/(2\lambda)\) whenever \(a^2 < 2\lambda\); hard-SVM gives \(1/a\). For any fixed \(\lambda\), pick \(a\) small enough.
- Problem 2 = Exercise 15.4 (p. 214).
  1. Fix any \(x' \in \mathcal X\) and \(y' \in \mathcal Y\). Compare \(f(x', y')\) with \(\min_x f(x, y')\) and with \(\max_y f(x', y)\).
  2. You get \(\min_x f(x, y') \le \max_y f(x', y)\). Which side depends on \(x'\), which on \(y'\)?
  3. Take the max over \(y'\) on the left and the min over \(x'\) on the right. (If extrema are not attained, the same argument works with inf and sup.)
- Exercise 15.1 is lesson 15a’s Problem 1; Exercise 15.2 is lesson 9a’s radius-and-margin claim.
