Terms:
- model selection: choosing among learners or parameter settings using the data.
- validation set \(V\): \(m_v\) fresh i.i.d. examples, independent of \(S\); validation risk \(L_V(h)\) is the average loss on it.
- hold-out guarantee: \(|L_V(h) - L_{\mathcal D}(h)| \le \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2m_v)}\) w.p. \(\ge 1 - \delta\), \(V\) independent of \(h\).
- candidate set \(H = \{h_1, \dots, h_r\}\): outputs of the learners; may depend on \(S\), not on \(V\).
- model-selection curve: empirical risk and validation risk against model complexity.
- k-fold cross validation; leave-one-out (\(k = m\)).
- test set: a third part, used once, for the reported number.
- learning curve: empirical risk on prefixes and validation risk against prefix size.

Misconception: "the test set is clean because we never trained on it". Why wrong: choosing on it makes the chosen hypothesis depend on it, so step 2 of the hold-out proof fails; with 60 independent candidates of true risk 0.2, the winner reports 0.137 on average.
Second misconception: "more candidates can only help". Why wrong: the margin grows with \(\log(2r/\delta)\); a big search overfits \(V\).
Third: "the cross-validation error is the true risk of the shipped hypothesis". Why wrong: it estimates hypotheses trained on fewer examples; Exercise 1 is off by 1/2.
Fourth: "\(L_S\) small means approximation error small". Why wrong: a class that shatters the sample has \(L_S = 0\) with any approximation error.

Derivation:
- SRM bound (11.2) from Equation 11.1 and Theorem 7.4: \(\varepsilon_d = \sqrt{g(d)\log(1/\delta_d)/m}\) meets \(m^{\mathrm{UC}} \le m\); \(\delta_d = w(d)\delta\), \(w(d) = 6/(\pi^2 d^2)\), \(\sum 1/d^2 = \pi^2/6\); union bound; \(\log(\pi^2 d^2/(6\delta))\). The prose proves Theorem 7.4's union step inline instead of citing it.
- Theorem 11.1: condition on \(S\) (and learner randomness), independence keeps \(V \sim \mathcal D^{m_v}\), \(\theta_i = \ell(h, z_i)\) i.i.d. in \([0,1]\) with mean \(L_{\mathcal D}(h)\), Hoeffding, law of total probability, solve for \(\varepsilon\). The book states the theorem from Hoeffding without the conditioning steps; the spelled-out steps are ours.
- Theorem 11.2: conditional on \(H\), Hoeffding at \(\delta/r\) per candidate, union bound. The \(2\varepsilon\) chain for the selected candidate is ours (standard).
- Error decomposition (p. 152) and the \(L_S(h_S)\) split; the explicit one-sided lower bound \(L_{\mathcal D}(h^*) \ge L_S(h_S) - \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2m)}\) is ours (the book says "bounded quite tightly (as in Theorem 11.1)").

End-of-chapter problems (§11.5, pp. 154 to 155; the chapter has only two). In the prose: Problem 1 = Exercise 1, Problem 2 = Exercise 2. Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Exercise 1 (leave-one-out fails), standard.
  1. What is the true risk of any constant hypothesis when labels are fair coins, independent of the message? So what is the true risk of the learner's output, whatever \(S\) is?
  2. Let \(P\) be the parity of all \(m\) labels. Leaving out example \(i\), what is the parity of the remaining labels, in terms of \(P\) and \(y_i\)?
  3. The left-out example is wrong exactly when \(P \oplus y_i \ne y_i\). When is that? So every left-out example is wrong if \(P = 1\) and right if \(P = 0\): the estimate is \(P \in \{0, 1\}\), and \(|P - 1/2| = 1/2\). (Checked by reasoning; the build-it's constant learner shows a milder version.)
- Exercise 2 (union ERM vs validation), standard.
  1. Write the two guarantees: ERM over \(\mathcal H\) with \(m\) examples pays the complexity of the union (for finite classes \(\log \sum_i |\mathcal H_i|\), or the union's VC dimension); the second pays each \(\mathcal H_i\)'s complexity on \((1 - \alpha)m\) examples plus \(2\sqrt{\log(2k/\delta)/(2\alpha m)}\).
  2. Try all \(\mathcal H_i\) equal (or nested with the largest the best): the union is no richer than one class, and the second method throws away \(\alpha m\) examples. Which wins?
  3. Try \(\mathcal H_1\) small containing a good hypothesis and \(\mathcal H_2\) huge (all functions on a large domain): ERM over the union returns a consistent but useless hypothesis from \(\mathcal H_2\); validation catches it. Also \(k\) small vs the union's VC dimension much larger than any \(\mathcal H_i\)'s.
