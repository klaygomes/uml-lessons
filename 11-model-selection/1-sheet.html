Model Selection and Validation

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can say why a test set that chose a hypothesis can no longer grade it, state and prove the hold-out guarantee and its finite-candidate version (and the 2ε2\varepsilon2ε consequence for the chosen candidate), derive the SRM bound from uniform convergence with weights 6/(π2d2)6/(\pi^2 d^2)6/(π2d2) and explain why it is pessimistic, read a model-selection curve and refine a parameter grid, run k-fold cross validation and say what it does and does not guarantee, size a train, validation and test split from a label budget, and diagnose a failed learner with the validation decomposition and learning curves.

Terms:

  • model selection: choosing among learners or parameter settings using the data.
  • validation set VVV: mvm_vmv​ fresh i.i.d. examples, independent of SSS; validation risk LV(h)L_V(h)LV​(h) is the average loss on it.
  • hold-out guarantee: ∣LV(h)−LD(h)∣≤log⁡(2/δ)/(2mv)|L_V(h) - L_{\mathcal D}(h)| \le \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2m_v)}∣LV​(h)−LD​(h)∣≤log(2/δ)/(2mv​)​ w.p. ≥1−δ\ge 1 - \delta≥1−δ, VVV independent of hhh.
  • candidate set H={h1,…,hr}H = \{h_1, \dots, h_r\}H={h1​,…,hr​}: outputs of the learners; may depend on SSS, not on VVV.
  • model-selection curve: empirical risk and validation risk against model complexity.
  • k-fold cross validation; leave-one-out (k=mk = mk=m).
  • test set: a third part, used once, for the reported number.
  • learning curve: empirical risk on prefixes and validation risk against prefix size.

Misconception: "the test set is clean because we never trained on it". Why wrong: choosing on it makes the chosen hypothesis depend on it, so step 2 of the hold-out proof fails; with 60 independent candidates of true risk 0.2, the winner reports 0.137 on average.

Second misconception: "more candidates can only help". Why wrong: the margin grows with log⁡(2r/δ)\log(2r/\delta)log(2r/δ); a big search overfits VVV.

Third: "the cross-validation error is the true risk of the shipped hypothesis". Why wrong: it estimates hypotheses trained on fewer examples; Exercise 1 is off by 1/2.

Fourth: "LSL_SLS​ small means approximation error small". Why wrong: a class that shatters the sample has LS=0L_S = 0LS​=0 with any approximation error.
