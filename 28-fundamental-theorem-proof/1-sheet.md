# Proving the Fundamental Theorem

Objective: after this, you can prove, with explicit constants, the three quantitative bounds of the fundamental theorem for binary classification: the agnostic (noisy) ceiling through loss vectors, Sauer, Massart and the Rademacher generalization bound; the agnostic floor through two-world and \(d\)-coin adversaries with the overlap lemma; and the realizable (clean) ceiling through \(\varepsilon\)-nets, a ghost sample and a swap. You can also read a label budget against a proved floor and a proved ceiling and say whether an auditor can sign it.

Terms:
- ceiling / floor: an upper / lower bound on the sample complexity \(m_{\mathcal H}(\varepsilon, \delta)\); a ceiling is proved by analysing one learner, a floor by defeating all learners.
- estimation error: \(L_{\mathcal D}(A(S)) - \min_{h \in \mathcal H} L_{\mathcal D}(h)\); a miss is estimation error \(> \varepsilon\).
- mistake patterns \(A\): \(\{(\mathbb 1[h(x_i) \neq y_i])_{i \le m} : h \in \mathcal H\}\); in bijection with the labellings of \(x_1, \dots, x_m\), so \(|A| \le \tau_{\mathcal H}(m) \le (em/d)^d\).
- Rademacher complexity: \(R(A) = \frac1m \mathbb E_\sigma[\sup_{a \in A} \sum_i \sigma_i a_i]\) (recap of lesson 26).
- overlap and Bhattacharyya coefficient: \(\sum_y \min(P(y), Q(y))\) and \(B = \sum_y \sqrt{P(y)Q(y)}\); overlap lemma: overlap \(\ge 1 - \sqrt{1 - B^2}\).
- majority vote (maximum-likelihood learner): at each shattered message, the label most of its examples carry, a coin flip on a tie; optimal on average over uniformly random directions.
- \(\varepsilon\)-net: a sequence \(S\) such that every \(r \in \mathcal R\) with \(\mathcal D(r) \ge \varepsilon\) contains a point of \(S\).
- mistake region and symmetric difference: \(h \triangle c = \{x : h(x) \neq c(x)\}\); \(L_{\mathcal D}(h) = \mathcal D(h \triangle c)\) under clean labels; \(\mathcal H_c = \{h \triangle c\}\) has the same growth function and VC dimension as \(\mathcal H\).

Misconception: "the proved ceiling is the number of labels you need". Why wrong: it is what the proof can vouch for; for the two-world game the truth is 269 labels, the floor 81 and the intervals' noisy ceiling 547,743.
Second misconception: "the floor applies only to weak learners". Why wrong: the adversary chooses the world after the learner is fixed, and the majority vote, optimal there, still fails.
Third misconception: "clean labels only shrink the constant". Why wrong: the rate changes from \(1/\varepsilon^2\) (estimate every risk) to \(1/\varepsilon\) (hit every heavy mistake region once).
Fourth: "the noisy ceiling proved here is the theorem as stated". Why wrong: it carries an extra \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\) next to \(d\); removing it needs lesson 27's chaining plus a covering-number bound for VC classes that the course does not prove (book p. 392: "beyond the scope of this book").

Derivations (as in the prose; deviations from the book in Assumptions):
- Noisy ceiling: \(|A| = |\text{labellings}| \le (em/d)^d\); Massart with \(\|a - \bar a\| \le \sqrt m\) gives \(R(A) \le \sqrt{2d\log(em/d)/m}\); Theorem 26.5(1) with \(c = 1\) and \(\delta/2\); same for the loss \(-\ell\) (\(R(-A) = R(A)\)); union bound; \(\sqrt a + \sqrt b \le 2\sqrt{a+b}\): \(|L_{\mathcal D} - L_S| \le 2\sqrt{(8d\log(em/d) + 2\log(4/\delta))/m}\). Solved with the tangent-line lemma at \(t = 64e/\varepsilon^2\): \(m^{\mathrm{UC}} \le (64d\log(64/\varepsilon^2) + 16\log(4/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\); ERM needs level \(\varepsilon/2\): \(m_{\mathcal H} \le (256d\log(256/\varepsilon^2) + 64\log(4/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\).
- Confidence floor: worlds \(\mathcal D_b\) with gap \(\varepsilon' \in (\varepsilon, 1/\sqrt2)\); max over worlds \(\ge\) average \(\ge \frac12\sum_y \min(P_1, P_0)\); overlap lemma (min identity, Cauchy–Schwarz on \(|\sqrt p - \sqrt q|(\sqrt p + \sqrt q)\)); \(B = (1 - \varepsilon^2)^{m/2}\); \(\log(1 - x) \ge -2x\) on \([0, \frac12]\); \(m < \log(1/(4\delta))/(2\varepsilon^2)\) gives failure \(> \frac12(1 - \sqrt{1 - 4\delta}) \ge \delta\).
- Dimension floor: risk identity; average over uniform \(b\); per message, \((b_i, y^I)\) independent of \(y^{\neg I}\); half overlap \(\ge \frac12(1 - \sqrt{1 - e^{-2\rho^2 n_i}}) \ge \frac12(1 - \sqrt{2\rho^2 n_i})\); \(\mathbb E\sqrt{n_i} \le \sqrt{m/d}\); \(\max_b \mathbb E[\text{est. error}] \ge \frac\rho2(1 - \sqrt{2\rho^2 m/d}) > \rho/4\) for \(m < d/(8\rho^2)\); bounded-average lemma with \(\rho = 8\varepsilon\), \(a = 1/8\): miss probability \(> 1/7\) for \(m < d/(512\varepsilon^2)\), \(\varepsilon \le 1/(8\sqrt2)\).
- Combined floor for \(\delta \le 1/8\): \(\log(1/(4\delta)) \ge \frac13\log(1/\delta)\); \(\max \ge\) average; \(m_{\mathcal H} \ge (d + \log(1/\delta))/(1024\varepsilon^2)\).
- \(\varepsilon\)-net theorem: \(B\), ghost sample \(T\), \(B'\); Claim 1 by Chebyshev (\(\varepsilon m \ge 8\)); Claim 2 by the swap and the hypergeometric bound \(\binom{2m-r}{m}/\binom{2m}{m} \le (1 - r/2m)^m \le e^{-\varepsilon m/4}\); Sauer \(\tau(2m) \le (2em/d)^d\); tangent-line lemma at \(t = 16e/\varepsilon\): \(m \ge \frac8\varepsilon(d\log(16/\varepsilon) + \log(2/\delta))\) suffices, so the theorem's larger \(m\) does.
- Clean ceiling: \(\mathcal H_c\) has the same patterns count (bijection \(P \mapsto P \triangle (c \cap C)\)); on the \(\varepsilon\)-net event every \(h\) with \(\mathcal D(h \triangle c) \ge \varepsilon\) has \(L_S(h) > 0\); ERM has \(L_S = 0\).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. \(\varepsilon < 1/\sqrt2\): needed for \(\varepsilon'^2 \le \frac12\) in \(\log(1 - x) \ge -2x\) (the step from \((1 - \varepsilon'^2)^m\) to \(e^{-2m\varepsilon'^2}\)); without it the exponential form fails (the overlap lemma itself still holds). \(\delta < 1/4\): \(\log(1/(4\delta))\) must be positive, otherwise the condition \(m < \log(1/(4\delta))/(2\varepsilon^2)\) is empty and the theorem says nothing; also \(\sqrt{1 - 4\delta}\) must be real and \(1 - 2\delta > 0\) for the squaring step. \(\varepsilon' > \varepsilon\): a miss needs estimation error strictly greater than \(\varepsilon\); with gap exactly \(\varepsilon\) a wrong guess satisfies the PAC requirement, so no failure is shown. (The book's §28.2.1 uses gap \(\varepsilon\) and "\(= \varepsilon\)"; accept a learner who spots this.) Strictness of \(m < \dots\) is what lets \(\varepsilon'\) exist.
2. The directions \(b_1, \dots, b_d\) are independent fair coins under the uniform choice of \(b\); labels at \(c_k\) depend only on \(b_k\) (and the positions \(j\), which are independent of \(b\)). So \((b_i, y^I)\) is independent of \(y^{\neg I}\), and conditioning on the other labels leaves the posterior of \(b_i\) unchanged. If the adversary always used \(b = (1, \dots, 1)\), a learner could hard-code it and have estimation error 0: the averaging step needs the uniform prior; the floor is a statement about the worst \(b\), proved through the average. Look for "independence" and "prior" explicitly.
3. \(d = 2\), \(\varepsilon = 0.05\), \(\delta = 0.01\): confidence floor \(\log 25/(2 \cdot 0.0025) = 643.8\) (so \(m \le 643\) impossible); dimension floor \(2/(512 \cdot 0.0025) = 1.56\) (applies: \(\varepsilon \le 0.0884\), \(\delta < 1/7\)); confidence floor larger by about 642 labels (factor 412). Noisy ceiling \((512\log(102{,}400) + 64\log 400)/0.0025 = 2{,}516{,}086\); clean ceiling \(160(4\log(16e/0.05) + \log 200) = 5{,}179.5\), so 5,180; noisy exceeds clean by about 2,510,906 labels, a factor of about 486.

Problems (written for this lesson; the chapter has no exercises; problem 1 is the realizable lower bound the chapter leaves "as an exercise", p. 392, in a simple \(\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon\) form). Hints only, never a solution:
1. A floor for clean labels.
   - Hint 1: when no training message is \(x_1\), what does the learner know about the label of \(x_1\)?
   - Hint 2: the chance that none of \(m\) messages is \(x_1\) is \((1 - 2\varepsilon)^m\); with the truth chosen by a fair coin, the learner is then wrong at \(x_1\) with chance \(\frac12\), and its true risk is \(2\varepsilon > \varepsilon\).
   - Hint 3: so in one of the two truths the miss probability is at least \(\frac12(1 - 2\varepsilon)^m\); use \(\log(1 - x) \ge -2x\) for \(x = 2\varepsilon \le \frac12\).
   - Hint 4: \(\frac12 e^{-4\varepsilon m} > \delta\) exactly when \(m < \log(1/(2\delta))/(4\varepsilon)\). The rate is \(1/\varepsilon\) because the learner only needs to *see* \(x_1\) once; no averaging of noise is involved. (At \(\varepsilon = 0.1\), \(\delta = 0.05\): 5.76.)
2. A cheaper noisy ceiling.
   - Hint 1: square both sides: \(a + b + 2\sqrt{ab} \le 2a + 2b\) is \((\sqrt a - \sqrt b)^2 \ge 0\).
   - Hint 2: the requirement becomes \(m\varepsilon^2/2 \ge 8d\log(em/d) + 2\log(4/\delta)\); apply the tangent-line lemma at \(t = 32e/\varepsilon^2\).
   - Hint 3: result \(m^{\mathrm{UC}} \le (32d\log(32/\varepsilon^2) + 8\log(4/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\); at level 0.05 for \(d = 2\), \(\delta = 0.05\): 256,127 labels (checked: the sharper bound is 0.0408 there), saving 291,616 labels against 547,743.
3. (Harder) The floor is nearly tight for its own worlds.
   - Hint 1: Hoeffding on one message with \(n\) labels: the majority vote is wrong with probability at most \(e^{-n\rho^2/2}\) (the fraction of labels toward \(b_i\) has mean \((1 + \rho)/2\), and a wrong vote needs it at or below \(\frac12\)).
   - Hint 2: \(\mathbb E[e^{-\lambda n_i}] = (1 - (1 - e^{-\lambda})/d)^m \le \exp(-m(1 - e^{-\lambda})/d)\), and \(1 - e^{-\lambda} \ge \lambda/2\) for \(\lambda \le 1\).
   - Hint 3: so the expected estimation error is at most \(\rho \exp(-m\rho^2/(4d))\). Maximize over \(\rho\): the worst is \(\rho = \sqrt{2d/m}\), giving \(\sqrt{2d/m}\,e^{-1/2}\).
   - Hint 4: that is at most \(\varepsilon\) once \(m \ge 2d/(e\varepsilon^2)\): order \(d/\varepsilon^2\), matching the floor's shape.

- The noisy ceiling is solved for \(m\) with the tangent-line lemma (\(\log x \le \log t + x/t - 1\)), not the book's Lemma A.2. Book check verdict: **a choice**. Lemma A.2 (p. 419) is stated for \(a \ge 1\), \(b > 0\), and the book's \(b = \frac{4}{\varepsilon^2}(8d\log(e/d) + 2\log(4/\delta))\) is negative for larger \(d\) (positive at \(d = 3\) for every \(\delta\); at \(d = 4\) negative unless \(\delta < 0.0081\); negative for every \(\delta \ge 0.001\) at \(d \ge 5\)), so the citation is outside the lemma's stated hypothesis. But the lemma's conclusion holds for every real \(b\) when \(x > 0\) (with \(A = a\log 2a\) and \(u = x\): \(x - a\log x - b \ge 2A + u/2 - a\log u \ge a > 0\) at the boundary, checked on a grid by `check28/nums.py`), so the book's bound is valid. The lesson's bound, \((64d\log(64/\varepsilon^2) + 16\log(4/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\) for uniform convergence, has the shape \((d\log(1/\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\), which implies the book's (28.1). Outside the book: the tangent-line lemma (elementary).
- The book's §28.1 makes the uniform-convergence gap smaller than \(\varepsilon\) and calls that an \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-learner; ERM needs \(\varepsilon/2\)-representativeness (Lemma 4.2), so the lesson states the uniform-convergence ceiling and then substitutes \(\varepsilon/2\) for the learning ceiling (factor 4). Verdict: **confirmed** (a slip in the stated \(m\) only; the theorem's "there exists \(C\)" absorbs it).
- Lemma B.11 (p. 428, Slud's inequality plus normal tail bounds, stated in the book without proof) is replaced by the overlap lemma (\(\sum \min(P, Q) \ge 1 - \sqrt{1 - B^2}\), proved by Cauchy–Schwarz) plus \((1 - \varepsilon^2)^m \ge e^{-2m\varepsilon^2}\). This gives exactly the book's form \(\frac12(1 - \sqrt{1 - e^{-2m\varepsilon^2}})\) with a complete proof. Verdict on the tie claim: **confirmed**. On p. 395, \(\sum_{N^+} P_-[y]\) is \(\mathbb P[X \ge m/2]\) and \(\sum_{N^-} P_+[y]\) is \(\mathbb P[X > m/2]\) for \(X \sim\) Binomial\((m, (1-\varepsilon)/2)\); the book calls both "greater than \(m/2\)" and bounds both with B.11, which covers only \(\ge\), so for even \(m\) the second is off by the tie mass. Lemma 28.1's majority-vote analysis (p. 397, \(\mathrm{sign}\) of a sum, bounded by \(\mathbb P[B \ge n_i/2]\)) has the same issue; the lesson's coin-flip tie and overlap form avoid it. Outside the book: Bhattacharyya coefficient, overlap lemma (standard Le Cam / Hellinger-type inequality).
