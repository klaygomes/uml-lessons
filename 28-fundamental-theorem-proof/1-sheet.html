Proving the Fundamental Theorem

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can prove, with explicit constants, the three quantitative bounds of the fundamental theorem for binary classification: the agnostic (noisy) ceiling through loss vectors, Sauer, Massart and the Rademacher generalization bound; the agnostic floor through two-world and ddd-coin adversaries with the overlap lemma; and the realizable (clean) ceiling through ε\varepsilonε-nets, a ghost sample and a swap. You can also read a label budget against a proved floor and a proved ceiling and say whether an auditor can sign it.

Misconception: "the proved ceiling is the number of labels you need". Why wrong: it is what the proof can vouch for; for the two-world game the truth is 269 labels, the floor 81 and the intervals' noisy ceiling 547,743.

Second misconception: "the floor applies only to weak learners". Why wrong: the adversary chooses the world after the learner is fixed, and the majority vote, optimal there, still fails.

Third misconception: "clean labels only shrink the constant". Why wrong: the rate changes from 1/ε21/\varepsilon^21/ε2 (estimate every risk) to 1/ε1/\varepsilon1/ε (hit every heavy mistake region once).

Fourth: "the noisy ceiling proved here is the theorem as stated". Why wrong: it carries an extra log⁡(1/ε)\log(1/\varepsilon)log(1/ε) next to ddd; removing it needs lesson 27's chaining plus a covering-number bound for VC classes that the course does not prove (book p. 392: "beyond the scope of this book").
