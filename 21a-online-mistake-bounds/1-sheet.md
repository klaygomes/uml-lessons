# Online Mistake Bounds

Objective: after this, you can state the online protocol and explain why no guarantee from lessons 2 to 20 survives an adaptive adversary; define mistake bounds and online learnability; prove the mistake bounds of Consistent (\(|\mathcal H| - 1\)) and Halving (\(\log_2|\mathcal H|\)); define shattered trees and the Littlestone dimension, prove the tree lower bound, the root split and SOA’s bound, and so the exact characterization \(M_{\text{SOA}}(\mathcal H) = \mathrm{Ldim}(\mathcal H)\); prove \(\mathrm{VCdim} \le \mathrm{Ldim}\) with an infinite gap; and give each learner’s time and space per round.

Bug: the spammers adapt after every decision the filter makes; mail arrives one message at a time with no fixed distribution, so every guarantee of lessons 2 to 20 (all assume \(S \sim \mathcal D^m\)) is void, and the nightly retrain loses every morning.
Fix: online learning with a proved mistake bound. Act 2 of the lab (new in the split, spec below): three realizable weeks, each with a class and a mistake budget; the learner chooses Consistent, Halving or SOA, and the week ships only if the learner’s proved mistake bound on that class is within the budget. The adversary then plays optimally and the realized count, never above the bound, is shown.

Key words: message, junk (1) / keep (0), round (not “step” or “iteration” for one pass of the protocol), prediction \(p_t\), label \(y_t\), mistake (\(p_t \neq y_t\); not “error” as a count), online learner, adversary (not “opponent” after its definition), realizable / realizability, mistake count \(M_A(S)\), mistake bound \(M_A(\mathcal H)\), online learnable, Consistent, version space (not “surviving set” as a term; “survivors” allowed as the plain word), Halving, vote, binary tree, root, depth, complete, shattered tree (shatter as in lesson 6a), Littlestone dimension \(\mathrm{Ldim}\), tree lower bound, root split (our name for the recursion), Standard Optimal Algorithm (SOA), VC dimension, mistake budget (the lab’s word for the week’s allowed mistakes). Notation: \(T\) rounds (never \(m\) for rounds; \(m\) stays the sample size, used only in Problem 2), \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\) do not appear, \(\log_2\) written out.

Terms:
- round: one pass of receive \(x_t\), predict \(p_t\), see \(y_t\), update.
- online learner: an algorithm that predicts each round from the past rounds only.
- adversary: whoever chooses the messages, possibly after seeing all past predictions.
- realizable sequence: some \(h^\star \in \mathcal H\) has \(y_t = h^\star(x_t)\) for all \(t\).
- mistake bound: \(M_A(\mathcal H) = \sup_S |\{t : p_t \neq y_t\}| \le B < \infty\) over realizable \(S\); online learnable: some \(A\) has one.
- version space \(V_t\): the hypotheses consistent with rounds \(1, \dots, t-1\).
- shattered tree: complete binary tree of depth \(d\) of messages such that every label path is followed by some \(h \in \mathcal H\).
- Littlestone dimension: the largest depth of a shattered tree (\(\infty\) if unbounded).

Misconception: “online learning is batch learning one example at a time.” · Why wrong: what changes is who chooses the examples; thresholds have VC dimension 1 (easy in batch) and Littlestone dimension \(\infty\) (impossible online).
Second: “any consistent hypothesis will do.” · Consistent can be forced into \(|\mathcal H| - 1\) mistakes, Halving at most \(\log_2|\mathcal H|\).
Third: “the Littlestone dimension is the VC dimension renamed.” · It is at least the VC dimension and can be infinitely larger, because each next message may depend on the labels so far.

Derivation (every result proved in the prose):
- Consistent \(\le |\mathcal H| - 1\); Halving \(\le \log_2|\mathcal H|\) via \(1 \le |V_{T+1}| \le |\mathcal H|2^{-M}\).
- Tree lower bound (Lemma 21.6); finite classes \(\mathrm{Ldim} \le \log_2|\mathcal H|\); singletons 1; thresholds \(\infty\).
- Root split \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V) = \max_x (1 + \min\{\mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(0)}), \mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(1)})\})\) and monotonicity under subsets: stated and proved by us; the book’s proof of Lemma 21.7 uses the construction in one sentence. SOA’s bound (Lemma 21.7) then by contradiction; Corollary 21.8.
- VCdim \(\le\) Ldim (Theorem 21.9).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner; critique against these):
1. The prediction rule is used in exactly one place: “SOA predicted \(p_t\) because its side had the larger (or tied) dimension, so \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(p_t)}) \ge \mathrm{Ldim}(V^{(y_t)}) = d\)”; this is what puts both parts at dimension \(d\) and lets the root split give \(d + 1\). For the smaller-side variant, a mistake leaves the larger part, whose dimension can equal \(\mathrm{Ldim}(V_t)\), so the potential need not drop. Example: the A∪B class (Ldim 2): at \(x_0\) the variant predicts with A (keep, dimension 1); the adversary says junk and keeps B, which has dimension 2 and forces 2 more: 3 mistakes \(> 2\). Accept any class with an unbalanced split; on \(\mathcal H_1\) every useful split is balanced, so it does not work there.
2. Ldim 5 (`thr31.py`: the binary search depth, \(\lfloor\log_2 32\rfloor\)). Consistent’s bound: \(|\mathcal H| - 1 = 31\). Halving’s: \(\log_2 32 = 5\). SOA: exactly 5, since the tree lower bound forces 5 on every learner and SOA makes at most \(\mathrm{Ldim} = 5\). Budget 5: Halving and SOA ship, Consistent does not (its bound 31 is over budget; the bound is the certificate, a lucky run does not count). On \([0, 1]\) the class is infinite, Halving’s bound is \(\infty\), and \(\mathrm{Ldim} = \infty\), so by the tree lower bound no learner has any mistake bound: nothing ships, whatever the budget. Look for the learner using the tree lower bound for “exactly”, not just SOA’s upper bound.

End-of-chapter problems (§21.7, PDF pp. 305 to 306). In the prose: Problem 1 = Exercise 2, Problem 2 = Exercise 5 (online-to-batch). Hints one rung at a time; never the solution.
- Exercise 2 (Halving tight), standard.
  1. You want every mistake to remove exactly half the version space, so you want every vote to be a tie. Which class splits evenly on every new message?
  2. Take \(\mathcal X = \{1, \dots, k\}\) and \(\mathcal H\) = all \(2^k\) functions from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\{0,1\}\). After rounds on \(1, \dots, t-1\), the version space is all functions with those labels fixed: \(2^{k-t+1}\) of them.
  3. On message \(t\), exactly half say 1: a tie, so Halving predicts 1. Label it 0. Each round is a mistake; after \(k\) rounds one function remains, and it is in \(\mathcal H\), so the sequence is realizable. \(k = \log_2|\mathcal H|\) mistakes. Works for every power of two.
- Exercise 5 (online-to-batch), standard; hint in the book points at the SGD proof (Theorem 14.8).
  1. \(\mathbb E_r[L_{\mathcal D}(h_r)] = \frac1T\sum_t L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)\) for fixed messages. Take expectation over the messages too.
  2. \(h_t\) depends only on \(x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}\) (and their labels by \(h^\star\)); \(x_t\) is independent of them. So \(\mathbb E[\mathbb 1[h_t(x_t) \ne h^\star(x_t)] \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}] = L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)\).
  3. Sum over \(t\): \(\sum_t \mathbb E[L_{\mathcal D}(h_t)] = \mathbb E[\text{number of mistakes}] \le M_A(\mathcal H)\), since the sequence is realizable. Divide by \(T\).
